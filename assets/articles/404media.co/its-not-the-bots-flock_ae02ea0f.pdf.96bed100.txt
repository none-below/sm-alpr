Garrett Langley, the CEO of automatic license plate reader company Flock, told
an audience of police chiefs at a private police conference Sunday that anti-
Flock protests around the country are not real, according to leaked audio
obtained by 404 Media.
He also said his wife advised him to never apologize, said he would prefer to
never do interviews with the press again, and claimed without evidence that the
social media opposition to Flock is driven mostly by Russian bots.
“Somebody was paying for [the social media posts and] stopped paying for it,”
Langley said about the many viral posts criticizing his company’s technology.
“Social media chatter on Flock is down about 95 percent from July. It went down
50 percent last week when Ukraine bombed a Russian data center with a drone.
Literally 50 percent overnight. And so look, SI’m the last person, my wife as well,
she’s like ‘This is crazy, these people are getting paid to say these things,’” he
added, repeating a disproven theory that Ukraine’s strikes on Russian data
centers had a significant impact on internet bot activity. 
“It’s anonymous bots, and so it is not real,” he continued. “Inorganic activity has
led to organic activity, but when I look at protests across the country, it is the
same people with the same signs and the same message. That’s not normal.
That’s not organic at all.” Langley’s comments echo another unsubstantiated
theory that said opposition to Flock comes from a far-left network spreading
propaganda at the behest of China.
Langley made the comments at the International Association of Chiefs of Police
(IACP) conference, which is running Saturday to Tuesday in Orlando, Florida.
Many law enforcement contractors exhibit at the conference, and speakers get
to attend with more than 16,000 public safety officials.

Crackgate: Apple’s $2,000 folding phone, the iPhone Duo, came out just two
days ago and people are already seeing big fat creases down the middle of the
folding screen, and some devices are cracked in the same specific spot too.
People waited in line overnight at Apple stores to get this thing, and now we’re
seeing photos of heinous cracked and creased-up Duos all over social media. In
some cases, the phone screens have cracked along the crease, causing a
crashout people are now calling “crackgate.” 
Bold choice. Let’s see if it pays off. Proton, the company behind ProtonMail and
other privacy-focused products, told staff on Monday it would be ‘re-
interviewing’ staff across the company about how they use AI in their jobs, with
the implication that the interviews may decide whether staffers have “continued
employment,” according to an internal Proton email obtained by 404 Media.
“These re-interviews are consequential,” a part of the email reads. “Interviewers
will document their assessments, which will inform the broader review of our
organization and talent. That review may lead to changes in roles,
responsibilities, and continued employment. Individual outcomes will be
communicated directly once decisions are finalized.”
