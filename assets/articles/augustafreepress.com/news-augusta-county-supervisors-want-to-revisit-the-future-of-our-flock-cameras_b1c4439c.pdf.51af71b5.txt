Trump reiterated, when pressed by a reporter on Air Force One on Sunday, that he likes the
automated license plate readers, “because of law enforcement.”
The sentiment from members of the Augusta County Board of Supervisors who brought up the
Sheriff’s Office contract with Flock for a mere two cameras in the Fishersville area at a recent
BOS meeting is more on the side of privacy protections for the citizenry.
From the archives
Coverage of issues raised with Flock cameras: click here.
Scott Seaton, who represents the Wayne District, where those two cameras are located, said
last week that he wants the Board to review the contract, noting the low hit rate – he said the two
cameras had recorded, in the 30 days prior to last week’s Board of Supervisors meeting, eight
hits for cars that may have ties to some sort of crime from more than 65,000 surveillance
opportunities, “and that gives it a ratio of pretty much 999,999 innocent people for each person,
for each car, that’s on the hit list,” Seaton said.
It’s not quite 999,999 to one, but his point is made.
Steve Grepps, who represents the Beverley Manor District, piled on, saying he has spoken to
“quite a few people, either via phone or out in public, and they’re not in support of the Flock
cameras, and neither am I, and I just wanted to make that point clear.”
We can expect the BOS to discuss this at their next staff briefing, which is on the schedule for
Monday, Sept. 21.
How much Trump’s support of Flock cameras “because of law enforcement” will factor into the
review will be worth taking a look at.
