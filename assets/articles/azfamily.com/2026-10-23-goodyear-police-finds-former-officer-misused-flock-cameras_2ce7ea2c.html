Goodyear police finds former officer misused Flock cameras

Chief says officer would have been fired
By Ben Bradley
Published: Oct. 23, 2026 at 1:22 PM MST|Updated: 11 hours ago
Add Us On GoogleAdd as a preferred source on Google

GOODYEAR, AZ (AZFamily) — The Goodyear Police Department has completed an investigation into the alleged misuse of a license plate camera system, finding that an officer would have been fired had he not already resigned.

During a routine audit of Flock automated license plate reader (ALPR) activity in July, internal affairs flagged a possible violation of department policy. The officer identified during the audit was immediately placed on leave and restricted from accessing Goodyear PD computer systems.

The department then reached out to Glendale police to investigate whether the misuse could lead to criminal charges. Glendale investigators later found there wasn’t enough evidence to support charges.

Meanwhile, Goodyear police continued its administrative investigation into the alleged misuse of the database and other policy violations. The officer in question resigned on Sept. 17 as the investigation was ongoing.

Goodyear police say the investigation was recently completed and found that the officer committed three policy violations: abuse of authority, violation of the department’s body-worn camera policy and dishonesty.

The officer was with the department from October 2023 until his resignation.

Police Chief Brian Issitt said the officer would have been fired had he not left the department. He released the following statement:

“Accountability does not end when an employee resigns. Our community places an extraordinary amount of trust in our officers, particularly when it comes to accessing sensitive law enforcement information. That trust comes with an expectation that every employee will follow the law, uphold department policies and exercise sound judgment. Based on the findings of this investigation, I would have upheld the termination of this officer had he remained employed by the Goodyear Police Department. Resignation does not erase misconduct, nor does it relieve us of our responsibility to investigate it. We followed this investigation through to its conclusion because our community deserves nothing less.”

Goodyear Police Chief Brian Issitt

Law enforcement agencies in the Valley and other parts of Arizona conducted similar investigations as privacy concerns over ALPR cameras grew over the summer, leading to resignations.

An Apache Junction officer resigned in August after investigators said he used Flock cameras to track his wife. Days later, a Chandler officer resigned for reportedly using the system to track a family member.

While many agencies defend the use of ALPR cameras in solving crimes, others have committed to shutting them down. Tempe permanently banned the use of ALPR systems earlier this month.
