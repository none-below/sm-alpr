Photo courtesy Flock Safety Chestnut Hill Realty is pausing its installation of controversial Flock Safety license plate reader cameras in South Brookline after feedback from the community, the company said Tuesday. In a statement shared with Brookline . News, Chestnut Hill Realty’s chief operating officer Jennifer Murphy said the real estate company “appreciates the feedback from the Brookline Select Board and members of the community regarding our planned installation of Flock Safety cameras on Independence Drive in South Brookline.” Get our FREE newsletter in your inbox; now twice a week! We do not sell our email list. “In response to this input, we have decided to place the installation on hold for now. No cameras have been installed or activated, thus no data is being collected or shared,” the statement continued. Flock cameras capture license plate numbers from passing cars, and the company also operates a powerful data-sharing system intended to assist law enforcement agencies. After Brookline . News reported last month the company’s plans to install the cameras, the decision was met with widespread criticism from residents and Town Meeting members, who expressed concern about the expansion of mass surveillance and Flock’s track record, including its past collaborations with federal immigration authorities and legal clashes with municipalities . At its meeting last week, the Select Board began considering whether to allow the Brookline Police Department to use real-time data from Chestnut Hill Realty’s planned Flock cameras. Three of the board’s five members expressed reservations, but the board did not take a vote. Murphy said the company hopes to continue a conversation around the cameras. “Our intent has always been to enhance public safety by equipping our law enforcement partners with effective tools to help deter crime,” she said in the statement. “We respect the community’s and the Town’s concerns around policy and privacy related to this technology and are hopeful that, with additional time and discussion, those concerns can be addressed.” Brookline police leaders have advocated publicly for using real-time data from the license plate readers, and said the data would help them respond more quickly and efficiently to crime in Brookline.
