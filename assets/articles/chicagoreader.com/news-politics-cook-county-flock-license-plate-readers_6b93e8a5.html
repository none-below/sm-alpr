Cook County expands Flock license plate reader network

The Cook County board approved Sheriff Tom Dart’s $1 million request to expand his license plate reader network, even as his office admitted it used the controversial technology for years without an active contract.
by Shawn Mulcahy May 19, 2026May 19, 2026

The Cook County Board of Commissioners on May 14 approved roughly $1 million to nearly double the size of Sheriff Tom Dart’s license plate reader network.

The vote came a day after the sheriff’s office admitted it had worked with the surveillance company Flock Safety for years without an active contract governing the collection or use of private data. It also came amid a recent state audit that found Flock and other license plate reader companies had illegally shared driver data with federal immigration agents.

All but three commissioners—Alma Anaya, Tara Stamps, and Jessica Vásquez—voted in favor of Dart’s request for more than 50 new license plate readers, bringing his office’s countywide total to 125. During a May 13 hearing of the board’s Criminal Justice Committee, the three dissenting commissioners questioned Dart’s transparency around the surveillance technology and how the contract was awarded. 

“I am not at issue with the goal of what the sheriff wants to accomplish,” Vásquez said. “I am at issue with this vendor, who has been highlighted across the country in terms of violating contract agreements.”

The $900,000 contract is not with Flock but with Insight Public Sector, a reseller of the software and cameras. The sheriff’s office used a process called cooperative purchasing to piggyback on a contract negotiated by another jurisdiction. Such agreements are not subject to the county’s standard public notice and competitive bidding requirements. “It’s not considered our contract. It’s not considered the sheriff’s contract,” Anaya said. “It’s considered adding yourself to a contract that exists, which means you did not have any say in the way” it was procured.  

Dart’s use of Flock has never been subject to the county’s competitive bidding process. The sheriff’s office signed an emergency contract with Flock in fall 2022. At the May 13 committee hearing, deputy chief information officer Douglas Maclean testified the move was driven by a “rash” of “vehicle-related crime,” such as carjackings and drive-by shootings. The $75,000 contract for 25 license plate readers and Flock’s search software expired the following year, yet Maclean said the sheriff’s office has continued working with the company nonetheless. “Have we been acting without a contract? Yes, we have,” Maclean said. 

Maclean’s admission raises questions about the collection and use of private data by Flock, which the Illinois Secretary of State’s Office determined had illegally shared information with U.S. Customs and Border Patrol agents and with a Texas sheriff’s office that was investigating a woman who had allegedly self-administered an abortion. The Illinois TRUST Act prohibits local law enforcement agencies from cooperating with federal agents on civil immigration enforcement. A 2023 state law separately bars the use of license plate readers for tracking people seeking abortion care. 

In response to questions from the Reader, Matthew Walberg, Dart’s communications director, wrote in an email, “All data collection was subject to the terms of the 2022 contract.” He added that no Flock equipment was operated outside the scope of the agreement, and that, despite lacking an active contract, “the agency continued to pay Flock for the service on a month-to-month basis, following the policies and procedures that were outlined under the terms of the 2022” deal.

A provision in the contract for a “No-Fee Term” allowed Dart’s office to continue receiving complimentary “hotlist alerts,” which notify police in real time when a designated license plate is captured on a camera. It’s unclear whether the provision covered broader use of the technology or was limited to alerts from other agencies with active Flock contracts. 

A map of Flock license plate readers used by the Cook County Sheriff’s Office, based on an April 2026 response to a public records request.

After the contract expired in 2023, the sheriff’s office not only continued using Flock but also expanded its license plate reader network from the original 25 cameras to more than 70. It’s unclear how Dart financed the purchase of additional cameras, and Walberg repeatedly declined to elaborate.

The $900,000 approved on May 14 is in addition to the $815 million set aside for the sheriff’s office in 2026. A $300,000 line item in the county’s budget for another brand of license plate readers, Genetec, is unrelated, wrote Walberg. “The Genetec contract is for the installation of [license plate readers] at secure Sheriff’s Office parking locations to enhance our ability to ensure only authorized vehicles are allowed entry.”

Amar Patel, the sheriff’s office’s chief information officer, told the Criminal Justice Committee on May 13 that more than two hundred law enforcement agencies across Illinois have access to the sheriff’s office’s license plate readers. Patel said the agency audits the Flock user logs monthly to monitor for unauthorized access, but he acknowledged, in response to a question from Commissioner Kevin Morrison, that the sheriff’s office cannot monitor what authorized agencies do with the data they access.

The county board’s decision to expand its relationship with Flock comes as other municipalities are cutting ties with the company. In August, the Village of Oak Park voted to cancel its contract with Flock amid sustained community pressure against surveillance technology. That same month, the City of Evanston sent a news release announcing it, too, was pulling the plug. 

“The findings of the Illinois Secretary of State’s audit, combined with Flock’s admission that it failed to establish distinct permissions and protocols to ensure local compliance while running a pilot program with federal users, are deeply troubling,” Evanston officials wrote. “As a result, the City has deactivated the cameras and issued a termination notice to Flock.”
