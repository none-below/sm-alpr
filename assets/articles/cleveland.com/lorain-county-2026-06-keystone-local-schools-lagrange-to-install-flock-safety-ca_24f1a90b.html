Keystone Local Schools, LaGrange to install Flock Safety cameras on campuses and throughout village

  • Updated: Jun. 25, 2026, 4:08 p.m.
  • |Published: Jun. 25, 2026, 3:56 p.m.
By
  • Hannah Drown, cleveland.com

LaGRANGE, Ohio -- Keystone Local Schools and LaGrange officials are partnering to install Flock Safety license plate reader cameras at school campuses and other strategic locations throughout the village.

In a joint letter to school district families and community members posted on Facebook, officials said the cameras are intended to provide law enforcement with objective information to assist investigations, while including safeguards to protect residents’ privacy.

According to the announcement, the cameras can help investigators locate stolen vehicles, identify vehicles connected to criminal activity, assist with missing person investigations and provide timely information during serious incidents on or near school property.

“Every day, our schools welcome hundreds of students, staff, and visitors,” the letter states. “While our community is a safe and welcoming one, we also recognize our responsibility to stay ahead of challenges and give our first responders the tools they need to act quickly when it matters most.”

The school district’s contract with Flock Safety for equipment and professional services passed unanimously at Keystone Board of Education’s June 9 meeting.

cleveland.com reached out to Superintendent Zach Weagley for comment.

The announcement outlined what the cameras will capture, such as license plates and vehicle characteristics, as well as what they won’t do — use facial recognition technology, collect personal identifying information or track the daily routines of residents or students.

Officials assured the public that access to the system would be restricted and monitored through detailed audit trails. However, comments on the school district’s announcement post on Facebook were largely against incorporating the cameras on school property, with dozens of people voicing their strong opposition due to financial and privacy concerns.

The district has not shared the the cost of the contract or the where the cameras will be located on school property.

Officials acknowledged that the technology is not intended to prevent every crime, but said communities around the country have used Flock cameras to strengthen investigations, improve situational awareness and help first responders respond more efficiently.

The installation comes as Flock Safety cameras have become increasingly common across Northeast Ohio, where many communities use automated license plate readers to assist criminal investigations.

The technology has also drawn scrutiny from privacy advocates and defense attorneys, who argue the growing network of cameras can amount to widespread government surveillance because they record the movements of vehicles traveling on public roads.
