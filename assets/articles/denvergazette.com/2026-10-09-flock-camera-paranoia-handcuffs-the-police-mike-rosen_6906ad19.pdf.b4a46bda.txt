One of the most popular formats on network television and streaming services like Netflix
and Prime Video has been police and federal-agent crime-fighting dramas like “Law &
Order”, “Blue Bloods” and “NCIS”. Within that category are formats like “Chicago PD” and
“FBI” that routinely include high-speed car chases (and foot chases on “FBI)” that especially
feature forensic investigative, pursuit and capture technology. Showrunners aren’t making
this stuff up. Their production staffs obviously retain experienced expert consultants with
state-of-the-art technical knowledge.  
If I were a fugitive on the lam from a serious felony, I’d now be well aware to instantly get off
and stay off the grid. That means no credit card use.  No cellphone calls, lest I’m “pinged” at
a tower. Staying away from my LKA (last known address). Abandoning my own car on which
there’s a BOLO (be on the lookout for) alert. Better, I should steal a car, although that info
will be put out over the police network soon enough. Then there are the omnipresent

To make matters even worse, criminals have to contend with the dreaded Flock cameras,
an invention of the Atlanta-based Flock Safety company whose crime-fighting systems are
highly-valued by law enforcement agencies nationwide as force multipliers for solving
homicides, kidnappings, assaults, vehicle thefts, jewelry store robberies, missing persons,
human trafficking and more. It was a Flock camera in Boulder that quickly located the car
and aided police in the arrest and conviction for murder of the Islamist antisemitic terrorist
who firebombed a crowd on the Pearl Street Mall.    
Flock’s automated license plate readers, installed at intersections all over town with the
consent of local governments across the country, photograph the plate and record details,
including GPS locations, of every passing vehicle and compare the data to a “hot list” stored
in the Flock system of all wanted plates nationwide. Local police can be notified of the hot
plate vehicle in as little as 16 seconds.

Despite the claims of anti-Flock paranoids, their cameras don’t record your Social Security
number or what takes place in the privacy of your bedroom.    
One critic equated Flock’s cameras with Big Brother’s mass surveillance of the entire
populace in George Orwell’s classic dystopian novel, “1984.” But BB’s cameras were hidden
in two-way TV screens in everyone’s homes! Flock’s cameras are only in public venues. As
a law-abiding citizen who roots for the cops and FBI agents, I’m grateful for this modern
technology. I have nothing to hide in public places. This technology makes me safer by
helping to capture the robbers and other bad guys.      
As for personal privacy, don’t be naïve. We live in a technological era that already
compromises much of our privacy through our own behavior in internet interactions with
countless servicers, retailers, unknown scammers and on social media.   We’re easy prey
for sophisticated computer and email hackers. Your only sanctuary is to become a recluse.  
Another silly ploy of the anti-Flockers is to falsely invoke the Fourth Amendment’s protection
from unreasonable search and seizure, and the requirement of a warrant supported by a
probable cause. This is beyond Flock’s capabilities. And the Fourth Amendment isn’t
absolute; gray areas have forced many exceptions. Like allowing mass searching of
travelers by TSA agents at airports without “probable cause.” This is a rational tradeoff to
protect travelers from getting blown up in flight by terrorists or crashing into the World Trade
Center and Pentagon again. Will the next contrivance to handcuff crimefighters be
outlawing fingerprinting and DNA evidence? 
A large element of those who oppose Flock cameras are sanctuary-city activists who want
open borders, and who coddle and defend illegal immigrants. They don’t want data from
those cameras to be shared with federal authorities like ICE because they don’t want these
people or any illegal immigrants (including the criminals among them) to be deported, even
if Flock technology will assist crimefighters in apprehending criminals of all kinds. The
organizations and lawyers that support those here illegally describe themselves as
defenders of the rights of “undocumented immigrants.” More accurately, they’re defenders
of “illegal immigrant aliens.” If you drive a car without a license, that’s illegal; you’re not an
“undocumented driver.”
