(Stephen Swofford, Denver Gazette) ADVERTISEMENT ADVERTISEMENT The city of Golden is currently exploring how to exit its contract with the license plate reader company Flock and establish a framework for surveillance technology within the city. Golden has used Flock since 2023, according to an agenda packet for Tuesday’s City Council meeting. The decision to use Flock came in response to “extremely high” motor theft rates across the metro Denver region. The city’s current contract with Flock is set to expire in July 2028; the city pays Flock $27,500 yearly for the use of its cameras. Automated license plate readers like Flock have come under increased scrutiny over the past year as questions have arisen about privacy, surveillance and how the data collected by Flock cameras are used. Supporters of the cameras argue they make crimes easier to solve and prevent, while those opposed to the technology argue the cameras threaten the public’s right to privacy. ADVERTISEMENT ADVERTISEMENT Aurora Police Department officials recently said that Flock cameras at a high school led to arrests, but the department later shut off the cameras at the request of Aurora councilmembers. Many other police departments across Colorado have terminated their contracts with Flock in the wake of the controversy. Golden is set to discuss the city’s contract with Flock during a study session at Tuesday’s council meeting. During the time the city has used the cameras, they have proved valuable to the city in assisting with criminal investigations, including property recovery, missing persons cases, serving arrest warrants and collaborating with other area police departments, according to a memo from Golden Police Department Chief Michele Wagner. As concerns about Flock have been shared with the department, Wagner wrote that Golden PD has created amendments to its policy on Flock cameras, which implemented an audit process, limited the sharing of Flock data from Golden and limited data retention to 30 days. ADVERTISEMENT ADVERTISEMENT “The Golden Police Department is committed to the responsible use of this technology and the guardrails instituted to ensure this,” Wagner wrote. “The Golden Police Department believes this tool can be used responsibly to enhance public safety while maintaining the public’s trust.” Wagner also said the technology of ALPR cameras allows officers to search for license plate information more quickly. The cameras read and log the license plates of all cars that pass by, and can immediately notify law enforcement if an alert has been issued for the car, she wrote, and can also be used to search for specific cars when investigating a crime. According to a new business proposal also included in the City Council agenda packet, the proposal seeks to discontinue Golden’s use of Flock cameras and establish a “citywide and publicly available” framework for future usage of surveillance technology and data governance. The proposal is sponsored by councilmembers Sandra Knecht and Emily Kurzinski. The possible termination of the city’s contract with Flock comes after the council received several public comments regarding the privacy concerns of Flock and the accessibility of the data recorded by the cameras, which the proposal notes as a possible violation of the right to privacy. ADVERTISEMENT ADVERTISEMENT “The data scale, searchability, sharing, and analytical capabilities of modern surveillance networks create privacy and civil liberties concerns that violate the constitutional rights for our community, extending beyond any individual camera or law enforcement agency,” the proposal said. “We must protect our community from unconstitutional and pervasive surveillance.” In addition to seeking to terminate the city’s contract with Flock, the council also plans to establish a “comprehensive” framework for Golden that would govern any future acquisition of surveillance technologies. The evaluation would include looking at the data collection, search standards, sharing of data, AI analysis and “necessary stringent controls to prevent harm” to Golden. The meeting starts at 5 p.m. Tuesday, followed by the regular City Council meeting .
