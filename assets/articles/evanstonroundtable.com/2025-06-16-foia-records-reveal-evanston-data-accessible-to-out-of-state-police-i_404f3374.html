FOIA records reveal Evanston data accessible to out-of-state police making ICE searches

Unclear whether city responded to immigration-enforcement searches
by Alex Harrison June 16th, 2025August 28th, 2025

The Evanston Police Department shared its network of 18 automatic license plate readers with at least seven out-of-state agencies that sent vehicle searches to other Illinois cities for immigration enforcement cases, according to a RoundTable analysis of public records obtained via the Freedom of Information Act.

The agencies were matched between records disclosed by EPD and the police departments of Mount Prospect and Danville, all of which contract with surveillance technology company Flock Safety to use its license plate reader hardware and software. Evanston has 18 stationary cameras, primarily installed along the city’s boundaries with Chicago to the south and Skokie to the west, as well as a mobile “flex” camera that can be attached to a squad car.

Independent outlet 404 Media reported in May that Mount Prospect and Danville had been included in out-of-state searches for cases concerning abortion and immigration enforcement.

In response, lllinois Secretary of State Alexi Giannoulias on Thursday ordered a statewide audit of license plate reader use to ensure compliance with a 2023 state law that prohibits agencies from sharing their data with agencies investigating people for accessing reproductive health or over their immigration status.

Agencies that use Flock are able to search data from cameras well beyond their own borders, allowing for broad searches that include data from tens of thousands of cameras across the country.

In addition to the protections of state law, Evanston updated its Welcoming City Ordinance in January to prohibit sharing data with federal immigration agencies or groups that assist with civil immigration enforcement, and on June 23 City Council is set to consider a similar Health Data Protection Ordinance designed to protect reproductive and gender-affirming health care.

Matching out-of-state agencies

The RoundTable cross-referenced the list of agencies EPD shares its Flock network with as of June 4, obtained through a FOIA request, with “network audit” records listing information on external license plate reader searches posted to public records database MuckRock: one set for Mount Prospect with audit data through May 23, and two sets for Danville with audit data through May 4.

This comparison identified seven non-Illinois city and state police departments that were able to search EPD’s license plate reader network as of June 4 and also had made at least one 2025 search with “immigration” or “ICE” in the “reason” field that included Mount Prospect, Danville or both:

  • Sterling Heights (Michigan) Police Department: One search to Mount Prospect for “DIAZ immigration” on Jan. 14.
  • Wichita Falls (Texas) Police Department: Two searches to Mount Prospect for “ICE [U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement] HIT” on March 15 and 17.
  • Muskegon County (Michigan) Sheriff’s Office: Eight searches to Mount Prospect for “ice detain” on March 23.
  • Richmond (Virginia) Police Department: 35 searches to Mount Prospect for “ice ero” (Enforcement and Removal Operations) between March 31 and April 9.
  • Coffeyville (Kansas) Police Department: Five searches to Mount Prospect and Danville for “ICE” on April 10.
  • Loveland (Colorado) Police Department: Six searches to Mount Prospect and Danville for “ICE” on April 22 and 24.
  • Michigan State Police: 11 searches to Mount Prospect for “USBP [U.S. Border Patrol] Immigration Investigation” on May 19.

The “reason” field is self-reported by the searching agencies, and can vary widely in detail and phrasing. The RoundTable only included searches with reasons that began with the word “ICE” or included the word “immigration” in its analysis, and manually verified the resulting data to ensure accuracy.

Cases with other terms like “DHS” (U.S. Department of Homeland Security) or “HSI” (Homeland Security Investigations) could be related to immigration enforcement, but cannot be verified as such and were excluded from the RoundTable’s analysis.

Further, these matches do not confirm whether EPD actually received or responded to any immigration-related searches from these seven agencies or any others. The RoundTable requested EPD’s network audit records, which would show whether any such searches were made in Evanston’s data, but the city denied that records request on June 4, citing “ongoing investigations.” An appeal with the city is still outstanding at time of publication.

‘It at least warrants review’

In a phone call Sunday evening, EPD Cmdr. Ryan Glew said the department has received correspondence from Flock that “a small number” of non-Illinois agencies “appeared to have used a national lookup to access LPR [license plate reader] data” for immigration-related cases.

In a Thursday online post, viewable here as an archive, Flock stated that by May 30 it had revoked Illinois network access to 17 out-of-state agencies based on an internal audit, and later revoked access for another 29 at Giannoulias’ request. As of Sunday night, the post has been edited to state only that 47 agencies have been removed based on the company’s audit.

Glew said Flock’s communication said external agencies requesting Illinois license plate reader data have to “check that box” to agree the records won’t be used in immigration or reproductive health cases, and that the company had “removed access to Illinois LPR data” for agencies that violated this agreement. He said he later emailed Flock over the weekend to ask about the seven agencies identified by the RoundTable, but hadn’t heard back as of Sunday night.

“It at least warrants review,” Glew said of the seven matching agencies. “I’m not aware of any time our LPR data has been used to make an immigration arrest, charge anybody from out of state with a reproductive health issue — not aware of any.”

He said the department feels “very, very strongly of the value of this program” locally, citing the February 2023 hit-and-run of Soapie’s owner Yun Park and an April 2023 fatal shooting as instances where the technology aided investigations.

Nevertheless, Glew said EPD is “constantly reviewing our policy” for automated license plate reader data, even before Giannoulias entered the picture last week.

“If you’re going to have an ALPR system, you can’t have any reasonable expectation people are not going to want to know how it’s used,” Glew said, “and that they’re not going to be vigilant or hawkish with regards to privacy.”

Glew said he will verify at the start of the week whether the seven identified agencies were among those that had their access to EPD’s data revoked.
