Credit: Alex Harrison Update: On Wednesday, Flock Safety challenged the city’s intent to terminate its contract. Editor’s note: Leer este articulo en español . The City of Evanston has shut down its network of automated license plate readers and will end its contract with vendor Flock Safety early after a state audit found the company was illegally sharing Illinois data with federal agencies. A city news release issued Tuesday evening says that all 19 cameras operated by the city are “deactivated,” and that the city issued a notice to terminate its contract effective Sept. 26. The decision is attributed to the findings of a system audit ordered by Illinois Secretary of State Alexi Giannoulias, which were announced Monday afternoon. “The findings of the Illinois Secretary of State’s audit,” the city’s statement reads, “combined with Flock’s admission that it failed to establish distinct permissions and protocols to ensure local compliance while running a pilot program with federal users, are deeply troubling.” Giannoulias ordered the audit in late June after technology news outlet 404 Media reported that out-of-state police had searched cameras in Illinois cities for use in immigration enforcement. The RoundTable reported shortly after that several non-Illinois agencies that Evanston shared data with had made immigration-related searches, meaning Evanston’s cameras could have been searched as well. Police Chief Schenita Stewart soon implemented new restrictions, including cutting off all non-Illinois agencies from accessing Evanston’s camera data. previous coverage FOIA records reveal Evanston data accessible to out-of-state police making ICE searches by Alex Harrison June 16th, 2025 August 28th, 2025 Council Bytes: EPD bars out-of-state agencies from plate reader network by Alex Harrison and Emma Richman June 24th, 2025 July 13th, 2025 Davis proposes 24-hour time limit for holding license plate camera data by Alex Harrison July 3rd, 2025 July 3rd, 2025 A Monday news release from Giannoulias’ office says the audit revealed Flock “allowed U.S. Customs and Border Protection to access Illinois license plate cameras on Illinois roads and surveil drivers.” This was through a “pilot program” with federal agencies that “Flock leadership was unaware of,” and Giannoulias charges it violated a state law that took effect in 2024, which prohibits sharing plate reader data for investigations concerning reproductive health care or immigration. “The reality is that we are dealing with powerful surveillance technology, and it has been abused, which is extremely troubling, especially as it violates the law,” Giannoulias said in an accompanying video . “We will not tolerate any violations to our data sharing and privacy laws.” Evanston first contracted with Flock for 10 devices in December 2022 , which was invoiced for $24,999.90 in January 2023. Two months later, in March the city paid an additional $24,996 for the remaining eight license plate readers, a mobile “Flex” unit and implementation fees. Most of the stationary cameras are placed along the city’s borders with Chicago and Skokie, tracking vehicles as they enter and exit the city. Flock CEO Garrett Langley released a statement for the company on Monday titled “Ensuring Local Compliance,” where he writes that “some of our public statements inadvertently provided inaccurate information” about the company’s relationship with federal law enforcement, confirming that there were “limited pilots” with agencies under the U.S. Department of Homeland Security focused on “combatting human trafficking and fentanyl distribution.” “We clearly communicated poorly. We also didn’t create distinct permissions and protocols in the Flock system to ensure local compliance for federal agency users,” Langley wrote. “As of last week, all ongoing federal pilots have been paused.” Evanston Mayor Daniel Biss, who does not direct administrative decisions for EPD but has publicly voiced concern about Flock in the past, supported the termination in a text sent Tuesday evening. “Keeping Evanston safe means keeping Evanstonians’ data safe, which is why we’ve passed some of the strongest health and immigration data protections in the nation,” Biss wrote. “This vendor has failed to follow state and local data protection laws. With constant attacks from the federal government and other far-right actors, we will not sit idly by while our people’s data is shared inappropriately.” Support the RoundTable!
