Daily Brief Evanston’s latest news and features Raising Evanston 1X week: everything E-town for families Sign up Flock Safety , the private surveillance vendor hired to provide automated license plate readers to police in Evanston and thousands of other communities, is challenging the city’s intent to terminate the remainder of its contract early in light of alleged violations of state and local laws regulating how the reader network’s data can be shared. City officials ordered Flock to deactivate all 19 cameras and “issued a termination notice” to the company on Tuesday, according to a news release sent the same afternoon. This rapid shutdown followed a Monday announcement from Alexi Giannoulias, Illinois’ secretary of state, that his office’s audit of Flock networks across the state revealed that Illinois data had been shared with U.S. Customs and Border Protection through a “pilot program,” which he charged was a violation of a 2024 law that prohibits sharing the data for investigations concerning reproductive health care or immigration. previous coverage Evanston shuts down license plate cameras, terminates contract with Flock Safety by Alex Harrison August 26th, 2025 August 28th, 2025 The city released a copy of its termination letter Thursday afternoon through a Freedom of Information Act request. In it, Corporation Counsel Alex Ruggie writes that the city is “exercising its right to terminate the contract” and cites Section 11.1 of Flock’s terms and conditions , which requires both parties comply “with all applicable local, state and federal laws, regulations, policies and ordinances.” “Flock unlawfully made data collected within Evanston and the State of Illinois available to federal agencies,” Ruggie wrote, referencing the findings of Giannoulias’ audit. “This is not a procedural error; it is an intentional and unauthorized disclosure of protected data… Let it be absolutely clear: this breach is material, intentional, and cannot be cured. The City will not entertain remediation efforts or renegotiation.” COE Flock Cancellation Notice 25.08.26 Download The letter was intended to serve as a 30-day notice for the contract’s full termiantion effective Sept. 26. Beyond state law, Evanston’s Welcoming City Ordinance was also updated in January to explicitly prohibit sharing data with federal immigration agencies or groups that assist with civil immigration enforcement. Flock sent the city a response letter Wednesday written by Dan Haley, the company’s chief legal officer. A copy was shared with the RoundTable by a company spokesperson, Josh Thomas. Haley confirmed that Flock has deactivated all of EPD’s cameras as directed, but rebuked the termination notice as being “replete with conclusory and unsupported assertions” and wrote that the company “denies categorically” that it broke any laws or terms of the contract. He argues the city failed to actually specify a breach or allow Flock to try to cure it within 30 days of the notice, as required in the terms and conditions to allow for full termination. “We are aware of no legal basis for your apparent belief that the City may relieve itself of clear and unambiguous contractual obligations, simply by declaration, however emphatic, of its intention not to be bound by them,” Haley wrote. “Flock is likewise aware of no substantiated basis for the allegations contained in the Termination Letter, and is therefore presently at a loss as to what measures we must take within the Cure Period to fulfill our obligations.” Flock Response to Evanston 25.08.27 Download He also references “recent public statements and allegations” about Flock that he calls “unsubstantiated and incorrect,” referring to Giannoulias’ announcement. He writes that if the city doesn’t identify specific breaches to its satisfaction and allow for a “contractually-required opportunity to cure,” Flock intends to “take all necessary steps to enforce” the contract. If the contract is upheld, the city wouldn’t be compelled to reactivate the cameras but would be required to pay for the remaining years. The current five-year contract was approved by City Council on Jan. 8, 2024 at a rate of $48,500 per year, and Haley states in his letter that its term runs through Jan. 29, 2029. The city has paid the annual rate for the first two years, meaning it would owe $145,500 for the last three years ahead. City spokesperson Cynthia Vargas declined to comment on this story. Thomas, the Flock spokesperson, wrote in a statement accompanying the letter that the company “is unaware of any ongoing investigation by the Illinois Secretary of State’s office.” “We disagree, respectfully, with the Secretary of State’s legal assertions that Flock broke the law,” Thomas wrote. “We believe it is upon these unfounded allegations that Evanston requested to end the contract with Flock.” Editor’s note: This story has been updated to include additional details from the city’s termination notice, and to embed the two letters within the story.
