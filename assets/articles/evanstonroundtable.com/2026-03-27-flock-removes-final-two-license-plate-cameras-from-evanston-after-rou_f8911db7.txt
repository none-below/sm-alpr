Daily Brief Evanston’s latest news and features Raising Evanston 1X week: everything E-town for families Sign up Despite the City of Evanston ordering Flock Safety to take down all of its automated license plate readers last August, the company left two of the cameras installed and covered for the last six months, before swiftly removing them this week after the RoundTable requested comment on their presence. These lingering cameras, along with other city records and communications, suggested the contract between the city and company may still be in legal limbo despite the city’s prior intent to cancel it entirely. The Evanston Police Department began using Flock’s AI-powered surveillance camera network in December 2022 , and City Council approved a five-year contract extension in January 2024. Following reports last summer that the company shared vehicle data with federal law enforcement agencies, including U.S. Customs and Border Protection, the city moved in August to terminate the contract early and remove the 18 stationary cameras installed around Evanston, mostly on major streets near the city’s borders with Chicago and Skokie. Flock challenged that termination and later reinstalled the cameras without authorization, prompting the city to issue a cease-and-desist and cover the remaining cameras in plastic last September. previous coverage FOIA records reveal Evanston data accessible to out-of-state police making ICE searches by Alex Harrison June 16th, 2025 August 28th, 2025 Flock challenges city’s termination of contract for license plate readers by Alex Harrison August 28th, 2025 August 28th, 2025 City covers Flock cameras while waiting for removal by Alex Harrison September 25th, 2025 September 26th, 2025 Most cameras were later removed again, but the RoundTable observed that two south-facing cameras north of Howard Street on Ridge and Dodge Avenues were still in place as recently as Wednesday evening. The two cameras were mounted on and wired into public streetlight fixtures along with connected equipment, and both were still covered with the plastic placed by the city in September. However, both cameras have since been removed after the RoundTable contacted Flock on Thursday morning with questions about them and the status of the contract dispute. At both locations, Flock left behind metal utility boxes that are still wired into the lightpoles, which have stickers on them with the company’s name and a QR code. The cameras’ extended presence suggests the city still hasn’t fully terminated the contract, which has a $145,500 balance for the remaining three years. Flock’s Chief Legal Officer Dan Haley previously rebuked the city’s August termination notice as being “replete with conclusory and unsupported assertions” in a response letter to the city , and asserted the company would “take all necessary steps to enforce” the contract if needed. However, neither side appears to have filed a lawsuit over the issue at time of writing, and the city also hasn’t paid Flock its annual subscription fee for 2026. Two previous annual payments were on bills lists approved by City Council on April 8, 2024 and Feb. 10, 2025 , but no further payments appear on any bills list approved by the council since then. When asked why the two cameras were still installed and what the status of the contract was, a Flock spokesperson deferred to the city. City spokesperson Cynthia Vargas wrote that the city “is still working out a resolution to the terminated contract” and did not comment on why the cameras were still present for the last six months. Dozens of other cities have also moved to terminate their contracts with Flock , including Verona, Wisconsin, where city officials similarly covered Flock’s cameras when the company refused to remove them after the city canceled its contract. Emails show CEO called police chief, lawyers discussed ‘proposal’ While the city and Flock have been publicly silent since September, emails obtained via Freedom of Information Act requests show the two sides communicated throughout the fall, as Flock tried to repair its relationship with EPD to apparently limited success. On Sept. 18, Corporation Counsel Alex Ruggie ordered Flock to cease and desist reinstalling the cameras in an email to Haley, and then sent him a new letter laying out the reasons for the city’s termination notice and asserting that “the City cannot continue this partnership.” 25.09.18 City letter to Flock Download Haley responded the next day that there were “central assertions” in Ruggie’s letter that were incorrect, and asked to schedule a call with her to discuss. “We’d much prefer to explain via a constructive conversation with a valued partner, rather than in a formal, adversarial proceeding,” Haley wrote, alluding to the possibility of a lawsuit. Ruggie agreed, and the two had a one-one-one call on Sept. 23. Haley wrote in a follow-up email that Flock was “excited to have an opportunity to get ourselves and our relationship with Evanston back on a constructive footing” and agreed to remove the cameras “as an initial step.” He also wrote that “per our discussion,” he would set up a connection between Flock and EPD, specifically Chief Schenita Stewart and Sgt. Tom Giese, who managed the department’s use of the camera network. An email from Flock CLO Dan Haley to Corporation Counsel Alex Ruggie, sent after their one-on-one call on Sept. 23, 2025. Credit: City of Evanston Ruggie later asked Haley two times about when the cameras would be removed, and Haley wrote on Sept. 24 that “We’ll have five down this week and the rest next week.” The RoundTable reported on the cameras being reinstalled on Sept. 24, and the city began covering them with black plastic on Sept. 25. Flock’s re-connection with EPD began on Sept. 25 when regional sales manager Mike Hutton emailed Stewart and Giese, writing that Flock “regret[s] the current state of affairs” and asking to set up a call to “try to get our partnership back on track.” “We want you to know that we fully understand the difficult position our prior missteps put you in,” Hutton wrote. “While we maintain that no violation of law occurred, and that no breach of our Agreement occurred, we understand that you put your credibility on the line in support of our partnership, and we let you down.” An email from Flock sales manager Mike Hutton to EPD Chief Schenita Stewart and Sgt. Tom Giese seeking to schedule a call. Credit: City of Evanston That call, which happened on Oct. 3, apparently did not go well: Haley wrote to Ruggie on Oct. 6 describing it as “a mixed bag” and sharing that the company’s top leadership would step in to mitigate in the aftermath. “After hearing a read-out of that [Oct. 3] call, our CEO, Garrett [Langley], is going to place a call to the Chief to apologize directly for the events of the past couple of months,” Haley wrote. “Our law enforcement relationships are of paramount importance to us, and whether or not we have a chance at salvaging this one in the near term, it is important to him to give the Chief the opportunity to hear from him directly, and to address any lingering concerns to him, equally directly.” An email from Flock CLO Dan Haley to Corporation Counsel Alex Ruggie, sent after the Oct. 3 call between sales manager Mike Hutton and EPD Chief Stewart and Sgt. Giese. Langley spoke to Stewart on Oct. 19, after which Haley and Ruggie had a follow-up call on Oct. 23. The emails from this time don’t include details on how the calls went or what was specifically discussed, but another follow-up email from Haley on Nov. 10 reveals he and Ruggie had discussed some kind of proposal between Flock and the city. “Let me know if you were able to get any feedback on the proposal we discussed” on Oct. 23, Haley wrote, “or whether we need to move forward to negotiate a resolution.” The pair had one more call on Nov. 13. The city did not provide any further emails between Ruggie and Haley or anyone else at Flock after that point through Feb. 25, writing that “the public records requested do not exist.” Flock did not respond to questions asking what was discussed in these calls, what the substance of the “proposal” was or why email communications stopped after the Nov. 13 call. Vargas, the city’s spokesperson, wrote that the “proposal” concerned Flock making an offer to “continue the relationship,” which the city declined, and declined to comment on the Oct. 3 and 19 calls attended by Stewart. As for why the emails suddenly stopped, Vargas wrote that “there have been phone call discussions.” Support the RoundTable!
