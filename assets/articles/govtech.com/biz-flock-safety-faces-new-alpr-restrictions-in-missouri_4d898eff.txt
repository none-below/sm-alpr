GovTech Biz Flock Safety Faces New ALPR Restrictions in Missouri An executive order from Gov. Mike Kehoe puts data, compliance and other guardrails around license plate reading technology. It comes amid a national backlash against Flock and other surveillance technologies. September 18, 2026 • News Staff Facebook LinkedIn Twitter Print Email Flock Safety Flock Safety faces new data, compliance and facial recognition restrictions in Missouri, among the latest developments in the national backlash against the supplier of license plate reading technology. Gov. Mike Kehoe, a Republican, issued an executive order this week that applies to state and local agencies that receive state funding and use such tech . For starters, such users must permanently delete license plate and vehicle images within 30 days unless part of an “active and ongoing criminal investigation,” or ongoing emergencies and missing person alerts. Agencies covered under the order must receive from vendors of the technology “written certification” that data be deleted in 30 days when applicable. Courts may also order data retention past 30 days. The order also mandates that data captured by automated license plate reader systems is owned by the agency that uses the technology — not, for instance, vendors and contractors. The order prohibits the sale or access to the data for “any non-law enforcement purpose.” Kehoe’s order also prohibits use of data for anything other than “the administration of criminal justice and bona fide lifesaving efforts” — a reflection of the allegations of stalking and other misuses of the license plate reading technology. Agencies cannot integrate automatic AI-powered facial recognition into these tools, either, according to the order. Flock has come under fire for its auditing practices — and has revised those rules in recent months — but Kehoe told agencies covered by the order to “ensure every query records the user's identity and a valid active case number or specific law enforcement justification.” Penalties for misuse could include criminal charges.
