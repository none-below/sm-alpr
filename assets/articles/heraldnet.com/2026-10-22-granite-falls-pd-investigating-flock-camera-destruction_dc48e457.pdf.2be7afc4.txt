Granite Falls PD investigating Flock camera destruction
Published 11:32 am Thursday, October 22, 2026
By Jenna Peterson Herald Writer
EVERETT — A Flock Safety camera was destroyed in Granite Falls on Tuesday evening, the
Snohomish County Sheriff’s Office confirmed in an email to The Daily Herald on Wednesday.
The camera was destroyed at 8:20 p.m. Tuesday, sheriff’s office spokesperson Courtney O’Keefe said.
The camera was located at the intersection of Quarry Road and Mountain Loop Highway.
The Granite Falls Police Department is currently investigating. No suspects were in custody Tuesday
morning, and it remains an active investigation.
Parts and pieces of the camera are being recovered for evidence, O’Keefe said.
Granite Falls has seven Flock cameras, according to its online transparency portal
(https://transparency.flocksafety.com/granite-falls-wa-pd). Flock cameras are a type of automated license plate
reader that uses artificial intelligence to analyze vehicle footage, allowing police officers to search for a

vehicle by license plate or vehicle characteristics — such as a car’s make, model or color. Many law
enforcement agencies have implemented the technology as an investigative tool to assist with solving
crimes, including vehicle theft.
The city of Granite Falls did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
Agencies across the country have seen their cameras vandalized (https://www.npr.org/2026/08/21/nx-s1-
5939851/flock-cameras-police-block-surveillance-vandalize) in recent months as concern over the technology
grows (https://www.nytimes.com/article/flock-safety-camera-technology.html).
Since December 2025, Lynnwood (https://www.heraldnet.com/2026/02/23/lynnwood-becomes-one-of-the-1st-in-the-
state-to-terminate-flock-contract/), Mountlake Terrace (https://www.heraldnet.com/2025/12/05/mountlake-terrace-cancels-
flock-safety-contract/) and Edmonds (https://www.heraldnet.com/2026/05/27/edmonds-becomes-3rd-city-in-the-county-to-
cancel-flock-safety-contract/) have canceled their contracts with Flock Safety following reports of federal
immigration enforcement accessing local networks (https://www.heraldnet.com/2025/11/01/records-feds-accessed-
flock-camera-info-thousands-of-times-in-snohomish-county/). A recent Washington Post investigation
(https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/08/02/how-police-officers-used-vast-network-cameras-spy-their-exes/)
found that at least 50 law enforcement officers have been accused of misusing the Flock system, with
many cases involving surveilling a romantic partner.
