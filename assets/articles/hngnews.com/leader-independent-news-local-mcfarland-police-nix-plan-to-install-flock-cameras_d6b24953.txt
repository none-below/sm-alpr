CHRIS LEE chris.lee@apg-sw.com MCFARLAND — Following moves by Monona Police as well as the Dane County Board within a month’s time, the McFarland Police Department has announced it will not move forward with a planned purchase of the Flock Safety automated license plate reader system (ALPR). “Once Dane County lost their funding for Flock, we lost about half of the potential camera network within Dane County,” said Police Chief Brian Redman, according to a news release from the village. “I had to ask ‘Were we still going to get the return on our investment with that loss?’ With the network being chipped away, that wasn’t going to be the case.” The McFarland Village Board on Dec. 9 had approved use of the ALPR technology by the police department, which planned to install Flock cameras in the village this year for the first time, at an annual cost of $18,000 for six cameras. The department is exploring alternative uses for the unspent funds, according to the news release. In the past few years, many south-central Wisconsin counties, cities, and villages installed ALPR systems, which use cameras and software to automatically capture, analyze, and store license plate numbers, and often, additional identifying information from the backs of vehicles. That information is processed, and when used as part of a network of data, can alert law enforcement to stolen or wanted vehicles, help them solve other crimes, or locate missing persons. Atlanta-based Flock Safety calls itself the “largest public-private safety network” and says it is used by more than 5,000 law enforcement agencies in 49 states. Entities using Flock cameras can choose to share data with other agencies to aid searches that span multiple jurisdictions. In addition to the Dane, Columbia, and Jefferson County Sheriff’s offices, the Cottage Grove, Marshall, Monona, Sun Prairie, and Waunakee police departments were all using Flock cameras as of the start of the year, as were Jefferson, Lodi, Poynette, Watertown, and Whitewater. Lake Mills and McFarland police were to get them this year. But critics have pushed back against what they see as Big-Brother overtones and “surveillance creep” with the camera systems. There have also been concerns nationally about whether U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) and other federal agencies can access Flock data from local communities. Flock Safety said in a blog post that “ICE does not have direct access to Flock cameras, systems, or data, unless the agencies that control their data expressly and deliberately allow it.” But while each local government agency or business that has a contract with Flock can decide which other agencies it will share its data with, it is possible one of those other agencies could in turn still share that data with a federal agent. The Dane County Board voted 32-1 on April 16 to amend the County budget to remove $80,000 for the Sheriff’s Office’s annual Flock subscription, which runs through May 31. “At a time when Flock, some of its law enforcement partners, and federal immigration enforcement agencies have been found to collect and misuse personal information for tracking and surveillance in ways that violate constitutional rights, the board has a responsibility to act,” Dane County Board Chair Patrick Miles said after its vote. Then, on May 4, Monona Police Chief Brian Chaney told the city council that he had suspended the department’s use of Flock. Chaney said he made the decision to end the Flock subscription in consultation with the mayor and others. “Those changes mean less data would have been available to law enforcement in McFarland,” according to the news release. McFarland’s Chief Redman also cited a growing number of residents sharing concerns about the technology in recent months regarding privacy and Flock Safety’s data sharing practices, the news release said. The police department has not made a purchase from Flock Safety and has notified the company of its intention not to pursue a contract, it said. Verona, Dane County, Oshkosh, and Monona have now ended or voted to let their contracts with Flock expire. Some have indicated the possibility of using a different company’s license-plate reader system. The McFarland news release did not say whether its police department would consider using cameras from a different ALPR provider.
