Monona Police suspend use of Flock cameras

Chief tells Council he did not renew contract for ALPR system

  • CHRIS LEE chris.lee@apg-sw.com

    Chris Lee

  • May 5, 2026 Updated May 5, 2026

MONONA – Following a similar move by the Dane County Board last month, the Monona Police Department has decided not to renew the contract for its Flock Safety automated license plate reader system (ALPR).

“I have opted to suspend our use of Flock,” Police Chief Brian Chaney told the Monona City Council at its May 4 meeting. He said access to the system was suspended by staff a couple weeks ago.


Chaney said Monona’s two-year contract with Flock was coming due for renewal on April 20, just four days after the Dane County Board voted 32-1 to amend the County budget to remove $80,000 for the Sheriff's Office's annual Flock subscription, which runs through May 31.

Chaney said he had to decide quickly, “do I lock us in to a year or two contract?”

Besides the County vote as a factor, Chaney said his department has also been receiving numerous open records requests regarding the Flock system – with the majority not being Monona from residents – and staff was spending an exorbitant amount of time fulfilling those requests.

There have also been concerns nationally about whether U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) and other federal agencies can access Flock data from local communities.

Flock Safety said in a blog post that “ICE does not have direct access to Flock cameras, systems, or data, unless the agencies that control their data expressly and deliberately allow it.”

But while each local government agency or business that has a contract with Flock can decide which other agencies it will share its data with, it is possible one of those other agencies could in turn still share that data with a federal agent.

Chaney said he made the decision to end Monona’s Flock subscription in consultation with the mayor and others.

In the past few years, many south-central Wisconsin counties, cities, and villages have installed ALPR systems, which use cameras and software to automatically capture, analyze, and store license plate numbers, and often, additional identifying information from the backs of vehicles.

That information is processed, and when used as part of a network of data, can alert law enforcement to stolen or wanted vehicles, help them solve other crimes, or locate missing persons.

Atlanta-based Flock Safety calls itself the “largest public-private safety network” and says it is used by more than 5,000 law enforcement agencies in 49 states.

In addition to the Dane, Columbia, and Jefferson County Sheriff’s offices, the Cottage Grove, Marshall, Monona, Sun Prairie, and Waunakee police departments were all using Flock cameras as of the start of the year, as were Jefferson, Lodi, Poynette, Watertown, and Whitewater. Lake Mills and McFarland police are getting them this year.

Critics have pushed back against what they see as Big-Brother overtones and “surveillance creep” with the camera systems.

“At a time when Flock, some of its law enforcement partners, and federal immigration enforcement agencies have been found to collect and misuse personal information for tracking and surveillance in ways that violate constitutional rights, the board has a responsibility to act,” Dane County Board Chair Patrick Miles said in a news release after its vote.

Monona Police began using Flock ALPR cameras in 2023 and had three in use in the City at the end of 2025, with a fourth one approved.

Besides Dane County and now Monona, the Oshkosh Police Department has also ended its contract with Flock. All three indicated the possibility of using a different company’s license-plate reader system. Verona ended its contract with Flock last December.

Chaney said there was a “strong sense of disappointment” expressed at a recent Public Safety Committee meeting over not having Flock anymore.

“Many residents find value in the technology,” he said.

Chaney said that unless and until a replacement is found, cases that would have been solved sooner due to the help of an ALPR system are going to take more effort.

“We are going to have to pivot,” he said.

Chaney said that Monona’s contract with Flock could still be renewed at some point in the future.

(Updated at 8:18 p.m. on 5/5/2026 to remove a quote after getting a correction from Chief Chaney.)
