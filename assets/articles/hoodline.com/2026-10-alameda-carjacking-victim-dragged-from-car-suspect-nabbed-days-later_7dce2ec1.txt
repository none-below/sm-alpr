Subscribe More forecasts: Atlanta 30 day forecast Bay Area / Oakland / Crime & Emergencies Alameda Carjacking Victim Dragged From Car, Suspect Nabbed Days Later By Eric Tanaka Published on October 19, 2026 Source: Battlesnake1 / Wikimedia Commons A driver who tracked down their own stolen vehicle in Alameda ended up getting forcibly yanked out of it by the very person who took it, police say, in an incident that unfolded on the 100 block of Brush Street last Wednesday afternoon. Officers were called to the scene at about 1:20 p.m. after the owner located the car, only to have the suspect return, pull them out and speed off before anyone could stop it. Victim Tracks Own Car, Then Gets Pulled From It According to the Alameda Police Department , the vehicle had been stolen from another city before the owner found it parked on Brush Street. Officers were advised that the suspect came back to the car, forcibly removed the victim, and fled the scene, according to Patch . By the time patrol units arrived, the vehicle was gone and the suspect was nowhere in sight. Officers didn't stay in the dark for long. Using Automated License Plate Readers, they located the vehicle, though it had already been abandoned and was unoccupied when they found it, the outlet reports. That same evening, investigators identified the suspect and obtained an arrest warrant connected to the Brush Street incident. Suspect Also Wanted in Summer Robbery Case The warrant wasn't the only one with the suspect's name on it. The individual was also wanted in connection with a robbery that occurred in Alameda earlier in the summer, per the same account, and police say that case involved a firearm. Two days after the carjacking, on Friday, officers found the suspect and took them into custody on both warrants. Police described the arrest as a win for a surveillance tool that has become increasingly controversial nationwide. The department said the technology helped it quickly locate and return the victim's vehicle; officers took an individual with connections to firearms off the streets, Patch reports. Alameda police did not release the suspect's name, age, or the specific charges filed. License Plate Readers Draw Scrutiny Even as Police Tout Them Automated license plate readers have been around for decades, but the technology has recently drawn more attention amid privacy concerns tied to companies like Flock and allegations that data has been shared with federal immigration authorities, Patch reports. Other departments, including Berkeley's, have also credited the cameras with cracking cases that might otherwise have gone unsolved, according to the outlet. Alameda has leaned further into the technology in recent years. A city project described in a request for proposals aimed to add a fixed camera system to the department's existing mobile readers, giving officers and investigators what the city called critical information to generate leads in criminal investigations, according to the City of Alameda . The Brush Street case isn't the only recent instance where tracking technology helped Alameda officers recover a stolen vehicle. In August, officers responding to a stolen-vehicle report at Lincoln Avenue and Grand Street just after 6 a.m. found more than just the car, according to the Alameda Post . Location devices the owner had discreetly placed, combined with the department's license plate readers, let officers monitor the vehicle's location in real time, and when they caught up to it, they discovered thousands of dollars' worth of stolen professional-grade tools along with burglary tools, theft devices and narcotics inside. Brush Street Incident Ends in Confrontation The Brush Street incident began after the owner located the stolen vehicle; the suspect returned, forcibly removed the victim and fled. Statewide data suggests vehicle theft overall has been trending downward. Counties where California Highway Patrol crime suppression teams were deployed saw significant declines in vehicle thefts last year, including Alameda, which recorded 12,277 vehicle thefts, down 36.1% from 2024, according to the Office of Governor Gavin Newsom . Still, the violent nature of the Brush Street confrontation underscores why police continue to warn residents against trying to handle recovered stolen vehicles themselves.
