ITHACA, N.Y. — Several local law enforcement agencies including the Ithaca Police Department (IPD) and the Tompkins County Sheriff’s Office (TCSO) were collectively awarded approximately $450,000 from New York State on Monday to improve and expand technology to modernize operations. The State Division of Criminal Justice Services (DCJS), the agency that selected awardees and will administer the funds, awarded $240,610 to IPD, according to a statement from Gov. Kathy Hochul’s office sent Monday. Chief Tom Kelly said the department plans to fund updates to its 3D crime scanners— mounted devices that project a laser beam onto a 360 view of a crime scene, recording and painting a picture of the scene in minutes, as described in a recent study by the National Institute of Justice. Kelly said the scanners give his officers a “true representation of a crime scene.” The department will also purchase new radios and update technology inside their vehicles. TCSO was awarded $99,618, which Sheriff Derek Osborne said will be used to cover the expense of a handful of in-car systems and an internal camera system for the van the office uses to transport inmates. DCJS awarded funding to other local agencies, granting $56,000 to the Village of Dryden Police Department, 67,900 to the Cayuga Heights Police Department and $90,249 to the Trumansburg Village Police Department. For a small department with a small budget, Chief Joe Nelson said he was “beyond ecstatic” to hear the news earlier this week. The department has been without technologies most departments would consider essential since Nelson became chief about five years ago, such as a license plate reader and functioning portable radios. Over 300 law enforcement agencies, all of which are located outside of New York City, submitted applications to DCJS in September 2023 to receive funding. DCJS will direct a total of $127 million to these agencies in the coming months, according to the statement from the governor’s office. Departments around the state will use over half of the total funding to fund the aforementioned license plate readers, body-worn and patrol vehicle equipment and public safety camera systems. According to the statement, other awardees around the state will purchase modern equipment and technology including mobile and fixed camera systems, computer-aided dispatch systems, software, unmanned aerial vehicles, gunshot detection devices and smart equipment for patrol vehicles and officers.
