ITHACA, N.Y. — Activists covered at least five AI-powered cameras Saturday during a protest aimed at pushing local officials to hasten the removal of the controversial technology from city streets. About three months ago, the City of Ithaca and Tompkins County separately voted to end their contracts with Flock Safety after facing sustained public pressure. While advocates successfully pushed local officials to cut ties with Flock, the company’s cameras are still up. Concerned residents are demanding more transparency about when they will be removed from city streets, but local officials say the cameras are off and will come down soon. Flock’s cameras capture images of vehicles on the road, then process them using AI to allow law enforcement to track and estimate the path they traveled using simple descriptions, such as a car’s make or color. The anti-surveillance group Flock Off Ithaca organized a protest of about 24 people on Saturday near the intersection of North Albany and West Seneca streets during the 49th annual Ithaca Festival. “You said clearly that you refuse to be surveilled on your own streets,” Anne Johnson, a member of Flock Off Ithaca, said to the crowd. “But the cameras are still up. They are still capturing and storing our data because local leaders and Flock will drag their feet on removing the cameras as long as they can.” Photo 2 caption: Flock Off Ithaca member Anne Johnson called on city officials to remove Flock Safety cameras from Ithaca. Credit: J.T. Stone / The Ithaca Voice In an email to The Ithaca Voice , Tompkins County Sheriff Derek Osborne said that the county-controlled Flock devices were shut off when the county terminated its contract on May 28 and are expected to be removed this week. Osborne said that this timeframe was established when the legislature voted not to renew the contract. Osborne provided an email from a Flock representative to The Ithaca Voice to confirm the company has deactivated its cameras. City of Ithaca Alderpersons Kayla Matos and Hannah Shvets attended the Saturday rally. Matos said in an interview that the city’s Flock devices were disconnected after Memorial Day when the city’s contract ended. Shvets said in an interview that the city is currently waiting for Flock Safety to remove the cameras and that the city will have the authority to do so if a certain period of time passes. “We’ll continue to share information as we get it,” Shvets said. “We’re still figuring out things and we want to be as transparent as possible, so as soon as we know what’s going on, we’ll share that.” Matos added, “We appreciate the community coming together, showing up to public comment, messaging Common Council and continuing to apply pressure to show that this is a concern that our community is taking seriously.” Ithaca Police Chief Thomas Kelly said in a statement he did not have a removal date scheduled with Flock yet. Ithaca Alderperson Kayla Matos, left, and activist Jen Colt attended a May 30 anti-surveillance demonstration in downtown Ithaca. Credit: J.T. Stone / The Ithaca Voice In March, the Ithaca Common Council passed a resolution that directed the city attorney and city manager to terminate the city’s contract with Flock Safety at the “earliest possible opportunity.” The resolution also states that all Flock Safety equipment, such as license plate reader cameras and gunshot detection devices, must be turned off and disconnected within 14 days of the contract being terminated. It adds that the company must remove all related technology “as soon as practicable” after the contract ends. Aaron Fernando, another member of Flock Off Ithaca, said that the group submitted letters to the city and county weeks ago asking when the cameras would be deactivated and removed, but it did not receive responses. This spurred activists during the rally to cover five Flock cameras around downtown Ithaca with plastic bags while protesters chanted phrases such as “Block Flock!” “The city has not fulfilled its obligation to take down the cameras within the timeframe that was given,” Fernando said. Anti-surveillance activists called on city officials to remove Flock Safety cameras during a May 30 demonstration in downtown Ithaca. Credit: J.T. Stone / The Ithaca Voice Protesters highlighted instances across the country where Flock cameras have remained in communities for months after a municipality decided to end its contract with the company. Activists also pointed out that at least one city — Evanston, Illinois — has filed a cease-and-desist order against the company for re-installing surveillance equipment despite opposition from elected leaders. Advocates added that the Federal Bureau of Investigation, under the Trump administration, is seeking to buy access to a nationwide automated license plate recognition database, for which Flock Safety is a top competitor. Another rally attendee, Tracy Robertson, noted that U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement has accessed data collected by Flock to assist in the Trump administration’s immigration crackdown. He likened this development to the actions of fascist governments throughout history. “It’s like we’re moving toward a private, militarized police force being used by a kind of rogue regime and fascist leader reminiscent of Nazi Germany or reminiscent of things happening in Russia,” Robertson said in an interview.
