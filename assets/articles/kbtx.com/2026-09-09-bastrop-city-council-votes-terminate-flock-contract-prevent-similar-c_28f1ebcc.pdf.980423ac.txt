BASTROP, Texas (KBTX) - Bastrop City Council has voted to immediately terminate its contract
with Flock, as well as prevent any future license plate reading technology from coming to the
city.
“The surprise was that council member Cynthia Meyer went ahead and put forward a resolution
to not only stop the Flock contract, but to stop all future automatic license plate reader contracts
as well. So I think it’s a big win for privacy here in Bastrop,” said John Bush, a Bastrop resident.
Last week, Bastrop covered the cameras that were in the city, and after more citizen push, they
cemented their removal.
“I’d like to see things go a step further with the city charter amendment because that would be
permanent and go beyond just this council. But at the end of the day, we’re really pleased with
how this council has shown up. And I think a lot of people feel as though their voices have been
heard,” said Bush.
Flock has been a heated topic of discussion as residents, along with city and state officials
across the nation, are debating if Flock’s license plate reading technology is being used properly.
“And we’ve had evidence that it has helped us. But then there’s also, you know, where there’s
been a misuse of technology and the concerns of the privacy, the encroachment, the data
collection and access to our personal information and how much control we really have over
that,” said Ishmael Harris, the Mayor of Bastrop.
Bastrop is the 5th city or county in the past month to terminate their contracts, according to
DeFlock. This also comes as Gov. Greg Abbott announced a pause in funding the cameras and
Sen. Lois Kolkhorst called for an outright ban of Flock cameras.
“Local municipalities actually have more power to guide how this technology is deployed in city
limits than the federal government right now,” said Stacy Collins, another Bastrop resident.
Bush says that he hopes Bastrop is just the beginning of similar votes.
“So yeah, this is a big precedent, and we can only hope that other cities follow suit,” said Bush.
