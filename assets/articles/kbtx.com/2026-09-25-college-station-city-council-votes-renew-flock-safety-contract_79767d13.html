College Station City Council votes to renew Flock Safety contract

By Lillian Kosh
Published: Sep. 25, 2026 at 3:49 AM UTC|Updated: 10 hours ago
Add Us On GoogleAdd as a preferred source on Google

COLLEGE STATION, Texas (KBTX) - College Station City Council voted 4-2 Thursday night to renew the city’s contract with Flock Safety, keeping the city’s network of 84 license plate reading cameras in operation.

The vote came after nearly three hours of public comment, with more than 25 residents signing up to speak. The overwhelming majority of speakers urged the city to end its contract with Flock, citing concerns over privacy, data security and the potential for misuse.

With Mayor John Nichols absent Thursday night while recovering from a health issue, only six council members were eligible to vote, meaning anything less than four votes in favor, including a 3-3 tie, would have ended the contract outright.

In the end, council members David White, Scott Schafer, Melissa McIlhaney, and Mayor Pro Tem William Wright voted to renew the contract. Bob Yancy and Mark Smith voted against it.

David White credited local advocacy groups for helping shape his understanding of the technology. White ultimately voted yes, but said he’d push for guardrails, including possibly cutting back on the city’s camera count and adding an independent audit.

Council member Scott Schafer voted yes, but admitted the rollout moved too fast. He called for stricter guardrails in the future, saying he wants the technology used “in a way that’s more agreeable” to residents concerned about privacy.

Council member Melissa McIlhaney also voted yes, saying that even if the technology only helped in one case, it would be worth keeping for now.

Council member Mark Smith was one of two who voted no.

“The flock basically adds to the public sense that their privacy is being invaded,” he said, arguing that unlike other data collection residents already live with, “this is an easier thing to stop.”

Council member Bob Yancy was the second to vote no, arguing the company itself was untrustworthy and pushing instead for the city to switch providers to Axon or a similar technology.

“The problem is the vendor’s open architecture, their initial lack of concern for privacy in its initial design, and the company itself. Flock collects too much,” Yancy said. “It shares with too many. It uses inflexible contract language and marketing doublespeak.”

Finally, Mayor Pro Tem William Wright capped off the votes to renew the contract. He justified his decision by saying he answers to the entire city, not just those in the room.

“Ultimately, elected up here at large to represent 135,000 people,” Wright said. “And while y’all are a slice of it, you do not represent 135,000 people.”

After the vote, the council chambers quickly emptied. Many of the residents who had spent hours pushing for the city to end its Flock contract left disappointed, but not ready to give up the fight.

“Obviously, it’s a pretty disappointing outcome,” said Cody Fox, a member of DeFlock BCS. “I know John, Ivan and I have been spending the last nine months of our lives coming, showing up every two weeks, voicing our opinion, and sitting down with some of the council members. And it’s a pretty disappointing outcome, but we’ll bounce back and keep pushing and try to keep fighting for the community.”

College Station signed its original contract with Flock Safety in September 2025, with the first cameras installed the following month. The renewed contract is valued at nearly $400,000 a year.

Cities across Texas have moved in the opposite direction in recent months, with several municipalities, including Bastrop, Robinson and Manvel, canceling or declining to renew their own Flock contracts amid growing concerns over privacy and data sharing.

With the contract renewed, College Station’s Flock camera network will continue operating for at least another year, as city leaders say they intend to work with police on additional safeguards going forward.
