Brenham police confirm internal investigation into officer following alleged Flock camera misuse

By Lillian Kosh
Published: Oct. 6, 2026 at 11:30 PM UTC|Updated: 22 hours ago
Add Us On GoogleAdd as a preferred source on Google

BRENHAM, Texas (KBTX) - The City of Brenham confirms it’s conducting an internal affairs investigation into one of its police officers. The confirmation comes after a social media post claimed an officer was allegedly terminated following allegations they misused the department’s automated license plate reader (ALPR) system.

A social media post from a police watchdog group on Monday alleges an officer was investigated for misusing the department’s Flock Safety automated license plate reader (ALPR) system, and that the investigation resulted in disciplinary action, including termination.

KBTX reached out to the Brenham Police Department, which said it was not authorized to speak on the matter, but said the city’s public information officer would release a statement.

Brenham’s communications and public relations manager, Melinda Gordon, sent KBTX the following statement, confirming an internal affairs investigation is underway but declining to confirm the officer’s identity, employment status, or the nature of the allegations:

This week, the Brenham Police Department (BPD) received an inquiry concerning a BPD officer, claiming that the officer had been terminated for improper conduct related to his job. The BPD can confirm that there is an internal affairs investigation being conducted involving a BPD officer. All internal investigations are conducted through a fair, impartial, and comprehensive review process in accordance with departmental policy. The current investigation is ongoing, and no determination has been made concerning the officer’s status. The BPD will provide an update after the conclusion of the investigation.

City of Brenham

At this time, there has been no confirmation of Flock misuse at the Brenham Police Department. The department said it is not conducting interviews on the matter.

KBTX reached out to Brenham councilmember Steve Soman, who has publicly questioned Flock Safety in the past. Soman told KBTX that while the city has access to other ALPR systems and technologies, the city doesn’t have a contract with Flock. Soman said the Flock cameras in town are owned by Washington County, TxDOT and private businesses.

The City of Brenham and Brenham Police Department have confirmed that an internal affairs investigation involving an officer is underway. Internal investigations are conducted through a fair, impartial, and comprehensive review under departmental policy. The investigation is ongoing, and no determination has been made concerning the officer’s status. I will not comment on facts or allegations that are still under review. Policy exists so conduct is measured against clear rules. If a violation is found, the department’s disciplinary process applies. An update should come from the department and the city after the investigation concludes.

Steve Soman, Brenham City Council

An internal affairs investigation and any resulting department discipline are separate from a criminal case, and as of now, there is no indication charges have been filed or that prosecutors have made any charging decision.

KBTX is working to obtain more information and will update this story as more information is confirmed.
