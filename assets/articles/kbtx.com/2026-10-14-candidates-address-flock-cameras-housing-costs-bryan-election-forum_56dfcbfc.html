Candidates address Flock cameras, housing costs at Bryan election forum

By Cris Cordova and Alex Weister
Published: Oct. 14, 2026 at 4:06 AM UTC|Updated: 10 hours ago
Add Us On GoogleAdd as a preferred source on Google

BRYAN, Texas (KBTX) - Candidates from city, county, and state races spoke at a forum Tuesday at the Brazos Center ahead of the midterm elections.

Residents heard from state representative candidates for Districts 12 and 14, a Brazos County Precinct 4 candidate, and both Bryan and College Station mayoral candidates.

Bryan Mayor Candidates address Flock cameras

Bryan Mayor Bobby Gutierrez said police officers have voiced support for the city’s use of Flock cameras.

“We know our police officers love it; we know our officers talk about how it is. The last report I got, Flock is having to redo everything they are doing, and they have A.I. going in and checking everything,” Gutierrez said.

Bryan mayoral candidate Rafael D. Peña III said he would not have voted to continue funding the cameras in their current form.

“In its current form, I do not believe that Flock is the right way to go. If it came before this council, I would have voted not to renew the funding, because I think it has lost the confidence of the people,” Peña said.

College Station Mayor Candidates address housing costs

College Station mayoral candidate Elizabeth Cunha pointed to density bonus programs as a tool to encourage entry-level housing.

“So, we do have some solid programs in place to help. I’m for density bonuses in apartment complexes to encourage some people to have some entry-level housing. I want affordable housing, but I can’t promise you a fix,” Cunha said.

College Station City Council Place 5 candidate Bob Yancy, who is also running for Mayor, said development fees contribute to rising housing costs for young families.

“All of those fees roll downhill; they go from the developer to the builder, and then the builder puts them in the price, and the young family inherits the high cost of living,” Yancy said.

Second forum planned for Oct. 19

Another candidate forum is planned for Monday, Oct. 19, from 6 to 8:30 p.m. at the Brazos Center. Organizers say it will feature five Bryan City Council races.
