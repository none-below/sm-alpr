COLLEGE STATION, Texas (KBTX) - State Rep. Gina Hinojosa, the Democratic challenger for
Texas governor, spoke with KBTX to discuss her priorities for a first term and issues affecting the
Brazos Valley ahead of the Nov. 3 election.
Hinojosa is challenging incumbent Gov. Greg Abbott for a fourth term. Following his recent
campaign stop in Brazos County, Hinojosa spoke one-on-one with KBTX’s Sophia Ridley about
affordability, public schools, public safety, and local issues including Flock cameras and the
SpaceX Terafab project in nearby Grimes County.
When asked what she would bring to Brazos Valley voters, Hinojosa laid out what she called a
back-to-basics agenda to “secure the American Dream” centered on saving public schools,
putting money in people’s pockets with $1,500 to every Texas household, freezing electricity
rates, and ensuring health care for children.

When asked about the Terafab chip manufacturing project in Grimes County, Hinojosa tied the
project to her criticism of Abbott’s relationship with Elon Musk, whose company SpaceX is
developing Terafab.
“So there are real concerns about the governor doing the bidding of Elon Musk and billionaires
and not the people of Texas leaving the people to fend for themselves,” Hinojosa said. “The data
centers are a prime example. Terafab we need to make sure people have a say here because
this is first and foremost our Texas and our rights and our desires need to be heard when we are
doing any kind of economic development. And that is my commitment to the people.”

questions
Hinojosa said she filed a lawsuit against Abbott earlier this week in Travis County District Court to
release emails between him and Musk. On data centers more broadly, she called for a
moratorium on any new permits until protections for water, power and local input are in place.
School funding and vouchers
On school vouchers, which Abbott championed, Hinojosa said schools are in crisis with teachers
leaving and more than 150 districts moving to four-day weeks. She said she would fire TEA
Commissioner Mike Morath on day one, pause the A-through-F system tied to STAAR, and
reprioritize funding to teacher pay, small class sizes and special education.

On Abbott’s promise to zero out school district property taxes for homestead owners, Hinojosa
said she would instead end corporate giveaways to buy down property taxes.
Crime and Repeat Offender program
On public safety, Hinojosa was asked about Abbott’s Texas Repeat Offender Program, a joint task
force targeting Houston that Abbott said he is willing to expand to include the Brazos Valley.
Hinojosa said public safety has to be a priority and should not be partisan, adding she would
evaluate models from around the state and focus on data-driven policy and coordination with
local, state and federal law enforcement.

Hinojosa sharply criticized Abbott’s handling of state grant funding for Flock license plate reader
cameras, saying the cameras were never authorized by the Legislature.
“What would you plan for flock cameras as governor? I think that they were put up by Greg Abbott
in violation of law, frankly. He didn’t have the authority to create a surveillance state in Texas,”
Hinojosa said. “So I think that was a waste of money. I think the people need to be refunded for
that. And I think they need to come down.”
She added there might be some places where the cameras make sense, but said they have
proliferated without public support:
“There might be some places where they make sense, but the way they’ve just proliferated
across the state, most people don’t want. In fact, people are taking it upon themselves.
Everywhere we go, we see them cut down,” Hinojosa said. “It’s a crazy thing. It takes a lot of
effort to cut those down. So people are angry about it. I mean, we really believe in liberties and
privacy in this state. And that is violating that.”
