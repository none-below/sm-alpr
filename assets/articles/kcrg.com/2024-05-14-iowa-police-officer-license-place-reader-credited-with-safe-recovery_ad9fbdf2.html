Iowa police officer, license place reader credited with safe recovery of endangered person

By KCRG Staff and KCCI
Published: May 14, 2024 at 3:40 PM UTC
Add Us On GoogleAdd as a preferred source on Google

INDIANOLA, Iowa (KCRG) - The Indianola Police Department is crediting the fast actions of an officer and a newly installed license plate reader camera for recovering a missing and endangered person from Missouri over the weekend.

In a news release, the police department said Officer Drew Stever was on his routine patrol when the LPR camera sent out an alert that the endangered person was in the area.

The officer immediately moved to catch up with the vehicle and initiated a traffic stop at Elk Horn and Highway 65/69, just north of city limits in rural Warren County, Iowa.

The driver of the vehicle was identified as the missing person, who has dementia.

Officer Stever took the person to the police department, where they were able to be safely reunited with family.

KCCI in Des Moines spoke with Officer Stever, who said without the camera, the vehicle would have passed right by him.

The endangered may drove nearly 200 miles undetected, until being pinged by the newly installed camera.

“So I ran the vehicle through my NCIC system and the in-car system and it showed positive that it was a missing person out of Missouri,” Stever said.

The Flock cameras were just installed within the last two weeks to scan license plates and send alerts to officers if a plate matches one on a national criminal database.

“I want to commend Officer Stever for his quick actions,” Police Chief Brian Sher said in the release. “I believe he saved a life today and I know the family is very grateful. I have complete faith and trust in my officers’ ability to think and act quickly and professionally, even when that means leaving city limits or going above and beyond their typical duties because of extenuating circumstances.”
