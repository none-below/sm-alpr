CEDAR RAPIDS, Iowa (KCRG) - The Cedar Rapids Police Department shared its policies for
automated license plate reader (ALPR) data following recent reports that raised concerns over

their potential to cause harm.
The department says its policies are strict and limit who is able to access data.
The data is only available for 30 days, and a transparency portal keeps track of how many license
plate images are captured, plates associated with persons of interest and how many searches
are made.
In addition, the department publicly discloses the locations of its 76 readers and releases
quarterly reports on the city’s website.
CRPD uses readers made by Flock Safety and Vigilant Solutions. It says outside agencies must
be approved by the department before they can access the city’s data.
The data is also not used for immigration enforcement, according to the department’s policies.
The Department says the cameras have helped officers in a few recent cases.
Officers arrested a man this month for attempted murder connected to a drive-by shooting in
January by searching traffic data from the readers.
CRPD say they also used ALPRs to find an 84-year-old man with dementia who had travelled
from Cedar Rapids to Chicago and reunite him with his family.
The American Civil Liberties Union with the University of Iowa Technology Law Clinic published a
report this week pointing out cases where the technology has been misused and recommending
more regulations and uniform transparency.
CRPD’s policy states misuse or abuse of ALPR data will result in disciplinary action and may lead
to termination.
