North Liberty considers removing Flock cameras

Council tables decision after hours of public comment
By Elizabeth Schultz
Published: Oct. 14, 2026 at 3:25 AM UTC|Updated: 10 hours ago
Add Us On GoogleAdd as a preferred source on Google

NORTH LIBERTY, Iowa (KCRG) - North Liberty is deciding whether to remove its Flock cameras. The cameras were installed without the City Council approving its contract with Flock, the company that supplies them.

How the cameras were installed

The process began in 2024, when the City Council unanimously approved the annual city budget, which included installing 12 license plate reading cameras for $40,000.

Since then, North Liberty police say Flock’s technology has helped find missing people and stolen vehicles.

All public comments against cameras

At Tuesday’s meeting, dozens of people spoke during public comment. All of them spoke against keeping the cameras.

A Flock representative was also at the meeting to answer council members’ questions about how data is stored and shared.

A former city council member who voted in favor of the cameras in 2024 also spoke against keeping them.

Police Chief Diane Venenga said the department has made changes to its technology. She said the department will now store information for seven days instead of 30.

“The information is hard sanitized after seven days and we are committed to using the system for legitimate law enforcement purposes,” Venenga said. “I think we’ve shown we are able to adapt and move along with this technology.”

Many people at the meeting were not convinced and raised concerns about Flock’s technology.

“This is not a criticism of our police officers,” one speaker said during public comment. “This is about the technology, the information it collects, and the fact we are handing that information over to a private third-party company we don’t have any input on.”

Council tables decision

Council members debated whether to remove the cameras or keep them with stronger safeguards. They did not reach a final decision.

Instead, council members said they needed more time to consider changes to the camera system. They voted 4-1 to table a decision until the next City Council meeting in two weeks.
