How the cameras were installed
The process began in 2024, when the City Council unanimously approved the annual city budget,
which included installing 12 license plate reading cameras for $40,000.
Since then, North Liberty police say Flock’s technology has helped find missing people and stolen
vehicles.
All public comments against cameras
At Tuesday’s meeting, dozens of people spoke during public comment. All of them spoke against
keeping the cameras.
A Flock representative was also at the meeting to answer council members’ questions about how
data is stored and shared.
A former city council member who voted in favor of the cameras in 2024 also spoke against
keeping them.
Police Chief Diane Venenga said the department has made changes to its technology. She said
the department will now store information for seven days instead of 30.
“The information is hard sanitized after seven days and we are committed to using the system for
legitimate law enforcement purposes,” Venenga said. “I think we’ve shown we are able to adapt
and move along with this technology.”
Many people at the meeting were not convinced and raised concerns about Flock’s technology.
“This is not a criticism of our police officers,” one speaker said during public comment. “This is
about the technology, the information it collects, and the fact we are handing that information over
to a private third-party company we don’t have any input on.”
