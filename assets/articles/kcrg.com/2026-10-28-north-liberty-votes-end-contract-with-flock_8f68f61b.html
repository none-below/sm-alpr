North Liberty votes to end contract with Flock

City Council voted 3-1 Tuesday night to turn off data sharing
By Elizabeth Schultz
Published: Oct. 27, 2026 at 8:01 PM CDT|Updated: 15 hours ago
Add Us On GoogleAdd as a preferred source on Google

NORTH LIBERTY, Iowa (KCRG) - The North Liberty City Council voted 3-1 Tuesday night to end its contract with Flock.

The vote follows months of discussion among police, council members and residents. No one spoke in favor of keeping the cameras during public comment at Tuesday’s meeting or previous meetings.

The city operates 12 Flock cameras that scan license plates and store the data in a police database. The contract costs about $36,000 a year.

Residents have repeatedly raised concerns about who can access the data and how the information could be used.

Police tout benefits

In September, North Liberty police explained how the cameras are used and emphasized the department’s transparency portal. Police have said the cameras have helped investigators find missing people and recover stolen vehicles.

At the previous City Council meeting, North Liberty Police Chief Diane Venenga outlined changes to the cameras, including reducing data retention from 30 days to seven days and limiting which outside agencies can access the system.

3-1 vote

Councilor Brian Wayson voted to keep the Flock cameras, saying they were intended to help protect public safety. He also raised concerns about whether ending the city’s access would guarantee that other agencies could no longer use the data.

Councilor Tonja Fortney supported ending the contract, emphasizing the importance of community trust. Councilor Paul Park also supported removing the cameras and the idea to explore other safety options.

Councilor Brent Smith was not present at Tuesday’s meeting.

What’s next

The city plans to turn off data sharing Wednesday, meaning North Liberty will no longer share data with other agencies or access data from their systems, according to Mayor Chris Hoffman.

The physical cameras could remain in place while the city works through the contract requirements with Flock.

TV-9 asked Police Chief Diane Venenga and Councilor Brian Wayson for comment, both refused to speak.
