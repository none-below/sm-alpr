Columbia Flock cameras sent over 5,500 alerts in first year,
report says
Cooper Ten Bruin, KOMU 8 Reporter
Jun 15, 2026
COLUMBIA — The Columbia Police Department released its 2025 Annual Surveillance
Technology Report Sunday detailing successes from the first year of Flock Safety cameras being
used to assist the Police Department in proactive policing and post-incident investigations. 
The city has reached 86% of the planned implementation of Flock Safety cameras, according to
the report. The Police Department received 5,521 total alerts from the Flock Safety system in
2025:
Warrants - 2,877 hits (52.1%)
Stolen license plates - 1,452 hits (26.3%) 
Stolen vehicles - 1,192 hits (21.6%)
Flock Safety cameras are designed to capture objective, vehicle-based evidence and to deliver
actionable intelligence to police. Flock Safety collects vehicle data only, with no facial recognition,
with data being purged every 30 days. Data is kept within the Columbia Police Department and
only shared with other law enforcement agencies within Missouri, according to the Police
Department.
The report said that in 2025, there were no substantiated complaints regarding the use of Flock
Safety cameras.
The Police Department said Flock Safety cameras have helped police investigations by improving
case solvability by strengthening evidence. These cameras can be used to find vehicles near
crime scenes, movement patterns of suspects, clarifying timelines and possibly eliminating

The cameras give real-time alerts for officers.
"When a stolen vehicle, wanted vehicle or vehicle associated with a missing person enters a
monitored area, officers receive immediate notification," the report said. "This allows for timely
patrol response, targeted enforcement and rapid suspect containment."
According to the report, "Flock is a force multiplier, not a standalone solution. It does not prevent
crime by itself, nor does it replace traditional investigations, community policing or officer
judgment."
Flock assisted the Police Department with many cases over the past year. In 2025, when two
children were taken from Columbia motel, the Flock system found the car minutes after an Amber
Alert was released, according to the Police Department. This information was given to Missouri
State Highway Patrol, which located the car 60 miles away from Columbia and arrested the
suspect. 
There were 217 case outcomes from the 5,521 total alerts:
Arrest was made - 69 cases (31.8%)
Lead generation - 52 cases (24%)
Active investigations - 33 cases (15.2%)
Unable to locate - 31 cases (14.3%)
The Police Department found elevated detection patterns during some parts of the year.
According to the report, "Monthly trends indicate elevated enforcement and detection periods
during late summer and fall 2025, with peak daily alert volumes exceeding 60-70 hits. These
increases are consistent with expanded camera deployment, increased plate-read capture rates
and seasonal crime trends."
The first year implementation costs will be the highest Columbia pays for Flock Safety
surveillance. In 2025, the city spent $584,850 on the cameras, including hardware, installation,
software access and initial service. Just over $539,000 of that was funded through police and

Columbia's contract with Flock Safety expires March 2027 when the city will renegotiate terms.
