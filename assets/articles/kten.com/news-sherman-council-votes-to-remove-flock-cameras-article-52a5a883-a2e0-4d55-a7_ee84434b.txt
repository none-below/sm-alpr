Facebook Twitter WhatsApp SMS Email Print Copy article link Save SHERMAN, Texas (KTEN) — Flock surveillance cameras have been used by Sherman police as a tool to help track down criminals. "There are concerns," said City Council member Clay Barnett. The cameras' future in Sherman was a topic of discussion at Monday night's City Council meeting. "I hate that we are taking a tool out of their tool belt,” Barnett added. “But that being said... what happens to the data once it leaves Sherman's hands?" Currently, Sherman has 11 Flock automated license plate reader cameras. However, the technology has recently gained national attention. "We recognize that there are other issues throughout the country with this type of camera system. Most of which is surrounding privacy concerns,” explained Sherman Mayor Shawn Teamann. "Some also would be constitutional issues or potential constitutional issues. We consider all of those things. We saw this become a centerpoint for conversations." At Monday night's meeting, council members reconsidered their contract with Flock Group Inc. "This resolution would provide for the non-renewal of those existing contracts. Remove those from the city, and it would also create a future provision that would require staff to take any new ALPR installations back to council for approval," said acting City Manager Clint Phillips. Ultimately, the council decided to move forward with that plan and to not renew the city’s contract with Flock cameras. "For that reason, I'm making a motion to approve this resolution," Barnett added. It is a decision that Sherman resident Gary Stobel believes is a mistake. "There are bad apples in every group ... don't matter whether it's the police department,” he said. “We need all the help we can get, and I think it's a mistake. I hate to see it go. Just have to go back to the old way, which is slower, and even more mistakes are made."
