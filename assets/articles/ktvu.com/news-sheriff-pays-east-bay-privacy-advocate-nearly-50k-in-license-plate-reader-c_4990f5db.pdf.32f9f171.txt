Sheriff pays East Bay privacy advocate nearly $50
license plate reader case
Published November 16, 2020 8:47 AM PST
SAN PABLO, Calif. - The Contra Costa County Sheriff has settled a case with
East Bay privacy advocate after deputies stopped him and his brother last
Thanksgiving because license plate readers flagged him as a car thief.
Brian Hofer, chair of Oakland’s Privacy Advisory Commission, received $49,50
an August payout from the sheriff's department. 
Hofer, who makes it his life's work to expose technology that he says violates
citizens' privacy, said he was happy with the settlement, despite it being a
relatively low amount.
"Thankfully, we weren't shot or didn't end up in the hospital or jail," he said.
Last Nov. 25, he and his brother, a political science student at UC Berkeley, w
detained at gunpoint by Contra Costa County sheriff's deputies when an
automated license plate reader near the San Pablo Lytton Casino off Intersta
Highway 80 alerted police that they were riding in a stolen car.
