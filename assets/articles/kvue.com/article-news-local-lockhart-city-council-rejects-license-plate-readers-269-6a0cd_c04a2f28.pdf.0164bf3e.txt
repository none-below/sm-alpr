LOCKHART, Texas — The city of Lockhart will not be using Automatic License Plate Readers
(APLRs) any time soon.
At its meeting Thursday night, the Lockhart City Council voted 6-1 against a proposal to
enter a contract with Flock Safety to install seven license-plate-reading cameras in the city
limits, opting against using the controversial technology. Lockhart Mayor Lew White served
as the one vote in favor of adopting the technology.

However, the cameras have become a cause for concern for some residents in recent
months, with some concerned that the cameras could be used for mass surveillance and by
federal agencies like Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE).
Opposition at Thursday's meeting was organized by the Caldwell/Hays Education Fund and
Ground Game Texas, a pair of activist groups. Among those that spoke out against the
cameras was former Lockhart Mayor Jimmy Bertram.
“I ask you to please do what you're first called to do and that is defend my rights, defend my
grandchildren’s rights, defend all of these people's rights first," Bertram said on Thursday.
"Them cameras ain't worth that [...] We don't need them at the cost of violating everybody's
rights.”
Back in June, the city of Austin chose to end its contract with Flock Safety and not use the
cameras anymore. In the same month, the city of San Marcos shot down the idea to add
more cameras; instead, officials added new safeguards to the ones in place. A petition was
filed in Sunset Valley back in August, also asking for the removal of the cameras.
Caldwell County as a whole, however, has adopted the cameras, as the sheriff's office
announced an agreement with Flock Safety earlier this month. The cities of Pflugerville,
Round Rock and Kyle also have contracts with Flock.
