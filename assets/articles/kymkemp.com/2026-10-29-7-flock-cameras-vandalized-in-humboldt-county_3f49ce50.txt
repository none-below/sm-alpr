[All photos by Ryan Hutson] The Humboldt County Sheriff’s Office says seven of its Flock license plate reader cameras are down due to vandalism, and only two are still in service. A public information officer with HSO has confirmed that none of the cameras were intentionally decommissioned. The Sheriff’s Office said in a reply to our email inquiry, “Two cameras are in service. The rest…are all down due to vandalism.” In recent days Redheaded Blackbelt visited five mapped Flock locations in northern Humboldt County. At three of those places, the pole was still in place or lying on the ground, with its solar panel attached, but no camera unit was visible and cables hung loose. At a fourth location in Glendale near Blue Lake, a pole could not be found at all. Separately, KMUD News independently confirmed that a camera is also gone from a mapped location near Rio Dell on Highway 101. The California Highway Patrol told Redheaded Blackbelt it has never had Flock cameras in Humboldt County. The Sheriff’s Office, which operates the cameras, told Redheaded Blackbelt on Oct. 29 that the Myrtle Avenue camera was damaged by vandalism months ago, that it has recorded seven incidents of vandalism against Flock cameras, and that it has not removed or decommissioned any cameras on purpose. Myrtle Avenue, north of Eureka A Flock pole on Myrtle Avenue is lying on the ground with its solar panel still attached and wires hanging loose. No camera unit was visible. The Sheriff’s Office confirms that it operates this Flock camera, and that it was “down and damaged due to vandalism…” months ago. Murray Road, in McKinleyville On Oct. 24, the pole at this location was upright with its solar panel in place, but the bracket under the panel was empty. [Flock pole close-up of the solar panel and the empty bracket area] An electric cable hung loose from a connector near the top of the pole, with exposed pink and blue wire ends. Like with the Myrtle Avenue location, cobwebs were strung across the open bracket. [Flock camera pole with hanging cables with exposed pink and blue wire ends, on Murray Rd.] Central Avenue, near Clam Beach The pole on the far northern end of Central Avenue in McKinleyville was upright, but again, no camera was attached. Two cables hung from the top of the pole: wires dangled ending with a loose connector at the end. [Flock pole with hanging cables and no camera] A connector clearly visible on one cable suggests it was unplugged rather than cut, while the missing camera is consistent with vandalism documented at other locations. [Close-up of the solar panel and the empty bracket area] Central Avenue, at Harry Lane in McKinleyville A pole was still standing at this location, however, it could not be observed safely from a distance, and RHBB was unable to confirm if the camera head was in place – or missing, like the others nearby. Glendale Drive, near Highway 299 RHBB reported to, and searched this stretch of Glendale Drive several times in both directions and found no pole or camera at the location where a public map places one. Highway 101, near Rio Dell Redcrest KMUD News has independently confirmed that a camera is no longer in place at a mapped location along Highway 101 near Redcrest, as of this week. HSO Confirms Investigation Feedback from the Humboldt County Sheriff’s Office (HSO) clarified the pattern of vandalism. The office knows as soon as a camera is non-operational, because “there is no data received from it.” Summer Hansen, the PIO, said the office is aware of seven incidents and said an investigation is underway. She said no camera has been removed, relocated, or decommissioned on purpose. The office did not provide a list of existing camera locations. The Sheriff’s Office publishes a transparency page for its Flock program, which includes plate-scan figures over 30-day periods. That website does not list locations of Flock cameras in the county.
