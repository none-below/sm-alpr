What Michigan governor contenders say about Flock cameras, and 4 other major stories

  • Published: Sep. 14, 2026, 5:00 a.m.
By
  • Bradley Massman | bmassman@mlive.com
  • MLive Express Desk

Michigan gubernatorial candidates are beginning to define their positions on Flock cameras, automated license plate readers that have become increasingly controversial as their use expands across the state.

It is one of five “must-read” stories from MLive over the weekend.

What do candidates for Michigan governor say about Flock cameras?

Critics argue the cameras collect data on all drivers, not just suspects, raising privacy and Fourth Amendment concerns.

Republican candidate John James said the issue requires balancing public safety with constitutional privacy protection. Democratic candidate Jocelyn Benson said the main problem is a lack of transparency.

Reporter Danielle James shares more in this story.

This beloved Frankenmuth restaurant is where employees fall in love, get married, and raise their kids to work there too

Zehnder’s of Frankenmuth is portrayed not just as a long-running restaurant but as a multigenerational workplace where families build careers, relationships, and traditions.

The business remains family-led, with president Nick Shelton identified as a fourth-generation descendant of the founders.

Read more from reporter Justin Engel in this story.

Fraternity shut down, lawsuit pending in death of University of Michigan student

The family of University of Michigan student Lucas Mattson, who died in after attending a fraternity party, still plans to file a lawsuit against anyone deemed responsible.

The 19-year-old’s cause of death was hypothermia, with acute intoxication as a contributing factor.

Attorney Robert Raitt says potential defendants may include the University of Michigan and the Delta Chi fraternity.

Reporter Jordyn Pair shares more details in this story.

Video shows Coast Guard rescue 70-year-old hiker at Isle Royale National Park

A 70-year-old hiker was rescued from Isle Royale National Park after being missing for about a week.

Craig Berg was last expected to leave the island on Sept. 5 via ferry, but he never returned home.

The U.S. Coast Guard Air Station Traverse City released a three-minute video showing Berg being hoisted in a rescue basket into a helicopter.

Read more from reporter Bradley Massman in this story.
