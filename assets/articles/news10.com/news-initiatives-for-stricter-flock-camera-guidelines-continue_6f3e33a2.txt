Watch WTEN+ NOW PLAYING News Initiatives for stricter Flock camera guidelines continue by: Julia Barcello Posted: Nov 10, 2026 / 05:16 PM EST Updated: Nov 10, 2026 / 05:34 PM EST by: Julia Barcello Posted: Nov 10, 2026 / 05:16 PM EST Updated: Nov 10, 2026 / 05:34 PM EST SHARE EAST GREENBUSH, N.Y. ( NEWS10 ) — Across the region, controversy about the use of license plate readers is growing. Some communities are planning to vote to end contracts with Flock to use their cameras, while the state is looking to setup guidelines on their use. Monday, the New York State Assembly held a hearing that focused on rules on how Flock can or cannot operate. Assemblyman John McDonald says it was a non-partisan hearing in hopes to support the community’s concerns. “Yes, we’re concerned about safety, but we’re also very much concerned about protecting individuals, constitutional rights,” said McDonald. McDonald says the biggest takeaway is improving guidelines, not on the cameras themselves, but on the usage of their data. “How that information is shared and with who it’s being shared; and tangential to that there should be a conversation about who actually has access to that information. It needs to be clearly delineated. It needs to be very tight, and it needs to be auditable,” said McDonald. The statewide coordinator for DeFlock New York, Matt Brockbank, agrees saying Flock as a company, lacks credibility. “They’re pretty much operating in a way that makes the public not only feel uncomfortable, but extremely distrustful of this organization and that state that’s on them,” said Brockbank. In a statement to NEWS10, Flock Media Relations Specialist Andrew Smith said, “More than 40 courts across the country have determined that fixed license plate readers do not violate the fourth amendment and cannot track the whole of an individual’s movements. Customers fully control their data, and flock does not sell data.” Get the latest news, weather, sports and entertainment delivered right to your inbox! Towns including East Greenbush are working to enforce stricter guidelines. Brockbank says if local communities don’t take action, the state can. “We would like East Greenbush to come up with a local law that either bans or extremely restricts this technology altogether. And again, that’s to protect the public and their Fourth Amendment rights… So, you know, East Greenbush may not have to do anything. New York State may put restrictions or a law in place that bans or restricts this technology as well,” said Brockbank. Officials agree the technology can support investigation, but the concern is how. “Yes, they’ve been helpful with regards to significantly serious crimes. Those are important issues. Once again, their focus is, yes, we want our communities to be safe, but we also want to be very safe with the information that’s being gathered,” said McDonald. Brockbank shared a similar sentiment. “So we very much are fans and trust the East Greenbush Police Department, but we don’t trust this technology, and we certainly, certainly, do not trust this vendor,” he said. The next East Greenbush DeFlock meeting is November 11 at 6:00 p.m. East Greenbush is set to vote on “deFlocking” the town on November 18.
