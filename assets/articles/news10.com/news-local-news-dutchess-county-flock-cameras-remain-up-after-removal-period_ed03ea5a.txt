Watch WTEN+ NOW PLAYING Local Dutchess County Flock cameras remain up after removal period by: Dan Passante Posted: Nov 2, 2026 / 05:00 PM EST Updated: Nov 2, 2026 / 05:14 PM EST by: Dan Passante Posted: Nov 2, 2026 / 05:00 PM EST Updated: Nov 2, 2026 / 05:14 PM EST SHARE ALBANY, N.Y. ( NEWS10 ) — When Dutchess County District Attorney Anthony Parisi and County Sheriff Kirk Imperati decided to withdraw their support for Flock cameras over a month ago, they expected the cameras to come down within a 30-day period. The decision happened on September 25 after residents in the county raised concerns at community forums. “Public safety depends on public trust,” Imperati said in a statement. “We cannot lose sight of that.” Get the latest news, weather, sports and entertainment delivered right to your inbox! According to officials in Dutchess County, many of the cameras remain up. The County Executive’s Office told NEWS10 in an email that the county had 64 cameras, and 35 of them have been taken down. “The remaining 22 are located on NYS DOT right-of-way/property and require permits from the NYS DOT for work to be done,” reads an email from a county official. “Those cameras will be removed following issuance of the necessary permits.” Download our WTEN+ streaming app on Roku, Fire TV and Apple TV! NEWS10 spoke to a representative from Flock Safety on the phone. They said that their company has been in contact with Dutchess County officials for a number of weeks now. The representative noted that there is a process for taking the cameras down, which normally takes 60 days. Factors like weather can delay the cameras actually being taken down. The representative shared a statement with NEWS10. “Flock is grateful for the opportunity to partner with the Dutchess County community and respects their decisions,” reads the statement. “All cameras have been deactivated and we are working with the sheriff’s office on removals.” Ulster County executive bans AI surveillance tech under her authority The Flock representative confirmed the work orders are in to remove the remaining cameras. There is no timeline for when the cameras will be removed.
