( NEWS10 ) — Ulster is the latest local county to take action on Flock cameras. Thursday Ulster County Executive Jen Metzger signed an executive order “… prohibiting all departments and employees under her authority from purchasing, contracting for, or deploying Flock cameras or any similar AI‑assisted surveillance technologies.” She told NEWS10 the order is in response to a number of concerns she has. “This particular realm of surveillance of camera technology using AI assisted camera technologies, it’s very clear cut to me,” said Metzger. “We do not need that.” Get the latest news, weather, sports and entertainment delivered right to your inbox! Metzger says one of her concerns about how fast the technology can make updates. She says it’s faster than governments can keep up. “Companies do these automatic updates of software when you get, you know, when governments enter into these, software agreements,” said Metzger. “You have no idea what that functionality is necessarily.” Download our WTEN+ streaming app on Roku, Fire TV and Apple TV! The county executive also cited security breaches that can expose people’s information, as well as the misuse of the cameras by some law enforcement in other parts of the country. She does not want to have AI doing the work of law enforcement. “This is AI doing the work, potentially, you know, analysis of bringing data in from many different sources, “said Metzger. “Creating databases, potentially sharing databases and introducing all kinds of bias and error, potentially.” Lawmakers call for guardrails on Flock cameras Metzger included that she has the support of County Sheriff Juan Figueroa. She added that Figueroa will be releasing his own statement on the technology soon. The executive order will prohibit the technology on county property but does not have authority over municipalities or the sheriff’s office. Flock cameras are not currently being used by Ulster County. The executive order goes into effect immediately. “We are drawing the line a hard line and saying no to these technologies,” said Metzger.
