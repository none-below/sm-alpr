Watch WTEN+ NOW PLAYING NY Capitol News NYS lawmakers discuss standards for license plate reader cameras by: David Rush Posted: Nov 9, 2026 / 05:42 PM EST Updated: Nov 9, 2026 / 08:10 PM EST by: David Rush Posted: Nov 9, 2026 / 05:42 PM EST Updated: Nov 9, 2026 / 08:10 PM EST SHARE ALBANY, N.Y. ( NEWS10 ) – The debate over Automated License Plate Readers, or ALPRs, has reached the New York State Capitol. Lawmakers are taking a look at how the license plate reader cameras are being used throughout the state. Download our WTEN+ streaming app on Roku, Fire TV and Apple TV! The leaders of three Assembly committees held a public hearing at the Capitol on Monday. They include the Assembly Standing Committees on Consumer Affairs and Protection, Governmental Operations, and Local Governments. Supporters of ALPRs, including law enforcement, said they are needed to help track down criminals and solve crimes. But members are particularly interested in examining ways to establish standards for the use of automatic license plate reader systems in New York State. The standards revolve around data privacy, information sharing, and other consumer protections, and safeguards to ensure that the use of such technology is consistent with both public safety objectives and individual privacy rights. While the hearing was going on inside the Hamilton Hearing Room, protesters against the cameras gathered outside. They voiced their concerns over privacy and the selling of data. Get the latest news, weather, sports and entertainment delivered right to your inbox! “We are giving all this data to a private company,” said Michael Chameides of the Columia County Sanctuary Movement. He adds, “The way the contract works for Columbia County is the private company is actually allowed to do whatever it wants, it owns that data that New York state is paying for, and it can do whatever it wants, and that’s a huge risk.” One protester from Western New York is concerned that law enforcement can abuse the data and use it for nefarious reasons, such as a member of the Albany County Sheriff’s Office allegedly using the cameras to spy on an ex-lover . “They’re using it for a reason right out of Peyton Place. They’re using it for soap opera logic,” said Buffalo attorney Brendan Gilbert. He adds, “They’re using it to determine whether or not their co-worker was turning left or turning right in order to support their workplace gossip and bullying. This is ridiculous.” Right now, there are no universal minimum standards for data usage and sharing by the license plate reader systems in New York.
