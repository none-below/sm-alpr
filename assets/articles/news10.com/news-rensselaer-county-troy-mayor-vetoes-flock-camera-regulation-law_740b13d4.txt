( NEWS10 ) — Troy Mayor Carmella Mantello has vetoed a law passed by the Troy City Council on October 1 that aims to regulate the city’s Flock Safety automated license plate readers (ALPR). Mantello announced her veto of Local Law 3 on Thursday evening. Download our WTEN+ streaming app on Roku, Fire TV and Apple TV! Fourth District City Councilmember Noreen McKee proposed the legislation, which was the first of its kind in New York. Local Law 3 reduced how long police can store license plate data from 30 days to 72 hours and restricts the sharing of the data without explicit judicial authorization. Under the legislation, camera searches are also limited to investigations related to or involving a felony. On Thursday, in Mantello’s veto letter, she said the “law is biased in favor or criminal defendants,” and “significantly undermines the ability of the Troy Police Department to investigate and solve serious violent crimes committed in the City.” She also said purging the data after 72 hours would often happen before leads were fully developed and in some cases, before some crimes were even reported. As a result, Mantello said violent criminals will not be identified and will be able to evade arrest. Get the latest news, weather, sports and entertainment delivered right to your inbox! Her veto statement also explains that she believe the law doesn’t fulfill its stated purposes. Mantello stated that it’s not consistent with the protections provided under Article I, Section 12 of the New York State Constitution , which provides security against unreasonable searches, seizures and interceptions. Instead, Mantello said Local Law 3 “goes far beyond” those protections, as “there is no reasonable expectation of privacy in a public facing license plate or the exterior characteristics of an automobile being driven on the public ways.” Also, she expressed that the council’s “purpose to prevent misuse of constitutionally collected information by mandating that it be destroyed after 72 hours is trying to kill a fly with a blunderbuss.” “Not only is it overkill, but it misses the target while providing a substantial benefit to criminals,” reads part of the veto message. What prevents misuse of Flock data is professional police management and standards, not amateurish regulations drafted by non-professionals and driven by feverish phantom fears.” Mantello shared that Troy has had and used camera surveillance systems since at least 2015 and in those years, there have not been any instances where any Troy Police investigators have misused them. The mayor concluded her veto message by expressing her deep disappointment that the Troy City Council chose to enact Local Law 3, after law enforcement professionals said that the law was “unwise and completely misguided.” Questions remain about Greenport Flock contract after board meeting Mantello’s full veto message for Local Law 3 can be read below. “I hereby veto Local Law No. 3 of 2026, which purports to establish standards governing the City’s use of ALPR systems. This law is biased in favor Of criminal defendants. It significantly undermines the ability of the Troy Police Department to investigate and solve serious violent crimes committed in the City. It infringes on the Mayor’s and the Chiefs ability to manage the police force. And it mandates that constitutionally collected proof Of criminal activity be purged and destroyed after 72 hours (unless defense counsel requests preservation). This purge will occur in many cases before leads have been fully developed, and in some cases, before the crime has even been reported. There is no doubt in my mind that, as a result of this law, violent criminals will not be identified and will be able to evade arrest. Further, as District Attorney Donnelly has confirmed, prosecution of those who are caught will be substantially impaired, allowing many to escape justice when — without this law – the crime might have been easily proved. Moreover, this law does not fulfill its stated purposes. It is not consistent with the protections provided under Article I, Section 12 of the New York State Constitution and the Fourth Amendment to the United States Constitution. It goes far beyond them. There is no reasonable expectation of privacy in a public facing license plate Or the exterior characteristics of an automobile being driven on the public ways. The information collected by the Flock cameras is simply not private information. Also, the Council ‘s purpose to prevent misuse of constitutionally collected information by mandating that it be destroyed after 72 hours is trying to kill a fly with a blunderbuss. Not only is it overkill, but it misses the target while providing a substantial benefit to criminals. What prevents misuse of Flock data is professional police management and standards, not amateurish regulations drafted by non-professionals and driven by feverish phantom fears. Troy has had and has used various camera surveillance systems since at least 2015. In all those years, there has not been a single instance where any Troy police investigator has misused the City’s cameras for some improper purpose in violation of the department’s policy and regulations. Troy police investigate crimes not law abiding people. They share and value the same constitutional protections as everyone else. This law is an affront to their hard work and their ethics, as well as to the sound professional management of the force. Finally, I need to say that I am deeply disappointed that the Council has chosen to enact this Local Law. Every law enforcement professional who appeared before you — including all of our chiefs of the department and the Rensselaer County District Attorney – testified that this law was unwise and completely misguided. I cannot believe that you would not take their considered professional advice. For these reasons, I respectfully veto Local Law #3″ City of Troy Mayor Carmella R.
