Watch WTEN+ NOW PLAYING News Troy City Council suing mayor over Flock contract by: Alex Berger Posted: Oct 24, 2026 / 12:02 AM EDT Updated: Oct 24, 2026 / 09:28 AM EDT by: Alex Berger Posted: Oct 24, 2026 / 12:02 AM EDT Updated: Oct 24, 2026 / 09:28 AM EDT SHARE TROY, N.Y. (WTEN) — Troy City Council overrode Mayor Carmella Mantello’s veto of legislation that limits Flock Safety camera data storage to just 72 hours. Download our WTEN+ streaming app on Roku, Fire TV and Apple TV! Mantello told News10 she disapproves of the council’s decision Thursday night. When asked to speak on camera, a spokesperson for her office instead sent a statement saying the council has refused to work with them on this. She said in part, “The council has refused to listen to law enforcement professionals over and over regarding the usage of our ALPRs. This new pro-criminal law will undoubtedly have a negative impact on their ability to solve crimes and hold criminals accountable.” Get the latest news, weather, sports and entertainment delivered right to your inbox! But, Council President Sue Steele said they’re taking matters into their own hands. “We’re suing the mayor who entered into an illegal contract with Flock, and it will be a couple months down the road before we have the judge’s decision,” she said. “But it’s very possible that the judge will come back and say, ‘You’re right. That contract is null and void,’ and then we’re out of Flock.” It wouldn’t be until at least after the case that the automated license plate reader (ALPR) cameras would even potentially be taken down. Until then, they’re still going to be in operation all across the city. But what would a Flock-less Troy look like? “What did Troy look like before Flock?” Steele said. “Cases were solved. The police did their work. In communities all around the country, they’re getting rid of Flock, so their police are somehow doing their job. It’s conceivable.” Troy mayor vetoes ALPR regulation law In Ulster County, roughly an hour from Troy, County Executive Jen Metzger signed an executive order Thursday to ban the purchase or installation of Flock cameras or similar AI-assisted technologies in the county. Ulster County executive bans AI surveillance tech under her authority If Troy’s contract with Flock does become void, Steele said the city could submit a Request for Proposal (RFP) and choose a different APLR vendor. But, if the city ends up not paying for a contract like this anymore, Steele said there are options for where the money would go. “It could go right back into the police department to hire more police,” she said. “There are many possibilities.” Steele said the council doesn’t plan on regulating the cameras any further at this time. News10 has reached out to the Troy Police Department for an interview on this matter, but have not yet heard back. The council president said ultimately, the situation is out of their hands, and comes down to the decision of a judge.
