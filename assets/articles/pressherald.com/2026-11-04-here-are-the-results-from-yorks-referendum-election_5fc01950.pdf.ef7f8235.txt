YORK AMBULANCE FUNDING
Following a monthslong struggle to find a solution for a significant shortfall in funding, York residents voted to
approve a question that grants $500,000 to York Ambulance Association. A total of 7,096 people voted in favor,
while 1,291 people voted against the funding.
The town will use $500,000 from its unassigned fund balance to sustain the service through the end of its
contract with the town in July 2027. The service will initially receive $300,000 and will earn the rest in monthly
installments so long as it can meet a set of conditions established by the town.
Officials say they will continue exploring the possibility of transitioning to an in-house EMS service instead of
one that’s contracted.
SINGLE-USE PLASTICS
Residents decided in a 5,921-2,287 vote to make permanent an ordinance banning single-use plastics.

Restaurants and other establishments in York were already required to use earth-friendly or compostable stir
sticks, straws and utensils. The ban, first adopted in May 2025, would have expired Dec. 31 because of a clause
written into the ordinance.

‘STOP ALPRs’ ORDINANCE
Residents also voted to ban automated license plate reading cameras, or ALPRs, in a 5,386-2,043 vote amid a
nationwide debate over the ethics of similar technology.
The York Police Department terminated its contract with the ALPR company Flock Safety in September and
removed at least four cameras on public property following a citizen petition that garnered 115 signatures.

EMERGENCY MANAGEMENT, WIRELESS COMMUNICATIONS, REVENUE
BONDS
Voters also approved an emergency management question that reorganizes disaster relief funds in a 6,896-1,127
vote.
An ordinance redefining requirements for mounting wireless communication antennae passed with 6,130 votes
in favor and 1,511 votes against the ordinance.
Residents also considered a question created by citizen petition that would set new parameters for which
projects could use general obligation funds and which could be funded by revenue bonds. A total of 3,962
people voted to approve the ordinance, while 3,579 people voted against it.
