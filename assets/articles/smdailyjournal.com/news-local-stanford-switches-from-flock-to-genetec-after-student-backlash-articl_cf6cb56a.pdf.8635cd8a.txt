Stanford switches from Flock to Genetec after student
backlash
Bay City News
Aug 7, 2026
 
Stanford University has switched from Flock Safety to Genetec for its automated
license plate readers following backlash from students, according to the
university’s official newsletter this week.

There were 19 Flock cameras on campus. Flock service officially ended in late
July, and the cameras have since been turned off and physically covered,
according to university spokesperson Luisa Rapport. They will be replaced with
Genetec equipment by early September.
Flock cameras take photos of license plates and can share data with other law
enforcement agencies, but opponents worry that they can be used for
surveillance. Some are also worried about potential violations of state law that
limits the sharing of license plate data.
Stanford’s decision comes amid a wave of pushback against Flock from students.
In May, students circulated a petition to remove the cameras. To date, the
petition has collected over 1,000 signatures from Stanford staff, students, alumni
and affiliates.
According to the university, Genetec’s system differs from Flock’s since it does
not store captured images in the cloud for more than an hour. It would also not
share data with third parties.
