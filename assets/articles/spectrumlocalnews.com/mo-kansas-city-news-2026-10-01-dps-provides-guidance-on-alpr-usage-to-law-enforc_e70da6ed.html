DPS provides guidance on ALPR usage to law en­forcement agencies

BY Josephfer Williams Kansas City
PUBLISHED 2:58 PM CT Oct. 01, 2026 PUBLISHED October 1, 2026 @2:58 PM
SHARE

After Gov. Mike Kehoe signed Executive Order 26-18, the Department of Public Safety (DPS) ‌published guidance for Missouri law enforcement agencies on the best practices related to the use of automated license plate readers (ALPR). 

Executive Order 26-18, signed on Sep. 18, was an order that looked to protect the safety and security of Missourians and target technology like ALPR and Flock Cameras.

It also orders DPS to make its APLR guidance available within 10 business days and distribute it to all state and local enforcement agencies on record with the DPS.

“We’ve spent the last two weeks reviewing a wide range of law enforcement agency ALPRs policies, including from Missouri police departments and county sheriffs’ offices, as well as the work of national law enforcement associations,” Department of Public Safety Director Mark James said. “What stood out from my individual discussions with Missouri chiefs, sheriffs and the Missouri State Highway Patrol was that many agencies have proactively instituted very significant guardrails to protect privacy, the overwhelming desire to protect constitutional rights, and support for strong penalties for any violation of constitutional rights."

James added he's heard countless cases in which ALPRs were crucial in making arrests and crimes being solved – ranging from child abductions to highly organized national retail theft operations to homicides.

“Automatic license plate readers have proven to be an incredibly effective crime-fighting tool at a time when many law enforcement agencies are experiencing very significant officer shortages,” Gov. Kehoe said. “However, comprehensive legislation must be enacted to guarantee ALPRs are only used for legitimate law enforcement purposes, privacy is protected, and there are very serious consequences for those that break the rules regarding any ALPR restrictions that are adopted."

DPS guidance is designed to ensure strict safeguards on the use of ALPR technology, including Flock Safety cameras, and recommendations for best practices related to ensuring: 

  • a legitimate law enforcement purpose for any ALPR data search;

  • individual accountability for anyone with access to ALPR data; 

  • independent verification of ALPR alerts before enforcement;

  • limited (30 days in most cases) retention of ALPR data; 

  • routine auditing (at least monthly) and targeted reviews of ALPR use; and

  • prohibiting the integration of artificial intelligence facial recognition technology with ALPR systems
