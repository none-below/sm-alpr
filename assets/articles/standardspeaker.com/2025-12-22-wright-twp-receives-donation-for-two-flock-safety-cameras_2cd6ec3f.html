    Wright Twp. receives donation for two Flock Safety cameras

    By Pat Rushton | prushton132@gmail.com
    PUBLISHED: December 22, 2025 at 9:00 AM EST
    Getting your Trinity Audio player ready...

    Thanks to a donation by a township business, the Wright Twp. police department will soon become part of a network of law enforcement agencies using state-of-the art technology to fight crime.

    Township supervisors at their December meeting voted to accept the donation of two Flock Safety cameras. Police chief Royce Engler said the cameras were donated by a township business that wanted to remain anonymous. The cameras cost $6,300 and will be placed at two undisclosed locations in the township. While there is no initial cost to the township, Engler said there will be an annual fee of $6,000 starting in 2027.

    Engler told supervisors that neighboring Butler Twp. as well as Hazleton City are already using the Flock camera system and that both municipalities have successfully used the camera system to solve crimes. Engler noted that the system has been used to track vehicles from community to community.

    Using solar power, the cameras provide law enforcement grade license plate recognition along with the company’s “Vehicle Fingerprint” system. The “Vehicle Fingerprint” feature allows police access to vehicle type, make, color, license plate state and other unique features like bumper stickers, decals and roof racks.

    As part of Flock Safety’s system, police can request direct access to feeds from other law enforcement agencies as well as from privately owned Flock System license plate reader cameras in neighborhoods, schools and businesses.

    The system also provides participating departments with automated alerts when vehicles entered into established databases for missing and/or wanted persons are detected, including the FBI’s National Crime Information Center (NCIC) and the National Center for Missing and Exploited Children.

    Other news

    Rice Twp. supervisors are looking for state help to replace the Henry Drive bridge. Supervisors voted this month to apply for a $1 million grant from the PennDOT multimodal grant program for the replacement of the bridge which spans Little Wapwallopen Cree.

    Wright Twp. is looking for help. The township is seeking applicants for a full-time laborer and equipment operator for its public works department. Applications are available at the township office, 321 S. Mountain Blvd. and are due b y noon on Dec. 29
