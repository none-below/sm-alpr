Flock's AI surveillance cameras are continuing to cause
controversy, this time in Dayton, Minnesota, where it is alleged
that the company reinstalled two cameras despite express orders
from the city not to do so. The city's local government posted on
Facebook to explain, claiming that the Police Department was
made aware of a maintenance work order on September 17, to
which Dayton responded the next day, stating the program had
been put on hold and no work orders were to be completed.
However, Flock went ahead and reinstalled them anyway without
authorization from the city.
Dayton’s city council had already voted to suspend the automatic
license plate reader program and Flock cameras on August 25,
following community concerns over privacy and data misuse, The
Drive reports. Flock is infamous nationwide, with instances of its
cameras being used for unjust purposes, such as a car reviewer
mistakenly being pulled over because one of its cameras misread
the tags. In another case, Flock’s chief information security officer
also falsely assured the Oshkosh City Council that Flock did not
create a heat map of individual license plates, when it in fact does
have a heat map feature in the Flock system.
Flock cameras in the city had already been broken and vandalized,
leading police to disable and remove the cameras to prevent
further damage on August 20, before the program’s suspension
became official, local media reports. Despite the suspension,
almost a full month later, on September 17, Flock submitted the
offending work order, which led to this latest controversy.

We don’t know when Flock reinstalled the cameras, but Dayton
citizens had already complained and reported spotting the two
Flock cameras, leading to the September 29 Facebook post. The
local government further stated that the Dayton Police
Department had been informed and ordered to remove the
cameras.
It is unclear what happened in the chain of communication from
when the license plate reader program was suspended and
canceled its contract to notify Flock to cease work orders; either
Flock defiantly ignored the orders or instructions had not reached
the people responsible for installing the cameras; either way, this
could be another blow to Flock's reputation.
