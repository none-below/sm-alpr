Former JPD officer indicted on federal cyberstalking charge

Najwa Zandlo was taken into FBI custody the morning of Oct. 16
By Jaxon Steward
Published: Oct. 16, 2026 at 11:11 PM UTC
Add Us On GoogleAdd as a preferred source on Google

JACKSON, Tenn. (WBBJ) - A federal grand jury indicted former Jackson Police Department patrol officer Najwa Zandlo on one count of cyberstalking on Tuesday, following an investigation by the Jackson Police Department and the FBI.

Zandlo was taken into custody by FBI agents on the morning of Oct. 16, 2026.

Internal investigation

In October 2025, while Zandlo was employed with the Jackson Police Department, a local citizen filed a formal complaint regarding her conduct. JPD placed Zandlo on administrative leave and opened an internal investigation after receiving the allegations.

The internal investigation found Zandlo used police technology and databases for purposes unrelated to law enforcement. Zandlo resigned in lieu of termination before her scheduled internal disciplinary hearing.

Due to the findings of JPD’s internal investigation and the nature of the allegations, the matter was referred to the FBI for further investigation. The case was presented to a federal grand jury following a joint review of the findings by the District Attorney General’s Office and the United States Attorney’s Office, resulting in the federal charge.

The Jackson Police Department will also request that the Tennessee Peace Officers Standards and Training Commission revoke Zandlo’s certification as a law enforcement officer.

Chief’s statement

“The Jackson Police Department holds all officers to the highest standards of integrity, professionalism, and accountability,” said Chief Thom Corley. “Constitutional Policing is a foundational pillar at JPD, and the expectation that our officers adhere to it is non-negotiable. We remain committed to ensuring our personnel uphold the moral and ethical standards expected by the community we serve.”
