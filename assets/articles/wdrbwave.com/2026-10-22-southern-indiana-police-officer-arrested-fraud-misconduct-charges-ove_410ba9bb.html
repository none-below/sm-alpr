Southern Indiana police officer arrested on fraud, misconduct charges over alleged Flock misuse

By Reyna Katko
Published: Oct. 22, 2026 at 11:07 PM UTC|Updated: Oct. 23, 2026 at 9:17 PM UTC
Add Us On GoogleAdd as a preferred source on Google

LOUISVILLE, Ky. (WDRB+WAVE) — A Georgetown Police Department officer was arrested Thursday for alleged misuse of the Flock system.

According to Indiana State Police, a detective met with the Georgetown police chief after the chief was alerted to irregular search activity within the Flock system.

ISP said an internal review completed by Georgetown police showed officer Justin Craig conducted numerous searches in 2026 that “did not appear connected to any authorized investigation.”

Court records reveal an ISP trooper found 88 searches for the car Craig’s wife drives. He also said he found more than 230 searches for a car and motorcycle owned by a “previous acquaintance” of hers.

Investigators reviewed logs and determined Craig conducted repeated searches using reasons like traffic violations, drug offenses, assault or vehicle theft. Georgetown’s police chief said none of the vehicles were tied to any active investigations.

According to ISP, the searches were conducted during Craig’s scheduled duty hours.

ISP determined Craig accessed Flock information “in a manner inconsistent with department policy and without lawful purpose.”

A warrant for Craig was served Thursday and he was taken into custody. He has since been released on bond.

Craig was charged with two counts of fraud and one count of official misconduct.

Craig’s attorney Matthew Lemme released the following statement:

“Flock sells this same technology to cities, homeowners associations, and shopping malls. When public criticism of it came, the response was to put it on the shoulders of individual police officers.

The conduct alleged does not violate any law or department policy. If the public or police agencies believe it should, the answer is to write that law or policy. Having that public conversation about privacy and practice is appropriate. Charging officers before it has even started is not.

Officer Craig will file a motion to dismiss the charges against him."

Craig joined the Georgetown Police Department in May 2025, where he was one of three full-time officers.

The Georgetown Police Department released a statement on Friday:

“On Aug. 31, 2026, Officer Justin Craig was placed on paid administrative leave following an allegation of misuse and audit of the department’s LPR system.

Indiana State Police conducted an investigation and Officer Justin Craig was arrested on Oct. 22, 2026.

Due to pending criminal case, the Georgetown Police Department will not be providing any additional information at this time."
