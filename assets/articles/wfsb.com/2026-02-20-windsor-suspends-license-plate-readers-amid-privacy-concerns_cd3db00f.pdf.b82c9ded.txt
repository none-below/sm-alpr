WINDSOR, CT. (WFSB) - The Windsor Town Council voted to suspend automatic license plate
readers after residents raised concerns about where their personal information is being stored. 

--- page 2 ---
The Flock cameras, which scan license plates of every passing vehicle, were installed at busy
intersections and roads throughout Windsor to help police investigate crimes.
Windsor resident Jeff Pitkin said he supports the decision to shut down the cameras.
“I’m glad they got rid of it,” he said. “I don’t know how it’s being used. That’s what it is so I’d rather
stay safe and secure rather than have my information out just because I’m driving around they
can tell where I’m at at whatever time.”
Several residents spoke against the cameras at the town council meeting, expressing concerns
about data storage.
“While our police department has policies in place to ensure our officers handle the information
collected correctly, we have no power over others who have access to that data,” one resident
said.
“We know there are bad actors in the federal government for targeting people because of their
race and because of their gender identity and because of the religion and political affiliation
ideas,” said another person.
Windsor Police Chief Donald Melanson said officers view the suspension as limiting their
investigative capabilities.
“They would take that as something that inhibits their job. They are the ones that have to deal
with the victims of crime and that’s very difficult to do. They’re the ones that are going to have to
tell people we don’t have this tool available to follow that lead,” he said.
Flock Safety said it has no relationship with ICE and that data remains secure and under
Windsor’s control.
“We are working closely with the City of Windsor to address their concerns and ensure they are
familiar with all of the data sharing controls and audit tools built into our system,’ said the
company after the vote.
Retired Lt. Paul Vance said the cameras can be useful for police, but data control is crucial.

“The control of that data, though, is extremely important from a law enforcement perspective and
from a civilian perspective, people don’t want their data out there. They want to control and they
want to make sure that the controls are very, very secure,” he said.
Windsor’s mayor said the suspension is temporary while the town works to revise contract
language with Flock to better protect resident data. If the contract is terminated early, the town will
pay around $8,000.
