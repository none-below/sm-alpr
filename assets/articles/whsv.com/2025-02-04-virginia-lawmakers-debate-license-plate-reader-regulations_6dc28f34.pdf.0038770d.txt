RICHMOND, Va. (WWBT) - Concerns are growing over exactly how law enforcement is able to
track you and how that data can be used.
“Just because something catches the bad guys, so to speak, doesn’t mean it’s constitutional. We
do have constitutional rights,” said Brad Haywood, Justice Forward Virginia founder.
In Virginia, we’ve seen the emergence of Flock license plate reader cameras.
Police in Richmond and Henrico are using them too.
“They are not currently regulated in Virginia, so local police departments have been able to use
them as they wish and preserve the data as they wish. So there is actually a need for regulation
of automatic license plate readers in Virginia,” said Haywood.
Now a bill circulating inside the statehouse would create guardrails around the information
collected and what happens to it.
“This bill will limit the law enforcement use of license plate readers to active criminal
investigations and matters involving missing persons, wanted persons, human trafficking, stolen
vehicles and license plates,” said Del. Charniele Herring, D-4th District.
Also contained inside House Bill 2724 is the ability for Virginia State Police to now have those
license plate readers placed along 60,000 miles of state highways.
Right now, only local police can use them on local roads.
“This is a massive expansion of the surveillance state, and that’s what we are really concerned
about,” said Haywood.
The bill, which is from a state crime commission recommendation, would also make misusing that
data a class 1 misdemeanor.
“It requires that data that is stored to be destroyed within 30 days unless it is needed for on-going
investigation or prosecution and it requires law enforcement to adopt a policy on license plate
reader use,” said Del. Herring.
