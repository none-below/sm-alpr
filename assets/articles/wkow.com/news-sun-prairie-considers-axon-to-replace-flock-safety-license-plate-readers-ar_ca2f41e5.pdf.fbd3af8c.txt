Sun Prairie considers Axon to replace Flock Safety
SUN PRAIRIE, Wis. (WKOW) -- Sun Prairie will not renew its contract with Flock
Safety on Dec. 31 and is considering Axon as a potential replacement for its license
plate reader cameras.
Police Chief Kevin Warych says the decision isn't because Sun Prairie has experienced
problems with Flock. Instead, his department wants to consolidate its technology, as it
already uses Axon for body cameras and Tasers. The move also addresses concerns
about privacy, data retention and who can access the information.
"The license plate reader technology is very much the same. There is nothing, as far as
when I mean by that, is a picture of a license plate at that point in time," Warych said.
"No matter what vendor you choose, that's what they do."
The difference will come in how Sun Prairie handles the data. Warych says the city will
change how long information is kept and which law enforcement agencies can access
it.

"We're going to start from scratch, and we're going to work our way with what we
think is right," Warych said.
But David Bendit, who has lived in Sun Prairie for seven years, says switching
companies doesn't address concerns about an invasion of public privacy.
"Three cameras were added specifically around Woodman's and the dialysis center
over there, which means anyone going to that place cannot get there by vehicle
without being tracked by these cameras," Bendit said.
He questions whether the city needs the cameras at all.
"Policing existed long before it," Bendit said.
Warych says the technology can help police develop leads faster and that he wants to
be open and transparent about the new technology.
Community members will also have a chance to weigh in. A public hearing is set for
Oct. 7.
