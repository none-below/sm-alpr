Exhibit A: Scope of Services 
Project Understanding  
The Daly City Police Department, on behalf of the Cities of Daly City, San Bruno, and San Mateo, is seeking 
the services of an external consultant to evaluate the implementation of the Board of State and 
Community Corrections (BSCC) Organized Retail Theft (ORT) Prevention Grant, awarded in September 
2023. This $267 million grant program is intended to address rising rates of property crimes statewide, 
including ORT, Motor Vehicle Theft (MVT), and Motor Vehicle Accessory Theft (MVAT).  As a part of the 
State Budget Act of 2022 (S.B. 154), the grant cycle runs from October 1, 2023 through June 1, 2027. 
The cities of Daly City, San Bruno, and San Mateo are home to the four largest shopping malls in San Mateo 
County, each with over 100 stores, businesses, and restaurants, and all three cities enjoy a healthy 
downtown retail economy. In addition to statewide trends, property crime issues in neighboring San 
Francisco County and the closure of nearly half of all retail stores in San Francisco’s downtown hub has 
increased the need for this multi-agency effort to develop and implement a set of coordinated strategies 
to ensure employee and customer safety and prevent significant losses to retailers and residents.  
In preparing the grant application, Daly City Police Department (DCPD) conducted a comprehensive Needs 
Assessment to gather documentation from numerous sources to identify a set of intensifying challenges 
related to ORT, MVT and MVAT crimes. Recognizing that this is a regional problem that can be addressed 
most effectively through partnership, DCPD partnered with San Bruno and San Mateo Police Departments 
in their successful application to the BSCC. Together, these agencies will launch a coordinated and 
strategic effort that includes Community Service Officers (CSOs); an ORT Task Force; DCPD MVT and MVAT 
Operations; Community Awareness Campaigns; and technologies such as Starchase, C3 investigative 
tools, and automatic license plate readers to enhance current efforts to protect and serve.  
While a regional effort is the most efficient and effective method for implementing these strategies given 
the permeability of municipal boundaries, it does create measurement challenges. The successful 
evaluator must begin with a thorough understanding of the context in each of the jurisdictions, including 
existing data, current strategies and challenges, key stakeholders, and any nuance in objectives. Further, 
the ORT Grant Program has received significant media attention and addresses a set of issues that are 
deeply important to retail business owners, employees, and residents. Thus, a successful evaluation will 
need to factor in the complexity of a multi-city and a multi-pronged intervention strategy for this public 
facing project.  
To this end, the evaluator must be prepared to serve as a partner to the grant implementation team from 
the beginning, collaborating with the ORT grant team in identifying stakeholders, ensuring sufficient data 
sharing protocols and related guidance, monitoring data collection activities and results, assuring the 
security of data, and analyzing the data to prepare mid-stream findings a well as the Final Local Evaluation 
Report (LER) which is due to the BSCC on June 1, 2027. By joining the effort from the beginning, an 
experienced evaluator can help to identify successes and challenges along the way, not only with data 
collection, but in identifying the trends to be found in the data to assure an agile, responsive, and 
measurably successful program implementation.

Project Approach 
RDA believes that the function of evaluation is to strengthen the abilities of public agencies and service 
providers to fulfill their missions and serve their communities. We are committed to a collaborative 
evaluation process, working in partnership with our clients and their partners to help them articulate, 
design, implement, and evaluate programs that measurably achieve stated objectives. We recognize the 
importance of high-quality data, and our evaluations are rooted in robust, mixed method approaches that 
collect, analyze, and report on qualitative and quantitative data to explore targeted evaluation questions. 
A mixed-method approach allows us to maximize validity by examining social phenomena in different 
ways and from varying perspectives.  
While RDA’s approach to evaluation design is methodologically rigorous, it is also flexible. We recognize 
that evaluation activities may need to evolve in response to the realities of program implementation, 
particularly for new and innovative programs such as this multi-agency ORT prevention program. This 
flexibility allows our evaluation approaches to remain relevant and responsive to programs’ emerging 
needs and ensures the evaluation aligns with program goals and priorities.  
Further, our approach is informed by our expertise in the legal system and policing interventions and 
working with community members. We will leverage our knowledge, and the data capacity that the 
investment in the C3.ai platform will bring to the partners, to design a tailored evaluation informed by 
and accountable to the partners, your stakeholders, and all BSCC reporting requirements. 
Highlights of RDA’s approach include: 
• 
A collaborative and community-engaged evaluation process. This process ensures that the 
evaluation methodologies are relevant, culturally informed, that qualitative data is gathered from 
a range of stakeholders, and the resulting findings and recommendations are responsive to 
expressed needs and shared in an accessible manner.  
• 
Actionable recommendations. To maximize usefulness, evaluation findings that show program 
strengths, opportunities, and challenges must be linked with learning and action items. We work 
closely with our clients to develop actionable recommendations to improve the implementation 
process and associated outcomes. 
• 
A multi-level approach. Recognizing that interventions will be implemented across jurisdictions, 
in different communities that may have unique compositions and features, RDA will conduct a 
multi-level evaluation that captures findings about the project holistically, as well as lessons 
learned from the implementation of interventions from across different communities. 
• 
A developmental approach. This approach allows the partners to institutionalize processes and 
build ongoing capacity for evaluation by implementing recommendations as they are developed. 
Throughout our evaluation projects, RDA identifies opportunities to add value by building 
protocols, tools, and expertise within the client organization. These strategies foster streamlined 
and effective evaluations that meet clients’ needs while identifying opportunities to improve 
program elements.

Scope of Work 
RDA’s proposed evaluation of the Daly City, San Bruno, and San Mateo PD’s ORT Prevention Grant is 
structured in four phases: 1) Project Launch & Evaluation Planning, 2) Data Collection & Analysis, 3) 
Reporting, and 4) Project Management & Communication. Throughout this proposal, RDA has offered 
potential considerations for partners that may differ from the original Narrative Proposal submitted to 
BSCC to strengthen the evaluation and the assessment of causation.  
Phase 1: Project Launch & Evaluation Planning  
The Project Launch & Evaluation Planning phase, completed in months 1-4, lays the foundation for a 
successful evaluation, and serves both administrative and substantive purposes. Administratively, these 
activities support the development of a final work plan with agreed upon deliverables, communication 
procedures, and administrative practices throughout the course of the project. Substantively, this time 
will allow RDA to build relationships with the grant team that is comprised of the Grant Administrator, the 
Project Director, and any other personnel they see fit; confirm the vision and goals for the project; identify 
data sources; review existing evaluation materials to review to inform the evaluation design phase; and 
develop a thorough understanding of the community and stakeholder context. RDA and the Daly City 
Police Department will tailor needs as expectations are identified by the BSCC throughout the evaluation 
period or term of the grant. This work will culminate in a comprehensive Local Evaluation Plan (LEP). This 
phase will include the following activities: 
• Project Launch 
The in-person project launch meeting with RDA and the grant team will serve to kick off the 
project and confirm a shared understanding of the project goals, roles and responsibilities, 
timeline, activities, commitments, and deliverables, as well as to plan for future project activities. 
In this meeting, RDA will also work with the team to identify background documentation for 
review and key personnel to interview. The agenda for this meeting will achieve the following 
objectives: 
 
1. Confirm project goals, objectives, scope, and timeline; 
2. Highlight key priorities, goals, and challenges of the evaluation; 
3. Discuss outcomes of interest in addition to the five outlined in the Request for Services 
(RFS); 
4. Discuss expectations for communication and roles and responsibilities; and 
5. Review the availability of and steps needed to access the data and documents that RDA 
will collect and analyze. 
• Document Review 
RDA will conduct a careful review of background documents, as identified through the project 
launch meeting, to inform our understanding of the local jurisdictional context and trends in ORT, 
MVT, and MVAT, as well as associated intervention gaps and needs. These may include (but are 
not limited to) the Comprehensive Needs Assessment that was completed prior to the ORT 
Prevention Grant award, any relevant pre-existing partner annual, quarterly, or monthly reports 
or dashboards, as well as publicly available reports or media publications.

• Key Informant Interviews 
RDA will conduct up to four (4) key personnel interviews (either individual or small group 
interviews) as identified in the launch meeting. These will include representatives from the Daly 
City PD, San Bruno PD, and San Mateo PD. We will utilize these interviews to establish a strong 
foundational understanding of stakeholder priorities for the evaluation, as well as to gather initial 
input on jurisdictional context, challenges, and crime trends across Daly City, San Bruno, and San 
Mateo pertaining to ORT, MVT, and MVAT. RDA will produce an accessible graphic visualization 
of any differences in current and planned programs across the three partner entities, as well as 
nuances in objectives and the corresponding strategies to address these. This summary will inform 
the LEP and will guide evaluation activities. RDA will also use these interviews to identify any 
additional relevant background documentation for review. 
• Local Evaluation Plan 
RDA will collaborate with DCPD and partners in the first four months to develop a comprehensive 
LEP that aligns with BSCC requirements. This LEP will serve as a roadmap for the evaluation 
processes, and will include background information about the project, project goals and 
evaluation questions, the evaluation methodology and design, and a project logic model. RDA 
proposes two evaluation plan working meetings to gather necessary information to review and 
revise the evaluation plan. RDA can facilitate creating or revising a logic model for the program if 
the program has not already completed this component. A Draft LEP will be provided with 
sufficient time for review prior to finalization and will be completed in time for submission to the 
BSCC by the deadline of April 1, 2024. 
Consideration: Given the variety of proposed prevention strategies outlined in the BSCC 
proposal, RDA recommends considering a tiered implementation of intervention 
strategies so that the evaluator can attempt to isolate the individual impacts of certain 
strategies.  
Phase 2: Data Collection & Analysis 
The evaluation activities identified in this phase of the project will begin in Year 1 and will continue 
through Year 4. RDA will work with partners to collect quantitative data, complete a spatial analysis each 
month, and complete two site visits each year to collect qualitative data from partner agencies and 
community members directly impacted by the interventions employed in their communities. This phase 
will include the following activities: 
• Baseline Data Review (Year 1) 
In the first year of the project, RDA will review each partner agency’s previous crime data to 
establish a baseline.  
Consideration: RDA recommends provision of at least three (3) years of data to provide 
a sufficient baseline for subsequent evaluative comparison.  
• Qualitative Data Collection Tool Development (Year 1) 
• RDA will develop a series of tools for qualitative data collection, to include focus groups, 
interviews, or surveys as needed and site visits, to inform a nuanced understanding of local 
stakeholder perspectives. Areas of inquiry may include but are not limited to perceptions and 
experiences related to local ORT, MVT, and MVAT offenses, needs, and interventions employed 
as part of BSCC grant funding. In collaboration with the ORT grant team, RDA will identify 
appropriate stakeholder groups to participate in focus groups and/or surveys, such as retail store 
employees and/or patrons, officers and/or CSOs, and other community members. RDA agrees to

In Year 1, RDA will complete one (1) on-site visit, and in Years 2 and 3, RDA will complete two (2)

voluntary interviews and/or focus groups with personnel from partner agencies, retail employees

facilitate in-person meetings with the ORT grant team. Funding allocated to site visits will include 
all travel expenses for 2-3 RDA staff members per trip. The budget also includes gift card 
incentives for individuals to participate in focus groups and interviews. Gift cards for locally 
available companies will be provided to individuals who should be compensated for their time 
(e.g., local residents and store employees).  Pre-approval from BSCC shall be obtained prior to 
purchasing gift cards. 
• Qualitative Data Analysis (Years 1-3) 
In Years 1-3, RDA will analyze qualitative data from focus groups and interviews conducted during 
site visits. For the analysis of focus group and interview data, RDA will review and thematically 
code the narratives to systematically identify recurring themes and key takeaways using NVivo 
software. 
• Review of C3 Datasets (Years 1-4) 
On a quarterly basis, RDA will review de-identified C3 output data provided by agencies, or, if 
possible, work directly with a partner staff member to review the crime trends and track progress 
made toward the goals and objectives identified in the LEP. Additionally, this will provide RDA 
with an opportunity to discuss recommendations and modifications needed to ensure the 
evaluation is responsive to the project.  
• Spatial Analysis (Years 1-4)  
On a monthly basis, RDA will use ArcGIS software to perform spatial analysis of data provided by 
the three partnering police departments. This will include the mapping locations targeted for 
intervention, and geocoding of all ORT, MVT, and MVAT crime incidents. Based on the nature and 
quality of the data obtained, RDA will use advanced multivariate analytical techniques to examine 
any spatial relationships between intervention locations and crime incidents. 
Phase 3: Reporting 
RDA will use data collected as part of Phase 2 to develop a series of reports as requested by DCPD and as 
required by BSCC for ORT funding. 
• Quarterly Goals and Objectives Feedback (Years 1-4) 
Once every quarter, in the month prior to the required BSCC reporting, RDA will provide a 
summary of the progress made toward the goals and objectives of the project. This summary will 
include the analysis completed using the C3ai data review and a summary of the spatial analyses, 
as well as crime maps, completed in the interim months between quarterly reports. RDA will work 
with the grant team to identify those results that merit sharing out with key stakeholders.
