SECTION G: Additional Narrative 
 
1. Additional Narrative. Please provide any additional information necessary to detail the project’s 
progress during the reporting period. Any additional information that is project specific, which may help 
inform project progress, may be included here. If providing additional details in reference to a section 
within this report, please cite relevant sections.  
 
Description: On Metrics for ORT and MVT/MVAT: There currently exists a challenge in the literature 
regarding the most appropriate way of measuring ORT at any point prior to the prosecutorial decision 
to pursue a case with an ORT related charge. Due to this conceptual challenge and faced with the 
need to calculate short-term statistics that may be collected at the moment of occurrence or shortly 
thereafter, we interpret ORT and MVT/MVAT aggregate figures as potential incidents/arrests/referrals 
related to ORT and MVT/MVAT. An alternative interpretation could also be ORT-adjacent or 
MVT/MVAT adjacent events. Incidents were calculated using CAD calls with call types that were 
related to the category in question. Any incidents that were communicated to the police departments 
through alternative avenues are not included in these estimates. In order to most appropriately count 
cases and arrests related to ORT, we implemented textual analysis that interpreted in case/arrest 
charge description fields terms closely associated with retail theft, grand/petty theft, and shoplifting. 
Additionally, we cross-checked these numbers with the specific charge numbers associated with those 
calls. Similarly, for MVT/MVAT, we identified the case/arrest charge descriptions that were related to 
vehicle theft, vehicle accessory theft, and tampering with vehicles. Throughout this reporting process 
and hereafter, we will continue to analyze the methodology used to count these events for reliability 
and consistency. Acknowledging the challenges mentioned above, we include below some additional 
numbers that refer to the specific number of arrests and referrals made with an Organized Retail Theft 
charge: Incidents (10), Arrests (15) and DA referrals (15). Incidents for ORT-specific numbers were 
calculated using the total number of cases with offenses specifically related to ORT and are not 
calculated using CAD incoming calls.  Full-time FTE times are calculated based on total on-scene time 
for each ORT adjacent or MVT/MVAT incident plus a one-hour estimation on cases that resulted in DA 
referrals (referrals include arrests) per case due to additional time needed for staff to complete 
required documentation and investigative steps for cases that result in an arrest or referral.

Description: On Metrics for ORT and MVT/MVAT: There currently exists a challenge in the literature 
regarding the most appropriate way of measuring ORT at any point prior to the prosecutorial decision 
to pursue a case with an ORT related charge. Due to this conceptual challenge and faced with the 
need to calculate short-term statistics that may be collected at the moment of occurrence or shortly 
thereafter, we interpret ORT and MVT/MVAT aggregate figures as potential incidents/arrests/referrals 
related to ORT and MVT/MVAT. An alternative interpretation could also be ORT-adjacent or 
MVT/MVAT adjacent events. Incidents were calculated using CAD calls with call types that were 
related to the category in question. Any incidents that were communicated to the police departments 
through alternative avenues are not included in these estimates. In order to most appropriately count 
cases and arrests related to ORT, we implemented textual analysis that interpreted in case/arrest 
charge description fields terms closely associated with retail theft, grand/petty theft, and shoplifting. 
Additionally, we cross-checked these numbers with the specific charge numbers associated with those 
calls. Similarly, for MVT/MVAT, we identified the case/arrest charge descriptions that were related to 
vehicle theft, vehicle accessory theft, and tampering with vehicles. Throughout this reporting process 
and hereafter, we will continue to analyze the methodology used to count these events for reliability 
and consistency. Acknowledging the challenges mentioned above, we include below some additional 
numbers that refer to the specific number of arrests and referrals made with an Organized Retail Theft 
charge: Cases (9), Arrests (19) and DA referrals (9). Incidents for ORT-specific numbers were 
calculated using the total number of cases with offenses specifically related to ORT and are not 
calculated using CAD incoming calls.

1. Challenges: Describe the challenges encountered during the reporting period, as well any as efforts to 
overcome the challenges.  
Description: DCPD is still experiencing delays with ALPR camera installations due to supply chain 
issues, installer availability, and permitting challenges. The agency is working closely with the vendor 
to attempt to get the remaining cameras installed in the coming quarter. 
 
One challenge SBPD is facing is the ongoing changes to CIBRS, which affect what laws can be 
charged or reported. In Q5, the agency continued to collaborate with the DA’s Office and internal 
supervisors to ensure that officers receive proper training. 
 
SMPD collaborated with the Hillsdale Mall ownership group to finalize preparations for a police 
substation and to make it accessible to the public. One challenge encountered was securing an 
internet connection. The mall ownership was very helpful in coordinating with AT&T to finalize this 
necessity, allowing SMPD staff to access the department’s network and complete reports at the

substation. 
 
In the previous quarter, officers from all three agencies worked on a two-day deployment schedule, 
which was not ideal for maintaining consistency and coordination among the task force group. 
However, in Q5, partners returned to a four-day schedule that facilitated greater consistency and 
improved workflow among the agencies and retail partners. Officers reported a preference for the four-
day schedule. 
 
2. Project Highlights. Describe any highlights and/or accomplishments during the current reporting 
period.  
 
Description: During the holiday shopping season, DCPD and SMPD staffed details at major shopping 
malls with their jurisdictions. 
 
SMPD opened the substation at the Hillsdale Mall in time for Thanksgiving and the holiday season. 
This substation, staffed part-time by Community Service Officers, served as a space for officers to 
visit, complete reports, and conduct high-visibility patrols, etc. The substation received a number of 
visitors, from mall patrons as well as mall tenants, and received positive feedback. Additionally, SMPD 
staffed a detail using department funds, for two officers to conduct high-visibility patrols at Hillsdale 
Mall during the holiday shopping season. On the days that the detail was staffed, officers were busy 
responding to calls and making theft-related arrests. 
 
Moreover, RDA Consulting conducted – and SMPD helped coordinated - focus groups during this 
reporting period. Participants included members of the ORT task force, loss prevention staff, and 
community members from the three jurisdictions. Overall, while participants observed a decline in 
ORT, MVT, and MVAT incidents, these remain an issue. A positive outcome of the ORT grant 
collaboration was the strengthened partnerships among the three jurisdictions, the DA’s Office, and 
retailers. Participants highlighted the increased usage and enhancement of technology, along with 
increased outreach and education for retailers, loss prevention, and the public as opportunities for 
improvement. 
 
The ongoing efforts of the three agencies to communicate and maintain strong relationships with 
retailers and their loss prevention staff have led to success with arrests of prolific ORT suspects and 
crews. Since the beginning of the grant, our multi-agency ORT Task Force had successful 
deployments that led to approximately 229 arrests for ORT-adjacent offenses, and 199 case referrals 
to the DA’s Office due to some cases involving multiple arrests. Overall, shopping centers and retailer 
partners within our jurisdictions have welcomed our ORT Task Force, and some have participated in 
our zero-theft tolerance days, strengthening our collaborative efforts to combat ORT.

charge description fields terms closely associated with retail theft, grand/petty theft, and shoplifting.

Description: On Metrics for ORT and MVT/MVAT: There currently exists a challenge in the literature 
regarding the most appropriate way of measuring ORT at any point prior to the prosecutorial decision 
to pursue a case with an ORT related charge. Due to this conceptual challenge and faced with the 
need to calculate short-term statistics that may be collected at the moment of occurrence or shortly 
thereafter, we interpret ORT and MVT/MVAT aggregate figures as potential incidents/arrests/referrals 
related to ORT and MVT/MVAT. An alternative interpretation could also be ORT-adjacent or 
MVT/MVAT adjacent events. Incidents were calculated using CAD calls with call types that were 
related to the category in question. Any incidents that were communicated to the police departments 
through alternative avenues are not included in these estimates. In order to most appropriately count 
cases and arrests related to ORT, we implemented textual analysis that interpreted in case/arrest 
charge description fields terms closely associated with retail theft, grand/petty theft, and shoplifting. 
Additionally, we cross-checked these numbers with the specific charge numbers associated with those 
calls. Similarly, for MVT/MVAT, we identified the case/arrest charge descriptions that were related to 
vehicle theft, vehicle accessory theft, and tampering with vehicles. Throughout this reporting process 
and hereafter, we will continue to analyze the methodology used to count these events for reliability 
and consistency. Acknowledging the challenges mentioned above, we include below some additional 
numbers that refer to the specific number of arrests and referrals made with an Organized Retail Theft 
charge: 16 Cases, 15 Arrests, and 12 DA referrals. Additionally, there were 43 prior theft cases 
with arrests and 1 prior motor vehicle theft cases with arrests in this reporting period. Incidents for 
ORT-specific numbers were calculated using the total number of cases with offenses specifically 
related to ORT and are not calculated using CAD incoming calls. Regarding property recovery, please 
note that the estimate provided for ORT reflects the total value of properties recovered by all three 
partner agencies during grant-funded ORT task force deployments. For MVT/MVAT, the estimate 
includes vehicles recovered from both grant-funded and non-grant-funded ALPRs.

SECTION G: Additional Narrative 
 
1. Additional Narrative. Please provide any additional information necessary to detail the project’s 
progress during the reporting period. Any additional information that is project specific, which may help 
inform project progress, may be included here. If providing additional details in reference to a section 
within this report, please cite relevant sections.  
 
 
Description: On Metrics for ORT and MVT/MVAT: There currently exists a challenge in the literature 
regarding the most appropriate way of measuring ORT at any point prior to the prosecutorial decision 
to pursue a case with an ORT related charge. Due to this conceptual challenge and faced with the 
need to calculate short-term statistics that may be collected at the moment of occurrence or shortly 
thereafter, we interpret ORT and MVT/MVAT aggregate figures as potential incidents/arrests/referrals 
related to ORT and MVT/MVAT. An alternative interpretation could also be ORT-adjacent or 
MVT/MVAT adjacent events. Incidents were calculated using CAD calls with call types that were 
related to the category in question. Any incidents that were communicated to the police departments 
through alternative avenues are not included in these estimates. In order to most appropriately count 
cases and arrests related to ORT, we implemented textual analysis that interpreted in case/arrest 
charge description fields terms closely associated with retail theft, grand/petty theft, and shoplifting. 
Additionally, we cross-checked these numbers with the specific charge numbers associated with those 
calls. Similarly, for MVT/MVAT, we identified the case/arrest charge descriptions that were related to 
vehicle theft, vehicle accessory theft, and tampering with vehicles. Throughout this reporting process 
and hereafter, we will continue to analyze the methodology used to count these events for reliability 
and consistency. Acknowledging the challenges mentioned above, we include below some additional 
numbers that refer to the specific number of arrests and referrals made with an Organized Retail Theft 
charge: 10 Cases, 13 Arrests, and 6 DA referrals. Additionally, there were 38 prior theft cases with 
arrests and 1 prior motor vehicle theft case with arrest in this reporting period. Incidents for ORT-
specific numbers were calculated using the total number of cases with offenses specifically related to 
ORT and are not calculated using CAD incoming calls. Regarding property recovery, please note that 
the estimate provided for ORT reflects the total value of properties recovered by all three partner 
agencies during grant-funded ORT task force deployments. For MVT/MVAT, the estimate includes 
vehicles recovered from both grant-funded and non-grant-funded ALPRs.
