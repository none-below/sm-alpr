ITEM DESCRIPTION: Purchase of Axon Draft One AI-Assisted Report Writing Software

Proposed Action
The Cloquet Police Department requests authorization to purchase Axon Draft One AJI-assisted report writing
software at a total cost of $64,938.06 over a 34-month contract period (approximately $21,646.02 annually).

Background/Overview

Report writing is one of the most time-consuming aspects of modern policing. Officers frequently spend hours
documenting incidents, resulting in significant overtime costs and delays in report completion. The Cloquet Police
Department has been piloting Axon's Draft One software for the past two months to evaluate whether artificial
intelligence-assisted report writing could improve efficiency, accuracy, and thoroughness while reducing costs.
Draft One is an Al-powered tool that helps officers generate comprehensive police reports from body-worn
camera audio, interview recordings, and other documentation. The system incorporates multiple accuracy
checkpoints and safeguards to ensure reports meet professional standards while maintaining officer control over
the final product.

Discussion

The use of AI to assist with report generation is becoming increasingly common in law enforcement. Our
colleagues at the Duluth Police Department in our Sixth Judicial District have implemented similar technology
with positive results. Additionally, the Carlton County Sheriff's Office is evaluating AI solutions to improve
report writing, signaling a regional trend toward this technology. During our two-month trial period, Draft One
has demonstrated significant benefits:

TIME SAVINGS AND COST REDUCTION:

Officers have reported substantial time savings, particularly on complex cases. One patrol officer noted:
"Personally found Draft One useful. It has saved me time and saved the department from paying me overtime.
After taking a burglary report, I returned to the office around 5:00 p.m. and had a lengthy report to write. Draft
One completed the report in great detail, as good as, or better than, my report would have been. What would have
likely been a couple of hours of overtime ended up being me leaving on time, thanks to Draft One."

The same officer added, "As I have used it more, I have gotten better at articulating the information I want to
include when writing the report, but it might not otherwise be captured. I have also learned how to add additional

322 Policy Manual

Report Preparation

322.1 PURPOSE AND SCOPE

Report preparation is a major part of each employee's job. The purpose of reports is to document
sufficient information to refresh the employee’s memory and to provide sufficient information for
follow-up investigation and successful prosecution. Report writing is the subject of substantial
formalized and on-the-job training.

322.1.1 REPORT PREPARATION

It is the responsibility of the assigned employee to complete and submit all reports taken during the
shift before going off-duty unless permission to hold the report has been approved by a supervisor.
Employees should ensure that their reports are sufficient for their purpose and reasonably free of
errors before submission. Generally, reports requiring prompt follow-up action on active leads or
arrest reports where the suspect remains in custody should not be held.

Officers must prepare handwritten forms and reports legibly. If the form or report is not legible, the
submitting employee will be required by the reviewing supervisor to make corrections and resubmit
the form or report promptly. Employees who dictate reports shall use appropriate grammar.
Employees who generate forms or reports on computers are subject to all requirements of this
policy.

All forms and reports shall accurately reflect the persons' identity, all pertinent information seen,
heard, or assimilated by any other sense, and any actions taken. Employees shall not suppress,
conceal or distort the facts of any reported incident, nor shall any employee make a false report
orally or in writing. Generally, the reporting employee’s opinions should not be included in reports
unless specifically identified as such.

322.2 REQUIRED REPORTING
Written reports are required in all of the following situations on the appropriate Department-
approved form unless otherwise approved by a supervisor.

322.2.1 CRIMINAL ACTIVITY

When a member responds to a call for service or, as a result of self-initiated activity, becomes
aware of any activity where a crime has occurred, the member shall document the incident
regardless of whether a victim desires prosecution.

§ 626.5531)

322.2.4 INJURY OR DAMAGE BY CITY PERSONNEL
Reports shall be taken if an injury occurs that is a result of an act of a City employee. Additionally,
reports shall be taken involving damage to City property or City equipment.

322.2.5 MISCELLANEOUS INJURIES

Any injury that is reported to this department shall require a report when:
(a) The injury is a result of a drug overdose.
(b) Attempted suicide.
(c) The injury is major or serious, whereas death could result.

(d) The circumstances surrounding the incident are suspicious in nature and it is desirable
to record the event.

The above reporting requirements are not intended to be all-inclusive. A supervisor may direct an
employee to document any incident he/she deems necessary.

Reports that may be submitted by the public via online or other self-completed reporting processes
include:

(a) Lost property.

(b) Misdemeanor thefts of property, other than firearms or materials that threaten public

322.3 GENERAL POLICY OF EXPEDITIOUS REPORTING

in general, all employees and supervisors shall act with promptness and efficiency in the
preparation and processing of all reports. An incomplete report, unorganized reports or reports
delayed without supervisory approval are not acceptable. Reports shall be processed according to
established priorities or according to special priority necessary under exceptional circumstances.

322.3.1 GENERAL POLICY OF HANDWRITTEN REPORTS

Some incidents and report forms lend themselves to block print rather than typing. In general,
the narrative portion of those reports where an arrest is made or when there is a long narrative
should be typed or dictated.

Supervisors may require, with the foregoing general policy in mind, block printing or typing of
reports of any nature for Department consistency.

322.3.2 GENERAL USE OF OTHER HANDWRITTEN FORMS
County, state and federal agency forms may be block printed as appropriate. In general, the form
itself may make the requirement for typing apparent.

322.4 REPORT CORRECTIONS

Supervisors shall review reports for content and accuracy. If a correction is necessary, the
reviewing supervisor should reject (kick-back) the report in CentralSquare with the reasons for
rejection. The original report and rejection notification should be conducted as soon as practicable.
It shall be the responsibility of the originating employee to ensure that any report returned for
correction is processed promptly.

322.5 REPORT CHANGES OR ALTERATIONS

Reports that have been approved by a supervisor and submitted to the Records Center for filing
and distribution shall not be modified or altered except by way of a supplemental report. Reviewed
reports that have not yet been submitted to the Records Center may be corrected or modified by
the authoring employee only with the knowledge and authorization of the reviewing supervisor.

322.6 ELECTRONIC SIGNATURES

The Cloquet Police Department has established an electronic signature procedure for use by
all employees of the Cloquet Police Department. The Patrol Division Commander shall be
responsible for maintaining the electronic signature system, for ensuring that each employee
creates a unique, confidential password for his/her electronic signature and that the use of
electronic signatures otherwise complies with the law.

. Employees may only use their electronic signature for official reports or other official
communications.

. Each employee shall be responsible for the security and use of his/her electronic
signature and shall promptly notify a supervisor if the electronic signature has or may
have been compromised or misused.

304 Procedures Manual

Report Expectations

304.1 DOCUMENTING INCIDENTS

The Cloquet Police Department expects officers to properly document incidents reported to
the department. The police report serves as an essential document for legal proceedings,
investigations, and departmental records. It should provide a clear, complete, and accurate
account of the incident, enabling anyone who reads it to understand what occurred and the actions
taken in response.

There are instances when a report needs to be comprehensive, and other times when a more
condensed version is sufficient.

A comprehensive report includes:

. A detailed accounting of the officer’s actions and observations

. A detailed account of victim/witness/suspect statements

. A detailed accounting of the scene and any evidence related to the incident
A comprehensive report should be:

* Accurate — all details should be correct and verified, witness/victim/suspect statements
are accurately detailed

. Clear — easily understandable, well-organized, concise, and free of ambiguity

. Objective — factual and unbiased reporting

* Complete — elements of the crime and all relevant information should be included,
leaving no significant gaps

. Professional ~ well organized, neatly formatted, and free from spelling or grammatical
errors

CPD expects officers to complete comprehensive police reports on the following:

. All felonies

. In-custody cases

. Cases where a citation was issued - excluding traffic citations

. Person crimes

. Missing person incidents

. Crimes motivated by hate and/or bias

. Deceased person

. Cases that meet the solvability threshold for follow-up investigation — 5 or more points

. Extreme Risk Protection Orders (ERPO)

. Weapon violation incidents
