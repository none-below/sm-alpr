Item Background
In March of 2023, City Council approved a pilot program for automated license plate readers through
Flock Safety. This pilot program is now complete and based on Council and community feedback staff
is not recommending we renew the agreement with Flock Safety, citing privacy concerns and failures
by to the vendor to protect images stored on the city's ALPR network from unauthorized agencies. 
The police department has updated the ALPR policy to require a reasonable cause threshold for an
investigator to access the network. The police department will also increase the frequency of network
(external) and organizational (internal) audits to quarterly. 
Though the City had concerns with the vendor, the police department was very successful using ALPR
technology to solve serious crimes in our community, including murders, attempted murder,
robberies, and home invasions. Due to the success of this program, the Chief of Police recommends
continued use of ALPR technology. 
The police department has vetted four ALPR providers (Axon, Genetec, Rekor, Motorola). After the
vetting process, the police department recommends contracting ALPR technology from Axon. Axon
does not have a national look-up feature. Axon's ALPR data sharing software only operates on a "one-
to-one" basis. Based on the data collected from the pilot program. 
The police department recommends installing cameras at the following locations: northbound and
southbound Woodward, eastbound and westbound Eight Mile Road, and eastbound and westbound
Nine Mile Road. The agreement also includes installation, service and maintenance for nine cameras,
