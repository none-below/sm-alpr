MEETING DATE: June 02, 2026  
FROM: Chief David Maas, Police Department 
RE: Consideration of agreement for services between Axon and the Town of Grand Chute for 
the installation and operation of 23 fixed-point ALPR cameras 
TYPE: New Business 
Background Information: For about the past decade, the police department has leveraged 
automated license plate recognition (ALPR) solutions within the Town. The early version of this 
technology deployed rooftop mounted cameras on our patrol vehicles to photograph the license 
plates of vehicles approaching and receding from our squad cars. The data from these cameras 
was transmitted through the mobile data computer (MDC) before being transferred to the Town’s 
server for storage.  
In February of 2023, the Town of Grand Chute entered into an agreement with Flock for the 
installation and servicing of 20 fixed-point ALPR cameras within the Town. These cameras 
collected data at the highly travelled entry points to the Town, providing real-time alerting of 
vehicles that were wanted by law enforcement entering our jurisdiction. Following the 
installation of these cameras, the department began observing an immediate impact on our ability 
to solve cases quickly and efficiently. Fixed-point ALPR technology was shown to provide 
incredible value across the wide variety of circumstances encountered by law enforcement. This 
technology has helped our agency recover stolen vehicles, provided vital leads in violent crime 
cases, provided timely notifications in missing persons cases, and served to aid in many other 
legitimate law enforcement purposes. It is widely held that ALPR technology has revolutionized 
law enforcement from both an efficiency perspective and a crime clearance perspective. 
In early 2024, the department entered into an agreement with Axon for mobile ALPR 
technology, leveraging the existing Axon fleet cameras installed in our squad cars. This 
agreement enabled the collection of ALPR data in a similar manner to the legacy technology, but 
did so using the front-facing squad camera rather than roof-mounted auxiliary cameras. This 
change reduced the complexity of upfitting for our police vehicles, centralized the data 
collection, and expanded the number of vehicles collecting ALPR data. 
As we moved through 2024, an integration was enabled that allowed Axon ALPR data to be 
viewed within the Flock ALPR database. This provided significant benefit to our team, as they 
were able to see all ALPR data in one location. Following a change in course for the companies, 
this integration was ultimately severed and Axon moved to enter the fixed-point ALPR market as 
a competitor to Flock. 
The Town has maintained an agreement with Flock for fixed-point ALPR technology since early 
2023. In a companion agenda memorandum, you will read about some concerns regarding the 
vendor providing this technology. What must be clarified, however, is that the technology itself 
is vital to our continued pursuit of providing exceptional service to our community. I can provide 
many examples of ways in which our department has used this technology to advance our

mission of public safety. I can also tell you that we have never been made aware of an instance in 
which our officers have used this data inappropriately. Our department has a strict policy on 
ALPR technology and regularly conducts audits to ensure compliance. Given that we have nearly 
a decade of experience with ALPR, I believe our track record is important. 
 
My concerns with Flock’s fixed-point ALPR solution have nothing to do with the technology 
itself, but rather the manner in which it is deployed and controlled by the vendor. This is a 
sentiment echoed by leaders across the State. Recognizing these factors, Axon has developed a 
solution that gives ultimate control to the agency, rather than the vendor. This business model 
allows the agency to make decisions related to implementation and ensure the technology is used 
appropriately and within compliance of local policy. 
 
The department has been an Axon customer since March of 2004. For the past 22 years, we have 
seen Axon develop and grow a variety of products to improve and support law enforcement. In 
fact, nearly everyone you talk to has an understanding of Axon’s flagship product, Taser. In 
addition to obtaining Taser products from Axon, we also use Axon solutions for our body 
cameras, squad cameras, and digital evidence management. Axon’s digital evidence solution 
(Evidence.com) houses critical evidence such as body camera video, squad camera video, Taser 
firing logs, and ALPR data. This system also provides capability to ingest digital evidence 
provided by citizens and community partners through their “community request” feature. Finally, 
the digital evidence solution also enables seamless sharing of evidence with prosecutors through 
a secure portal with the District Attorney’s Office. This system is fully compliant with the FBI’s 
CJIS Security Policy, Department of Defense IL4, FedRamp High, SOC 2, HIPAA, and many 
other security standards. Our longstanding relationship with Axon has been built on a foundation 
of trust, and we have come to expect exceptional service from them. 
 
Every Axon product is built under a “Responsible Innovation” framework developed with input 
from ethicists, civil rights advocates, and community leaders. Axon has truly taken a very 
thoughtful approach to the development of their fixed-point ALPR technology. Below are a 
number of important distinctions of their solution: 
 
• The department owns all ALPR data and hardware. Axon does not pool, resell, or 
repurpose the agency data. Axon’s access is limited to rare support cases, only when 
explicitly authorized by the agency. 
• The department controls the retention period for data and data is automatically purged on 
schedule. 
• Data sharing is invite-only, visible, customizable, and revocable. Axon does not operate a 
national pool or enable automatic sharing between agencies. 
• Axon platforms maintain immutable audit logs to determine who accessed data, when, 
and why. Every query creates a permanent, reviewable record.  
• Axon does not use facial recognition and does not track individuals. 
• Axon never discloses data or responds to legal requests on the agency’s behalf.

• ALPR data is never sold to commercial entities, data brokers, or third parties. 
 
In addition to the above information, additional information on device security, ecosystem 
security, and data governance from Axon is attached. If you would like to research Axon’s 
commitments to, and implementations of, security, privacy, compliance, transparency, and data 
protection, you can view them at http://trust.axon.com.   
 
Attorney Rossmeissl has reviewed the proposed Axon agreement and no changes are proposed at 
this time. 
 
Fiscal Impact: As a result of extensive negotiation, Axon has agreed to terms that would match 
the rate the Town of Grand Chute had been paying under the 2023 Flock agreement. In addition, 
they have agreed to absorb the early termination fees ($11,500) from Flock incurred by the 
Town, shown as a discount in the first year of the agreement. Finally, they have also agreed to 
extend accident and vandalism coverage to all of the quoted cameras for the duration of the 5-
year contract period. In total, more than $75,000 in discounts were applied to this proposed 
agreement. Below is a summation of the proposed fee schedule for the agreement with Axon:

The police department had budgeted $52,500 for Flock ALPR in 2026. A donation of $20,000 
was received to support 3 ALPR cameras for a period of two years, beginning in 2026. The 
department previously expended half of the donation amount on the ALPR expansion project in 
2026 and earmarked the other half to support costs in 2027.  
 
The refund from the terminated Flock agreement is estimated at $43,883 and includes a portion 
of the recoverable donation funds. The remaining donation funds total approximately $10,000. 
Therefore, the funding for this proposed agreement would be covered by a combination of the 
refund from Flock and the remaining donation funds. The Town would then bear the full expense 
in subsequent years of the agreement.
