MEETING DATE: June 02, 2026  
FROM: Chief David Maas, Police Department 
RE: Consideration of early termination of the agreements for services between Flock Safety and 
the Town of Grand Chute  
 
TYPE: New Business 
Background Information: In February of 2023, the Town of Grand Chute entered into an 
agreement with Flock Safety for the installation and servicing of 20 fixed-point ALPR cameras 
within the Town. In June of 2024, the Town added a movable ALPR camera through a 
partnership with the Outagamie County Sheriff’s Department. In November of 2025, the Town 
expanded the network through the addition of 3 new fixed-point cameras. Since that time, the 
department has opted not to renew the June 2024 agreement and returned the movable ALPR 
camera to Flock. Below is a summation of our agreements with Flock:

In February 2025, we identified abnormal system downtime and initiated support tickets for 18 
of our 20 cameras. Downtime issues were determined to have begun as early as January 17, 2025 
and had not been addressed by Flock. Six of the cameras were scheduled for replacement, which 
did not occur within the timeframe established by the vendor. In May of 2025, we requested and 
later received a statement credit from Flock to compensate for the unacceptable downtime of the 
network.  
In April 2025, we were notified by WisDOT about an impending construction conflict with one 
of our Flock cameras. Flock was notified of this issue and provided information for the 
relocation of the camera. The camera was not relocated and we were notified by the contractor in 
July that it needed to be relocated immediately. The camera was removed by Flock over the July 
4th weekend, but was not reinstalled. During the subsequent months, we were provided a number 
of different reasons why the camera had not been reinstalled, including issues with permitting, 
utility locates, and supply chain issues. The responses appeared to regularly change depending 
upon the representative we spoke with. On October 29, 2025, this camera was finally reinstalled. 
We again requested, and received, a statement credit from Flock to compensate for the 
unacceptable downtime. 
On December 8, 2025, we identified abnormal system downtime and initiated support tickets for 
7 of our 20 cameras that were failing. Those issues were resolved approximately one month later. 
At the same time these service issues were plaguing our network, a public conversation was 
occurring about both the fixed-point ALPR technology and how Flock chose to structure their

services. In August 2025, news reporting revealed that federal immigration agents had direct 
access to police data through a pilot program, which allegedly contradicted nine months of 
denials. In other reporting, Flock CEO Garett Langley was quoted as addressing this issue with 
the following statements: “[T]here have been conflicting reports in the media about Flock's 
relationship with federal agencies, and some of our public statements inadvertently provided 
inaccurate information” and “We clearly communicated poorly. We also didn't create distinct 
permissions and protocols in the Flock system to ensure local compliance for federal agency 
users.” 
 
Around this same time, Flock made significant changes in how external agency searches 
appeared in our system audit logs. While we previously had visibility to each search of our data 
and the associated reason behind it, Flock changed their systems to make it far more difficult to 
audit and verify the appropriateness of external agency searches. This feedback was provided to 
Flock, yet they refused to reverse course. In response, we removed our agency from “National 
Lookup” and discontinued sharing relationships with every agency outside the State of 
Wisconsin. In addition, we have frequently demanded that Flock institute internal controls to 
restrict external agency searches to only those that comply with our internal policy. As of this 
time, they have still failed to take action on these concerns. This creates an environment where 
some external agency searches might not comply with our internal policy related to ALPR 
acceptable use. 
 
In October 2025, we began working with Axon to explore an alternative to Flock for fixed-point 
ALPR services. Our exploration of this solution has included a detailed analysis of the 
functionality of the system, along with an examination of Axon’s approach to the protection of 
personal privacy and data security. In addition, we have conducted our own test and evaluation 
of the equipment in order to ensure that the information provided by vendor representatives 
matched real-world performance. Over the past eight months, we have met extensively with 
Axon representatives to ensure that any transition was well-researched and provided significant 
benefit to our residents.  
 
Our exploration of Axon’s solution had been done in anticipation of a transition at the end of our 
current agreement with Flock (May 2027). However, recent events and ongoing concerns have 
caused us to reconsider that course of action. Therefore, we are now requesting consideration to 
terminate our current agreement with Flock.  
 
Over the past few months, concerns about Flock have been openly discussed in communities 
across Wisconsin. More locally, concerns were widely circulated prior to a proposed contract 
renewal in the City of Oshkosh. As the contract renewal was discussed, Flock representatives 
provided information which was later found to be inaccurate. This caused individuals to lose 
trust in Flock and, although the contract extension was initially approved in Oshkosh, the council 
ultimately reversed course and opted not to renew the contract. Flock’s response to the incident 
included a statement which read, in part: “It’s disappointing to hear that one small misconception

stated during a two-hour meeting, after many other meetings with Oshkosh’s leaders and Police

Following the reversal in the City of Oshkosh, Appleton Mayor Jake Woodford announced that 
the City of Appleton would cease their agreement with Flock. In his announcement, Mayor 
Woodford stated “[t]hat being said, concerns about the integrity of Flock’s underlying system 
have eroded our trust.” What is important to note is that both Mayor Woodford and some elected 
officials in Oshkosh have vocalized support for fixed-point ALPR technology, alongside their 
concerns about Flock. This has been a common sentiment I’ve heard from other leaders and 
members of the public. 
 
Our experience with Flock in 2025 included significant challenges associated with service of our 
equipment. Information provided by Flock representatives changed frequently and, at its best, 
could be described as inaccurate. After the August 2025 news reporting regarding federal 
immigration agents’ access to Flock data, Flock provided a statement that they had provided 
inaccurate information in a very public forum. Fast forward to the recent events in Oshkosh and 
we see, once again, inaccurate information coming from Flock. While Flock’s response to the 
Oshkosh events characterizes the issue as “one small misconception”, it seems there may be a 
pattern of inaccuracies, which causes significant concern. Fixed-point ALPR technology is 
powerful and must be used responsibly. We must be able to fully trust any vendor we do 
business with, especially one that provides such a powerful tool. The pattern of inaccuracies 
serves to erode trust, which cannot be ignored.  
 
Attorney Rossmeissl has reviewed the Flock agreement and the termination for convenience 
provisions therein.
