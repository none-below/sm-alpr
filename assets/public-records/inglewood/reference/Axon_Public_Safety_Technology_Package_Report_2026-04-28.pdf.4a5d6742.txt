BACKGROUND:

The City of Inglewood has made significant progress in advancing public safety, with recent data
demonstrating a continued decline in crime facilitated by strategic investments, proactive policing,
and strong community partnerships. The Police Department’s (Department) ongoing strategic
commitment to innovation, community engagement, and operational excellence has positioned the
City as a leader in modern public safety practices.

While a combination of technologies are currently in place, they operate independently of one
another and with limited synergy. Adopting a unified and scalable infrastructure will enhance
interoperability, improve operational effectiveness, and support long-term public safety and crime
reduction efforts. In Inglewood, Part I Crimes fell 13% in 2025 compared to 2024, led by a 60%
drop in Homicides and a 32% drop in Burglaries reported in our City. As of December 31, 2025,
compared to its peak in 1981, violent crime has fallen 76% and homicides have fallen 87%.

To sustain and build upon these historic Public Safety benchmarks, the Department proposes to
continue to strengthen its technological capacity through the implementation of updated, integrated
systems that will further enhance our public safety capabilities. These include automated license
plate readers, a comprehensive digital evidence management platform, augmented by Unmanned
Aircraft Systems, Body-Worn Cameras, and In-Car Video systems. This research has been

underway since August of 2025.

Additionally, in January 2026, the Chief of Police directed his staff to research the availability of

Because of these efforts, the City has been awarded a Fiscal Year 2026 Counter-Unmanned
Aircraft Systems (C-UAS) Grant through the California Governor’s Office of Emergency Services
(Cal OES). This grant supports enhanced detection, monitoring, mitigation and response
capabilities related to unmanned aerial systems and reflects the City’s continued commitment to
strengthening public safety through strategic funding opportunities.

As the City prepares to support major international and national events, including the FIFA World
Cup 2026, Super Bowl 2027, and the 2028 Summer Olympics and Paralympic Games, the demand
for enhanced public safety infrastructure will increase significantly. The City is expected to
experience a substantial influx of visitors associated with these events, requiring advanced and
integrated systems to ensure maximum public safety, operational readiness, and effective security
coordination.

This increase in activity across the region will place heightened demands on public safety
resources and coordination. Continued investment in an updated, integrated infrastructure reflects
the City’s strategic commitment to maintaining a safe community, enhancing real-time operational
capabilities, and ensuring readiness for both current and future public safety needs.

DISCUSSION:

The City has been awarded a Fiscal Year 2026 C-UAS Grant through the Cal OES. This grant will
support enhanced detection, monitoring, and response capabilities related to unmanned aerial
systems. Uses include radar detection, radio frequency (RF) detection, thermal imaging, command
and control systems, mitigation, and mobile response capabilities to address emerging drone-
related public safety risks. This grant-funded program will operate independently from the Officer
Safety Technology Package and further strengthens the City’s overall public safety readiness.

The proposed Officer Safety Technology Package represents a strategic investment in enhancing
the City’s public safety infrastructure and serves as a key component of the City’s broader effort
to leverage technology to upgraded policing operations. This initiative builds upon the City’s
existing public safety efforts by integrating advanced tools that support proactive, data-driven
policing and improved service delivery.

' Key components include a real-time crime center integration that will support coordinated, data-
driven decision-making throughout the Department, automated license plate readers to assist with
proactive identification of vehicles associated with criminal activity, a centralized digital evidence
enhancement system that will streamline evidence storage, sharing, and case management, body-
worn cameras with enhanced video capture capabilities to support accountability and evidence
collection; in-car video systems to document critical incidents from multiple perspectives, and
drone technology to provide real-time aerial situational awareness during critical incidents and
large-scale events.

Axon Enterprise, Inc. (Axon) is uniquely positioned to provide a fully integrated public safety
technology ecosystem that allows the City to implement a comprehensive solution within a single
platform. While other vendors may offer individual components, Axon delivers these systems in a
unified environment that enhances system compatibility, reduces administrative burden, and
improves operational efficiency. In addition, Axon provides comprehensive training, ongoing
technical support, and dedicated resources to ensure successful implementation and long-term
system effectiveness.

The proposed purchase is exempt from the City’s competitive bidding requirements, pursuant to
Inglewood Municipal Code section 2-198. 1(a)(5) (Cooperative Purchasing), which authorizes the
City to utilize contracts competitively solicited by other public agencies. The equipment and
services are anticipated to be procured through the OMNIA Partners cooperative purchasing
program, based on Contract No. 3544-21-4615 between Axon Enterprise, Inc. and the University
of Nebraska. Staff will ensure the final agreement is consistent with the pricing and terms available
under that cooperative contract.

The specific agreement with Axon Enterprise, Inc. is currently under development. Staffis seeking
City Council authorization to proceed with final negotiations and to execute the agreement upon
completion, consistent with the scope, pricing structure, and cooperative purchasing terms
described herein, and subject to City Attorney review and approval as to form.

FINANCIAL/FUNDING ISSUES AND SOURCES:

The Fiscal Year 2026 C-UAS Grant Agreement, in the amount of $2,782,208, will support a
separate public safety initiative and is not being used to fund the proposed Officer Safety
Technology Package. The City will have to front the expenditure and then be reimbursed upon
proof of implementation. New account codes will be established within the Public Safety Grants
Fund to receive and expend grant funds (Exhibit A).

The Mayor has negotiated a total estimated cost for Inglewood of the proposed Public Safety
Technology Package of $6,275,772, (a 27% discount from their normal and customary pricing)
distributed over a five-year period (average of $1,255,154 per year; however, the City has the
option to backload the payment schedule), in accordance with the pricing structure outlined in the
attached summary of the vendor quote (Attachment 3). The estimated equipment deployment cost
includes equipment, software, licensing, cloud storage, maintenance, training, and ongoing 24/7
support services (Attachment 4). The equipment package is currently supported by the General
Fund.

The cost structure reflects a phased, multi-year implementation model. The first year includes
initial deployment and training costs, while the second year reflects steady-state operations with
slightly lower costs. In subsequent years, costs increase as the program reaches full operational
capacity, including equipment refresh, software licensing, increased data storage, and ongoing
maintenance and support. This approach allows the City to distribute costs over time, while
ensuring long-term system reliability and performance.
