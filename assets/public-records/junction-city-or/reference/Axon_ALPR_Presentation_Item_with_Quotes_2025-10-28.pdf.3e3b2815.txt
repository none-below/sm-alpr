ISSUE STATEMENT 
AXON automated license plate recognition (ALPR) cameras will help JCPD capture vehicle 
data to support criminal investigations, improve public safety, and enhance coordination with 
regional agencies.  
 
BACKGROUND 
The Police Department has been looking at technology to help temporarily offset the need for 
more personnel. One encouraging emerging technology has been in License Plate Readers 
(LPR) or Advanced License Plate Readers (ALPR).  
 
Staff received a demonstration from AXON as they have the only real competitive product  
on the market and it is still in the testing phase. Axon products are called Outpost and Light 
Post and function almost exactly as the Flock LPR cameras with a few noticeable differences.   
• Axon cameras can be affixed to most of the existing streetlights.  
• They offer live view and playbacks for their cameras. 
• They capture both front and rear license plates. (Flock only captures the rear) 
• Axon video would be integrated into JCPD’s existing evidence.com.   
• Axon data currently is only shared with other Axon LPR agencies (Only OSP in Lane 
County) 
• Axon products will likely not be available until 2026. 
• The city owns the cameras with Axon, while Flock cameras are leased.  
 
At this time Staff is not asking for a decision to made between Flock and Axon. This 
presentation is for discussion and information purposes only at this time.

We must do something.
In 2022 in the U.S., 1,158 people — 1,096 civilians and 62 officers — lost their lives in gun-related incidents between 
police and the public, according to leading data sources. 
It won't be easy.
These losses reflect a complex set of issues, and the impact on communities is felt deeply and daily. We will need new 
ideas, new technologies, new training procedures, new policies, and even new regulations to guide the way.
Together, we can protect life.
We are calling on our own team members, leaders of the law enforcement profession, community organizations, 
technology companies, elected officials and others representing the best ideas to support and achieve this goal.
In short, we need a movement behind the mission to collectively develop realistic, research-based and responsible 
solutions to cut all gun-related deaths between police and the public in half by 2033.
