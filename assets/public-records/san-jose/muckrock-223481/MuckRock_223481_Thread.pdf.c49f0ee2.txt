Subject: California Public Records Act Request: SJPD Flock ALPR misuse matter (disclosed Sept.
11, 2026): investigation, audit-trail, and access-suspension records
To Whom It May Concern:
Pursuant to the California Public Records Act, I hereby request the following records:
On September 11, 2026, Chief Paul Joseph disclosed that the Department terminated an
officer who, while off duty, used a personal device to conduct unauthorized searches of
the Department's Flock Safety automated license plate reader (ALPR) system in connection
with a domestic violence matter involving family members. As reported, the Department has
said that the improper access was discovered in February 2025, that the officer was placed
on administrative leave and later terminated, that the matter was referred to the
Commission on Peace Officer Standards and Training, that a criminal case was submitted to
the District Attorney's office and no criminal charges were filed, and that audit logs
tracking access from the personal device provided the evidence. Below, "the Flock misuse
matter" refers to that matter.
Records requested:
1. Records of the Department's investigation of the Flock misuse matter and of the
discipline imposed, to the extent they are records described in Penal Code § 832.7(b)(1),
including any record relating to a sustained finding described in subparagraph (C) or (E)
of that paragraph, and including the categories of records listed in Penal Code § 832.7(b)
(3).
2. The ALPR access records the Department identified or relied on in the Flock misuse
matter as reflecting the unauthorized searches: the corresponding entries of the
Department's Flock organization audit, in native export form with every field the export
carries (including date and time, search type, networks searched, time frame, reason, and
case number), and any Flock record of the device, application, or access method used for
those searches (for example, mobile application or web browser). License plate numbers and
other civilian identifying information may be redacted. Civil Code § 1798.90.52 provides
that an ALPR operator that accesses ALPR information "shall do both of the following: (a)
Maintain a record of that access. At a minimum, the record shall include all of the
following: (1) The date and time the information is accessed. (2) The license plate number
or other data elements used to query the ALPR system. (3) The username of the person who
accesses the information . . . . (4) The purpose for accessing the information." Duty
Manual section L 4207 (2024) provides that "[a]udit trails shall be maintained by the
Department for a minimum of two (2) years." A response of "no responsive records" to this
item would mean that the Department no longer has the access records that, as reported,
provided the evidence in the matter.
3. Records reflecting the suspension or disabling of the officer's access to the Flock
ALPR system in the Flock misuse matter, including any request or authorization to disable
the account and the Flock user-management record of the change.

Thank you for the October 27 notice on request P010209-100826. I am writing to set out the
timeline and to ask for the determination.
The City received this request on October 8, 2026, as its acknowledgment states.
Government Code § 7922.535(a) provides that an agency shall, "within 10 days from receipt
of the request, determine whether the request, in whole or in part, seeks copies of
disclosable public records in the possession of the agency and shall promptly notify the
person making the request of the determination and the reasons therefor," and that if the
agency determines the request seeks disclosable public records, it "shall also state the
estimated date and time when the records will be made available." The tenth day fell on
Sunday, October 18, so the determination was due no later than Monday, October 19. I did
not receive one.
Section 7922.535(b) allows that time limit to be extended in unusual circumstances "by
written notice from the head of the agency or a designee to the person making the request,
setting forth the reasons for the extension and the date on which a determination is

expected to be dispatched," and provides that "[t]he notice shall not specify a date that
would result in an extension for more than 14 days." The October 27 notice arrived eight
days after the determination was due. It gives a reason ("awaiting responses from other
units") but no date on which a determination is expected. Even an extension given on time
could not have run past November 2. As of today, November 5, I have received neither a
determination nor an estimated date.
Section 7922.500 provides that "[n]othing in this division shall be construed to permit an
agency to delay or obstruct the inspection or copying of public records." For the Item 1
records, Penal Code § 832.7(b)(11) separately provides that, except where paragraph (8) of
that subdivision permits temporary withholding for a longer period, records subject to
disclosure under that subdivision "shall be provided at the earliest possible time and no
later than 45 days from the date of a request for their disclosure." For this request,
that is November 22, 2026.
Please send the determination for each of Items 1, 2 and 3, with the reasons for it. For
each item the Department determines seeks disclosable records, please state the estimated
date and time when those records will be made available.

Items 2 and 3 are discrete records: the audit-log entries that, as reported, provided the
evidence in the matter, and the record of the officer's Flock access being disabled.
Please produce them when they are ready rather than holding them for Item 1.
If any record or portion of a record is withheld, please identify it and the exemption
asserted, and set forth the names and titles or positions of each person responsible for
the denial (§ 7922.540(b)).
