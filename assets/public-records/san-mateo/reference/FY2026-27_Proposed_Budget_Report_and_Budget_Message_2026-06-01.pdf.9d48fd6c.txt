SUBJECT:
Fiscal Year 2026-27 Proposed Budget and 2026-31 Capital Improvement Program — Review
RECOMMENDATION:
Review the fiscal year 2026-27 proposed budget and the 2026-31 Capital Improvement Program budget and conduct a 
public hearing prior to the scheduled adoption at the June 15, 2026, City Council meeting.
BACKGROUND:
Prior to the City Council’s scheduled adoption of the fiscal year (FY) 2026-27 budget, it holds two public hearings to receive 
public comment on the proposed operating and capital budgets. This review of the budget is the first of those two public 
hearings.
FY 2026-27 is the first year of the City’s two-year 2026-28 business plan and the focus is on developing the City‘s operating 
budget. As part of developing the budget, each Department reviewed their operating budgets in detail and proposed two-
year budgets within the parameters set by the Finance Department. For the FY 2026-28 Business Plan, Finance staff 
directed Departments to maintain their baseline budgets which included making no new requests and working to maintain 
cost increases. The proposed budget also reflects the implementation of parts of the City’s Financial Sustainability Plan, 
including cost recovery enhancements and expenditure controls. In addition, although the CIP is not typically emphasized 
during this budget cycle, staff did conduct a thorough review of the CIP given the completion of the facilities assessment 
and the need to prioritize facility improvements and funding within the newly established Capital Investment Reserve. 
On April 20, 2026, Finance staff presented the City Council with a preview of the FY 2026-27 General Fund operating 
budget and the 2026-31 Five-Year CIP. Additionally, staff provided four scenarios for the General Fund ten-year forecast to 
illustrate potential fiscal outcomes based on property tax in-lieu of vehicle license fees (VLF) shortfalls and a potential 
elimination of Real Property Transfer Tax (RPTT). Following this meeting and the feedback provided by the City Council, 
staff worked to finalize the proposed budget. In addition, on May 21, 2026, the Measure S Oversight Committee met to 
review the proposed budget and CIP for the Measure S funds. At the meeting, the Committee voted to recommend the 
proposed budgets to the City Council and the Committee expressed their interest in prioritizing projects that fund 
pedestrian and bicycle safety.
The proposed FY 2026-27 citywide budget (for all funds) totals $356.5 million, which includes an operating budget of 
$290.3 million, and a capital budget of $66.3 million. The General Fund budget (including transfers) is proposed at $190.9 
million. Key budget highlights are summarized in the City Manager’s Letter of Transmittal, included in the opening section 
of the proposed budget book (Attachment 1), which was published on the City’s website on May 28, 2026. Additional 
details on all the City's revenues and expenditures are also included throughout the budget book.

City Manager’s Letter of Transmittal 
 
Honorable Mayor and Members of the City Council: 
I am pleased to present for your review and consideration the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2026-27 operating budget and the 
FY 2026-27 Capital Budget along with the City’s Capital Improvement Program (CIP) for the next five years. This budget 
reflects our commitment to maintaining core City services and making strategic investments in our community, while 
pursuing long-term fiscal health in a challenging and unpredictable environment.  
 
The proposed citywide budget, including both operating and capital, is $356.5 million for fiscal year 2026-27. This budget 
focuses on funding core services, Council priorities, and community needs. The five-year CIP reflects the City’s 
commitment to improve and maintain City facilities and infrastructure, by investing $350 million over the next five years in 
capital projects.  
 
Overall, the Citywide budget does increase in expenditures, which reflects growing operational pressure as inflationary 
impacts and rising labor costs increasingly affect the cost of delivering services. Costs tied to outside goods and services, 
utilities, insurance, and personnel continue to trend upward, reflecting broader economic conditions that have materially 
increased the City’s ongoing operating costs despite efforts to maintain a stable budget. Ongoing obligations such as 
payments toward unfunded pension liabilities also continue to place demands on the budget. In addition, funding 
challenges limit the City’s ability to continually maintain and modernize aging facilities and infrastructure, with over $348 
million in unfunded capital needs over the next five years. As the City continues to navigate this cost escalation, this budget 
proposes minimal programmatic expansion for FY 2026-27 given the estimated structural deficit tied to the provision of 
essential services.  
 
Simultaneously, over the past year, the City’s revenues have continued to face pressure, and the economic outlook has 
become increasingly uncertain. Factors such as global instability, persistently high inflation and interest rates, flattening 
sales tax revenues, and shortfalls in State Vehicle License Fee (VLF) funding are all affecting the City’s financial picture. A 
recent proposed ballot measure to eliminate the City’s real property transfer tax presents an added threat. In addition, there 
are broader risks to state and federal funding that could further impact local resources. 
 
Taken together, these factors present a risk to the City’s long-term financial stability. Owing to careful financial 
management by the City Council, both current and past, the City has built up reserves that can help bridge this gap in the 
short term. However, it is critical to balance this short-term solution with a proactive plan that ensures long-term 
sustainability. This includes maintaining core services, investing in critical infrastructure, managing costs responsibly, 
pursuing grant opportunities and increased economic development, and identifying reliable, ongoing sources of revenue – 
all facets of the City’s active Financial Sustainability Plan.  
 
Financial Sustainability Plan 
 
While we continue to closely monitor these fiscal challenges, we are taking proactive steps through our Financial 
Sustainability Plan. The plan is already underway, with several actions in progress this past year and additional measures 
planned to help strengthen the City’s financial future. Our first step has been to implement expenditure controls through 
freezing ten vacant positions, assessing and shifting appropriate expenses to Non-General Fund sources, generating 
benefit savings by reducing Worker’s Compensation allocations given a robust existing balance, and implementing 
various operating budget savings through contract renegotiations. These measures are estimated to save us 
approximately $3.2 million. The FY 2026-27 budget also proposes $2 million less in General Fund funding for capital 
projects to help offset some of the anticipated operating deficit.

Fiscal Reality and Challenges 
While we prioritize Council goals and community needs, this budget is developed amidst a complex and uncertain 
financial environment. We must acknowledge the current fiscal realities, including high inflation, economic uncertainty, 
and potential revenue shortfalls. Uncertainties related to our tax revenues raise concerns about our financial sustainability. 
Additionally, we also face increasing costs to provide services, ongoing pension obligations, and funding limitations for 
CIP projects.  
 
Tax Revenue Uncertainties 
San Mateo relies on tax revenues to fund our core services. Although secured property tax, the City’s largest tax revenue, 
remains a stable revenue source, this revenue base is projecting a moderate 4.0% annual growth for the next few years. 
Sales tax, as the City’s second largest tax revenue, is projected to stay flat with a moderate 1-2% annual growth. The 
City’s third largest tax revenue, property transfer tax, fluctuates with economic conditions and the housing market. Due to 
high interest rates and a stable, but slow housing market, property transfer tax is anticipated to grow moderately with a 
$9.0 million revenue projection for the upcoming year. However, recent potential efforts to eliminate Real Property 
Transfer Tax at the state level pose a threat to this essential revenue source.  
In addition, the City continues to face major financial uncertainty due to the State’s property tax in-lieu of vehicle license

Pension Costs 
Pension obligations continue to place a significant strain on our budget. CalPERS pension contribution requirements have 
increased significantly over the years due to a number of factors, including CalPERS lower-than-expected investment 
returns in prior years and changes in actuarial assumptions. The City’s unfunded pension liability is currently about $290 
million. Citywide pension costs are expected to continue to go up in FY 2026-27 as they did in FY 2025-26, though not by 
quite as much. For FY 2025-26, the General Fund pension total increased $1.2 million while an increase of $900,000 is 
expected in FY 2026-27. Some of the increase is due to historic changes to the unfunded pension liability resulting from 
the CalPERS’s investment loss of -6.2% in FY 2021-22 and the 5.8% investment gain in FY 2022-23, which did not meet 
the CalPERS investment projections. In the last two completed fiscal years, CalPERS met its investment objectives with a 
9.3% return in FY 2023-24 and a 12.1% in FY 2024-25. Thus, additional increases to the City’s unfunded pension liability 
are mostly due to updates to the actuarial assumptions. Given this history, the City’s overall pension costs are estimated to 
gradually keep increasing through FY 2030-31, after which pension requirements are expected to gradually ramp down, 
provided CalPERS can achieve its 6.8% annual investment return target for future years.

Aging City Facilities and Infrastructure  
It is well-acknowledged that resources such as parks, open space, playgrounds, libraries, vibrant recreation facilities, 
smooth and safe streets, storm and flood controls, and clean water infrastructure are the essence of a thriving community. 
The City is committed to continuing to invest in our infrastructure and improve our facilities to a level the community 
deserves. However, maintaining and improving our aged City facilities and infrastructure requires significant funding, and 
right now, our resources are limited.    
 
Streets and Transportation  
Currently, we rely on special revenue sources, including Gas Tax (state gas taxes), Measures A and W (countywide 
transportation focused sales taxes), Measure M (countywide vehicle license fee), and Road Maintenance and 
Rehabilitation Account (SB-1 state gas tax) funds, totaling about $9.2 million in FY 2026-27 to fund streets and 
transportation projects. However, the annual cost of maintaining our streets alone nears $8.0 million, leaving little for other 
critical projects. To address the funding gap, we often turn to the General Fund and Measure S for extra funding support. 
This may become more and more challenging as the General Fund itself is experiencing a significant budget deficit. 
 
Aging City Facilities  
The General Fund, which supports all City facility repairs and improvements, faces increasing pressure. Although General 
Fund and Measure S combined provide about $6.4 million annually to fund CIP projects, the FY 2026-27 contribution 
proposes a $2 million reduction in General Fund funding for capital projects to help offset some of the anticipated 
operating deficit. Additionally, the Measure S allocation is reduced to allow the fund to build, given that significant capital 
budgeting of Measure S funds over the last two years has committed much of the fund’s balance. At the same time, the 
budget recommends a one-time Measure CC contribution of $1.25 million to help address critical infrastructure needs and 
offset the lack of General Fund resources. Overall, the anticipated contribution totals from the General Fund, Measure S, 
and Measure CC are proposed at $4.2 million, with $2.25 million from the General Fund, $0.7 million from Measure S, and 
$1.25 million from Measure CC.  
 
During FY 2025-26, the City completed the Citywide Comprehensive Facilities Condition Assessment to ensure effective 
investments and prioritize the many competing needs. Based on Council direction, staff is proposing $6.7 million in 
allocations from the Capital Investment Reserve to fund various projects identified in the key findings of the assessment. 
This funding is proposed to go towards project budget requests in FY 2026-27 for the King Recreation and Swim Center 
($4.0 million), New Fire Station 27 ($2.2 million), Downtown Parking Analysis ($0.25 million), and Comprehensive 
Recreation and Aquatic Facilities Assessment ($0.25 million). This contribution of $6.7 million will leave a remaining 
balance of $18.3 million in the Capital Investment Reserve and cover funding gaps between the total General Fund, 
Measure S, and Measure CC contributions and project budget requests in FY 2026-27. 
   
In addition to the proposed projects, many City facility projects were put on hold or deferred due to funding issues. These 
projects have high-level cost estimates that would need to be further refined at the point of becoming a funded project. 
Notable projects on the unfunded list include future phases of the King Center and Fire Station 27 after the currently 
funded phases are completed, the 28th Avenue Gap Closure project (construction funding), large-scale Marina Lagoon 
dredging (design and construction), and future full-scale implementation of Master Plan efforts including the forthcoming 
Storm Drain Master Plan and long-term implementation of the Bike Master Plan. This list highlights the significant need 
related to City facilities and infrastructure.   
 
Although funding is a challenge, our goal is to continue working collaboratively, exploring funding options, and prioritizing 
needs to create a plan to build the infrastructure and facilities our community deserves.
