BACKGROUND:
The City of San Mateo is committed to maintaining a high quality of life and essential municipal services that support the 
health, safety, and welfare of the community. Recent community surveys found that residents are highly satisfied with San 
Mateo’s quality of life and support continued investment in essential City services and infrastructure. 
Despite ongoing efforts to manage expenditures, improve cost recovery, pursue grant funding, support economic 
development, and identify other revenue enhancement opportunities, the City continues to face long-term financial 
challenges as the cost of providing essential services has outpaced revenue growth. In addition, the City has more than 
$350 million in unfunded capital improvement and infrastructure needs, including streets, sidewalks, parks, public 
facilities, stormwater infrastructure, and other critical public assets. 
Fiscal Outlook
At the April 20, 2026, City Council meeting, staff provided a preview of the Fiscal Year 2026-27 budget which estimated a 
$16 million deficit for the General Fund, the City’s primary operating fund providing essential services such as public 
safety, transportation, library and parks and recreation services. At that same meeting, City staff provided an update to the 
General Fund 10-year forecast and presented four different scenarios highlighting various threats to the City’s top revenue 
sources. Even under the most optimistic revenue assumptions, the forecast projected ongoing structural General Fund 
deficits of approximately $5-7 million annually, assuming some personnel savings and fewer General Fund contributions to 
capital.
At the June 1, 2026, City Council meeting, staff presented the recommended budget for Fiscal Year 2026-27, which 
included projected expenditures exceeding projected revenues by $17 million. The proposed budget also identified 
strategies to improve the City’s long-term fiscal outlook, including expenditure controls, cost recovery, economic 
development, grant funding, and evaluation of potential revenue enhancement options. Staff also identified that

Ballot Measure Development Process
City staff began evaluating potential ballot measure options with the Revenue Measure Ad Hoc Committee on August 6, 
2025, followed by a City Council study session on August 18, 2025. At the study session, the City Council agreed to further 
evaluate a ¼-cent Transactions and Use (sales) Tax measure to address the City’s structural deficit and a General Obligation 
Bond to address infrastructure needs. To better understand community sentiment regarding both potential measures, the 
City conducted a statistically valid survey in November 2025. 
The November 2025 survey was administered to a stratified and clustered random sample of 1,012 registered voters in the 
City of San Mateo who are likely to participate in the November 2026 election. Of those surveyed, 506 respondents 
answered questions regarding the proposed sales tax measure, and the other 506 respondents answered questions 
regarding the General Obligation Bond.  The survey followed a mixed-method design that employed multiple recruiting 
methods (email, text, and telephone) and multiple data collection methods (telephone and online). The survey provided 
statistically reliable results, with a margin of error due to sampling of +/- 4.3% at the 95% confidence level.
At the January 7, 2026, Revenue Measure Ad Hoc Committee meeting, and the January 12, 2026, City Council meeting, City 
staff and True North Research presented the results of the Baseline Revenue Measure Survey, which found 56% support 
for a potential ¼-cent Transactions and Use (sales) Tax measure and 54% support for a potential General Obligation Bond. 
While both measures received majority support, the General Obligation Bond did not meet the two-thirds voter approval 
threshold required for passage. Based on the survey results, the Ad Hoc Committee recommended, and the City Council 
supported, continuing community education and outreach regarding the potential sales tax measure while pausing further 
education and outreach related to the General Obligation Bond. 
Following several months of community education and outreach, True North Research conducted a follow-up survey 
between May 11 and May 15, 2026, to assess voter sentiment regarding the potential sales tax measure. The survey was 
administered to 689 registered voters in the City of San Mateo who are likely to participate in the November 2026 
election. True North Research employed the same methodology from the first survey and had a margin of error due to 
sampling of +/- 3.7% at the 95% confidence level.
On June 15, 2026, staff and True North Research presented the survey results to City Council. The Initial Ballot Test results  
found 58% of likely November 2026 voters surveyed in support, with 25% indicating they would definitely support the 
measure. Approximately 33% of respondents opposed the measure, and 9% were unsure or unwilling to state their 
position. The Council also received a presentation from the City’s elections consultant, Team CivX, regarding the ballot 
measure process and next steps. After discussion, the Council expressed support for placing the proposed measure on the 
November 2026 ballot. At the same June 15, 2026 meeting, the Council adopted Resolution No. 60 ordering and calling for 
an election to be consolidated with the General Municipal Election to be held on November 3, 2026.

Transactions and Use Tax (sales tax) Information
A Transactions and Use Tax, commonly referred to as a local sales tax, is an additional tax added to the existing sales tax 
rate that applies to most retail goods delivered within a jurisdiction. It does not apply to groceries or prescription 
medications. 
The total sales tax rate in San Mateo is 9.625%, which is made up of California’s 7.25% statewide base sales tax and voter 
approved countywide and local district taxes. The current rate includes the following voter-approved measures:
•
In November 2015, San Mateo voters approved Measure S, a sales tax, which extended Measure L, a ¼-cent sales 
tax. 
•
In November 2016, San Mateo County voters approved Measure K, extending the County’s ½-cent sales tax
•
In November 2020, San Mateo voters also approved Measure RR, a separate ¼-cent regional sales tax dedicated to 
Caltrain operations.
State law establishes a maximum allowable Transactions and Use Tax rate that cities may impose. The proposed ¼-cent 
increase represents the maximum additional local sales tax the City is authorized to place before voters under current 
state law.  
A ¼-cent sales tax increase would result in approximately $7 million in estimated revenue annually. For the consumer, this 
increase equates to an additional 25 cents on every $100 spent on taxable purchases. Independent annual audits and 
public reporting would be required to ensure funds are used as intended, and an independent resident oversight 
committee would review transparency and accountability. Under the proposed ordinance, this would be the same as the 
Measure S Oversight Committee established by San Mateo Municipal Code section 3.50.160.

Council Action:
Per the City Council’s direction at its June 15, 2026, meeting, staff recommends adoption of the proposed Resolution 
(Attachment 1) placing the ¼-cent Transactions and Use (sales) tax measure on the November 2026 ballot with the 
following ballot question: 
To provide funding for San Mateo city services, such as keeping local streets, sidewalks, parks, 
infrastructure, and community facilities safe, clean, and well-maintained, fixing potholes, providing fire 
protection, paramedic, crime prevention, and 9-1-1 emergency response, and improvements to traffic 
safety/traffic management, shall the City of San Mateo’s ordinance establishing a one-quarter cent sales 
tax be adopted, providing 7 million dollars annually for general government use until ended by voters, 
with independent audits and all funds locally-controlled?  
In accordance with state law, the foregoing ballot question is not more than 75 words, as the phrases “San Mateo” and 
“City of San Mateo” each count as one word.  
Under state law, submitting the measure to the voters must be approved by a two-thirds’ vote of the City Council.  Rev. & 
Tax §7285.9, Govt. §53724(a). With a five-member Council, this means that four votes are required to place the proposed

BUDGET IMPACT:
If the City Council adopts the Resolution placing the Transactions and Use (Sales) Tax measure on the November 3, 2026 
ballot and the measure is subsequently approved by the voters, the proposed ¼-cent additional tax is estimated to 
generate approximately $7 million in annual General Fund revenue, which would be retained locally for City of San Mateo 
general municipal services. 
The City Clerk’s Office Fiscal Year 2026-27 operating budget already includes funding for the standard costs associated with 
the November 3, 2026, General Municipal Election, including the election of City Council members, translation, and 
noticing requirements. 
The County of San Mateo Elections Division has estimated the additional cost of placing a citywide measure on the ballot 
at approximately $158,000, based on the number of registered voters. The proposed Resolution includes a supplemental 
budget appropriation of $158,000 from the General Fund unassigned fund balance to the City Clerk’s Office Fiscal Year 
2026-27 operating budget to cover these additional election costs.

3.51.120. FISCAL ACCOUNTABILITY PROVISIONS. 
 
The amount generated by this new general purpose revenue source and how it was used shall be 
included in the annual audit of the City's financial operations by an independent certified public 
accountant. In addition, there shall be a committee consisting of five members of the public to review 
and report on the receipt of revenue and expenditure of funds from the tax authorized by this chapter. 
The committee shall review the annual auditor's report and shall make recommendations to the City 
Council on the use of the tax revenue. The committee's recommendations shall be completed by a date 
to allow for it to be considered as part of the annual budget process. The committee members shall 
serve four-year terms and shall be the same as the committee established by Section 3.50.160.
