In accordance to Government Code Section § 7922.525 a legal review of your request was 
completed. The following letter is in response to your request submitted under Reference # 
W012665-0450426. 
 
This letter responds to your California Public Records Act (“CPRA”) request dated May 4, 2026, 
in which you seek audit logs from the San Mateo Police Department’s Automated License Plate 
Reader (“ALPR”) system. Specifically, you have requested records reflecting searches conducted 
by outside law enforcement agencies accessing the Department’s ALPR data through the Flock 
Safety network sharing platform. 
After careful review, the Department has determined that these records are exempt from disclosure 
for the reasons set forth below: 
Investigatory Records Exemption and Investigative Files Exemption (Government Code § 
7923.600). 
The requested audit records reflect search activity conducted by law enforcement agencies in 
connection with criminal investigations. Each log entry documents the existence, timing, and 
nature of an investigative act by another agency, including the category of criminal offense under 
investigation and the time frame of the search. Disclosure of these records would reveal 
confidential investigative activity. These records are therefore exempt from disclosure as 
investigatory records compiled by a law enforcement agency for law enforcement purposes 
pursuant to Government Code section 7923.600. 
In addition, the audit log entries at issue reflect the investigative decisions and activities of other 
law enforcement agencies, not those of the San Mateo Police Department. The Department is not 
the originating agency for these investigative records and is not in a position to assess whether 
disclosure would compromise another agency’s ongoing or pending investigations.  
Public Interest Balancing Test (Government Code § 7922.000). 
Even to the extent any portion of the requested records might not fall squarely within a categorical 
exemption, the Department withholds these records under the public interest balancing test set 
forth in Government Code section 7922.000. The audit logs contain a combination of data fields 
that, taken together, would allow identification of specific criminal investigations being conducted 
by specific agencies during specific time periods. The public interest served by nondisclosure — 
protecting the integrity of active and pending criminal investigations across multiple jurisdictions 
— clearly outweighs the public interest served by disclosure.

Official Information Privilege (Evidence Code § 1040). 
The requested records constitute official information acquired by law enforcement personnel in 
confidence. Disclosure of the investigative search activity of other agencies would be against the 
public interest because there is a necessity for preserving the confidentiality of this information 
that outweighs the necessity for disclosure in the interest of justice. Revealing which agencies are 
searching for which categories of criminal activity, and when, would compromise investigative 
strategies and techniques across multiple law enforcement jurisdictions. 
Some of the information you have requested has been posted publicly on the City’s website. Here 
is the link to website that contains the information publicly available regarding your request: Flock 
Safety - San Mateo CA PD Transparency Portal.
