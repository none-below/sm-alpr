Introduction
Per SMPD Standard Operating Procedure 205, our Department conducts quarterly audits of our ‘End-User’ data to 
ensure our employees are reviewing data in a manner consistent with policy and procedure. The data reviewed is 
generated and provided by the Northern California Regional Intelligence Center (NCRIC) and the Flock Platform 
Portal as they house data for the respective platforms. The results of these audits are summarized and presented to 
the Support Services Captain for review and later for retention by the Support Services Division.
Quarter 2, 2021 
PIPs / BOSS (April, May & June)
ALPR Committee met and determined that the PIPs/BOSS ALPR platform and hardware equipment was antiquated 
causing a significant drain on resources.  Additionally, the platform was often not performing correctly and had 
significant downtime due to connectivity and computer issues.  The ALPR Committee agreed that we would 
terminate the contract with Neology, Inc., the parent company of PIPS/BOSS.  On 5/25/21 official notification was 
sent to Neology via certified mail cancelling their services.  We continue to have access to BOSS for queries, but we 
are no longer contributing to the database through our PIPs cameras.  PIPs system cameras captured 263,227 reads 
this quarter.

Quarter 2, 2021 
Flock Safety April, May & June)
This is the first complete audit of the Flock data since going online in February 2021.  This audit will include a full 
sampling of the entire 3 months of data.  After the first Flock audit, training was determined necessary for staff to 
enter the appropriate reason for search.  We implemented training in the form of a procedure update memo and as 
a result 100% of the reason for query was entered in Q2.  This marked an 85% improvement in the entry of required 
reason for search.  There has been an overall 64% increase in the department’s usage of the Flock ALPR platform.  
The “Number of Searches” indicates searches of a license plate against a particular database; for instance, when an 
officer queries a plate, it searches NCIC, SVS, and other municipalities where we have access to their ALPR data.  
Single plate query appears as numerous searches because it is searching the individual databases, hence the high 
“Number of Searches.”

Analysis and Conclusion
Our department ‘End-User’ activity during the time period was consistent with Department policy and procedure. 
Our staff used this technology platform above normal usage rates, which is reasonable given the types of 
investigations they were conducting. No additional efforts are recommended.
