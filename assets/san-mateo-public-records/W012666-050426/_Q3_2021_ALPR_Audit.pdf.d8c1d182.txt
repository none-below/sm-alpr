Summary of Quarterly Audit of SMPD ‘End-User’ ALPR Data for Q4, 2021
Introduction
Per SMPD Standard Operating Procedure 205, our Department conducts quarterly audits of our ‘End-User’ data to 
ensure our employees are reviewing data in a manner consistent with policy and procedure. The data reviewed is 
generated and provided by the Northern California Regional Intelligence Center (NCRIC) and the Flock Platform 
Portal as they house data for the respective platforms. The results of these audits are summarized and presented to 
the Support Services Captain for review and later for retention by the Support Services Division.
Quarter 3, 2021 
PIPs / BOSS (July, August & September)
Although the contract with Neology has ended, we continue to utilize their ALPR hardware in our vehicles.  Two of 
the three in-car systems are functional, and we still have the ability to log in and collect ALPR data via the PIPs 
cameras.  We continue to upload this data into NCRIC via BOSS4 software. The following are the current end user 
data for the PIPs/BOSS queries from the NCRIC.   ALPR plate reads are continuing to decrease, as anticipated, as we 
move away from this technology.  263,227 reads in Q2 and 199,256 reads now in Q3.

Quarter 3, 2021 
Flock Safety (July, August & September)
This is the second complete audit of the Flock data since going online in February 2021.  This audit includes a full 
sampling if data compiled between the months of July, August and September 2021 (Q3).  The “Number of 
Searches” indicate searches of a license plate against a particular database; for instance, when an officer queries a 
plate, it searches NCIC, SVS, and other municipalities where we have access to their ALPR data.  Single plate query 
appears as numerous searches because it is searching the individual databases, hence the high “Number of 
Searches.”  As suspected, the Flock system is utilized often and had become a vital crimefighting tool.  Last quarter’s 
usage was up 64% from the previous quarter and end-usage jumped another 49.95% this quarter.   
There was a high number of entries for Flock that list “test” as the reason for query.  This is due to the continued 
effort to keep the cameras calibrated and troubleshoot as problems are identified.  In August, we learned that 11 of 
22 of our Flock cameras were malfunctioning, and were not collecting data during the nighttime hours.  The issue 
was the infrared system in the camera itself; those cameras were replaced on 10/18.

Analysis and Conclusion
Our Department ‘End-user’ activity during the time period was consistent with Department policy and procedure. 
Our staff used this technology platform above normal usage rates, which is reasonable given the types of 
investigations they were conducting. No additional efforts are recommended.
