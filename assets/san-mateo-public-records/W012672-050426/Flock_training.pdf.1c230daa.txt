Existing law authorizes the Department of the
California Highway Patrol to retain license plate data
captured by license plate recognition (LPR)
technology for not more than 60 days unless the data
is being used as evidence or for the investigation of
felonies.
Existing law authorizes the department to use LPR data for
the purpose of locating vehicles or persons reasonably
suspected of being involved in the commission of a public
offense, and requires the department to monitor the
internal use of the data to prevent unauthorized use and to
submit to Legislature, as a part of the annual automobile
theft report, information on the department’s LPR practices
and usage.

reasonable security procedures and
practices, including operational,
administrative, technical, and physical
safeguards, to protect ALPR information

Tips & Tricks
Search with Wildcards
Search for a Partial Match
Wildcards can help narrow your search
if you know most of a plate except for
specific characters. 
To use a wildcard, replace unknown
characters with an asterisk *
Your search results will contain any
plates that follow the pattern of your
search. For example, searching for
ABC*234 could return any of the
following: 
ABCD234
ABC1234
ABC234
ABC-234
ABCD1234
Partial searches can help narrow your
search if you know only some of the
characters on a plate. 
To use a partial plate search, enter the
characters you know.
Your search results will contain any
plates that contain the sequence you
entered. For example, searching for
ABC could return any of the following: 
ABC1234
1234ABC
12ABC34
If you’re unsure of a digit (ex. 8 or B),
use a ‘?’ to replace the unknown digit
when searching
