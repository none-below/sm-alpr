Context
As you are aware, on 2026-05-14 SMPD closed PRA W012663-050426 with the following dispositions:
- Items 1, 3, 4, 5 (records of NCRIC's onward disclosure of SMPD-originated ALPR data to University of 
the Pacific, Stanford University, and University of San Francisco; scope documentation; sharing 
agreements; SMPD awareness records): "No responsive records."
- Items 2 and 6 (audit-log entries for Named-Recipient access; SMPD-side audit-log entries 
memorializing SMPD's contributions to NCRIC): Withheld under Gov. Code § 7923.600.
Gov. Code § 7923.600 exempts records of investigations conducted by "any state or local police agency" 
and investigatory files compiled by "any other state or local police agency." The exemption turns on 
whether the investigative activity was conducted by a state or local police agency.
University of the Pacific's Chief of Police confirmed in writing on 2026-03-11 (produced by UOP and 
previously cited in SMPD-facing correspondence) that UOP is a private institution not subject to CPRA.

Stanford University and the University of San Francisco are 501(c)(3) private universities; their campus 
police departments are not § 1798.90.5(f) "public agencies." NCRIC's published Flock transparency 
portal (captured 2026-04-25) lists UOP, Stanford CA PD, and USF CA PD among 319 outbound 
recipients of SMPD's contributed data.
A threshold observation on the records sought. The records withheld under W012663 Items 2 and 6 
document disclosures of SMPD-originated ALPR data that have already occurred — to University of the 
Pacific, Stanford University, and University of San Francisco. Withholding the records does not undo 
those disclosures; it determines only whether the Department will produce records of them. As you are 
aware, any exemption the Department invokes for these items carries an implicit position on the 
lawfulness of the underlying disclosures:
If the Department invokes § 7923.600 — which exempts records of investigations conducted by "a state 
or local police agency" — the Department is asserting that UOP, Stanford, and USF are state or local 
police agencies. As you are aware, University of the Pacific's Chief of Police confirmed in writing on 
2026-03-11 that UOP is a private institution not subject to the California Public Records Act — a direct 
statement by the institution itself that it is not a public agency. If the Department's § 7923.600 invocation 
nonetheless rests on a determination that UOP is a state or local police agency, the Department should 
identify the authority under which that institution qualifies, explain how that determination is consistent 
with UOP's own written position, and state whether it considers NCRIC's onward disclosures to UOP, 
Stanford, and USF to have been consistent with Civil Code § 1798.90.55(b)'s restriction on sharing 
ALPR information except to "another public agency."
If the Department invokes any exemption premised on the recipients not being public agencies, or 
declines to produce records on any basis that presupposes the recipients are non-public entities, the 
Department's response should address whether it considers the disclosures themselves to have been 
consistent with § 1798.90.55(b). This request asks, in part, for records that would reflect the 
Department's position on exactly that question.
W012663's denial-procedure paragraph asked the Department to address segregability under Gov. Code 
§ 7922.525(b) and, if any portion were denied, to (1) provide specific exemption citations per Gov. Code §
7922.540; (2) identify the withheld record type and volume; (3) indicate whether the denial rested on a 
claim that the records are held by NCRIC or Flock Safety rather than SMPD; and (4) if the denial 
invoked Civil Code § 1798.90.55(b) or any similar rationale, state whether SMPD considers NCRIC's 
onward disclosure to UOP/Stanford/USF to have been lawful and identify the supporting legal authority. 
The May 14 response addressed (1) by citing § 7923.600; it did not address segregability or (2), (3), or 
(4).
Cross-references. This request follows W012663-050426 (closed 2026-05-14) and is informed by 
W012570-041926, W012664-050426, and W012674-050426. The records sought here are distinct from 
those sought in W012663: this request seeks the Department's analytical and decisional records 
supporting the W012663 dispositions, not the underlying audit-log records themselves.

- Penal Code §§ 832.7–832.8 (peace officer personnel records). This request does not seek disciplinary, 
complaint, or investigation content concerning any individual employee. It seeks records of the 
Department's analytical and decisional process in responding to W012663 — administrative records, not 
personnel records under Penal Code § 832.8(a).
- Attorney-client privilege / attorney work product (Gov. Code § 7927.705). To the extent any responsive 
record is asserted to be privileged, please segregate and produce any non-privileged portion (Gov. Code 
§ 7922.525(b)) and identify the asserted privilege with the specificity required by Gov. Code § 7922.540. 
The fact that a privileged communication occurred (e.g., a legal opinion was prepared on date X by 
counsel Y on subject Z) is itself non-exempt under Roberts v. City of Palmdale (1993) 5 Cal.4th 363 and 
is responsive even if the substantive content of the communication is withheld.
- Deliberative process privilege (Gov. Code § 7922.000). To the extent invoked, please describe the 
specific deliberative interest harmed by disclosure for each record withheld, and produce factual 
portions and final determinations as required by Times Mirror v. Superior Court (1991) 53 Cal.3d 1325.
- Gov. Code § 7923.600 (investigation records). The records sought here are records of the Department's 
CPRA response process — analytical, administrative, and decisional records — not records of any 
investigation or investigatory file.
- Civil Code § 1798.90.55(b). The records sought are the Department's analysis and decision records, not 
"ALPR information" as defined in § 1798.90.5(c).
- Segregability (Gov. Code § 7922.525(b)). Gov. Code § 7922.525(b) requires production of any 
reasonably segregable portion of a record after deletion of exempt portions. Where any responsive 
record contains both responsive and exempt content, the non-exempt portions are subject to that 
requirement.
- Existing records only / no obligation to create new records (Gov. Code § 7922.530). This request seeks 
existing records only. If no responsive record exists for any item, that itself is a responsive answer; please 
affirm the search performed and confirm no records were located.
If any portion is denied, please:
1. provide specific exemption citations per Gov. Code § 7922.540;
2. identify the withheld record type and volume; and
3. for each record withheld in whole, describe the segregability analysis performed and identify the 
specific content within the record that the Department determined could not be produced in redacted 
form.
