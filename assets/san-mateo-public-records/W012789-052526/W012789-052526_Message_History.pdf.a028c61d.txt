Context
As you are aware, PRA W012320-031126 Items 2(b) and 2(c) sought the per-category counts of searches 
where a case/incident/warrant number was or was not entered (2(b)) and where the connection between 
the searched data and the listed justification was or was not verified (2(c)). The Department's 4/22/2026 
response declined those items on the ground that "the report you seek does not exist as a record 
maintained by the Department or within the Flock platform" and that the CPRA does not require 
compilation. This request does not ask for compilation. It asks for the existing per-search record that 
underlies any such count, in a form that discloses no investigative content.
The Department has already established that per-search records of this type exist and are within the 
scope of what it produces under the CPRA:
- Under W012541-041426, the Department has produced, on a rolling basis, monthly native CSV exports 
of the Flock platform search audit for SMPD. The exports carry the column schema id, userId, 
searchDate, networkCount, reason. As you already know, that production demonstrates that (a) per-
search records exist in the Flock platform; (b) SMPD treats those records as agency records subject to

Anticipated exemption issues
- Gov. Code § 7923.600 (records of investigation). The record requested discloses no investigative 
content. Every cell value entered by a searching user — case number, plate, free-text justification, 
agency identifier, offense category — is redacted at production. Empty cells are not redacted because an 
empty cell is not a record of investigation. What remains is the existence and timestamp of each search 
(already published on the Department's transparency portal) and which compliance fields on each 
search were populated. § 7923.600 protects investigative content, not the structural fact of whether a 
procedural field was filled in. The W012541-041426 production already accepts that audit-export rows 
for SMPD-user searches are not categorically exempt; this request extends the same segregable-
redaction approach already in use to every populated responsive cell.
- Civil Code § 1798.90.55(b) (ALPR-information sharing). The record requested contains no "ALPR 
information" as defined in § 1798.90.5(c) (data captured by an ALPR system about a specific vehicle). 
The redacted export discloses no plate data, no captured-image data, and no search result; it discloses 
only the existence of a search and whether each compliance field on that search was populated.
- Catchall balancing test (Gov. Code § 7922.000). The public interest in oversight of a publicly funded 
ALPR program — including in whether the per-search compliance fields the Department's own SOP 
205.5.1 references are populated — is substantial. Any reliance on the catchall balancing test should 
identify the specific harm asserted, per Gov. Code § 7922.540.
- "Existing records only" / no obligation to create new records (Gov. Code § 7922.530). This request is 
for the existing native audit export, redacted under the segregable-redaction approach the Department 
already applies. Including a column already present in the audit table is a configuration choice within 
the existing export feature, not the creation of a new record. Sierra Club, 57 Cal.4th at 165–166. 
Applying a uniform cell-redaction rule to the resulting export is a routine redaction operation, not the 
creation of a new record.
- Records held by Flock Safety as SMPD's contractor. Per City of San Jose v. Superior Court (2017) 2 
Cal.5th 608 and Gov. Code § 7920.530, records held by a vendor on the agency's behalf in the course of 
the vendor's performance of the agency's business are agency records. The W012541-041426 production 
already accepts that the Flock audit table is within SMPD's scope of producible records.
