Department Response: 
The records you seek are contained in the audit logs from the San Mateo Police Department’s 
Automated License Plate Reader (“ALPR”) system. Specifically, you have requested records 
reflecting searches conducted by an outside law enforcement agency accessing the Department’s 
ALPR data through the Flock Safety network sharing platform.  
The requested records, in their entirety, are exempt from disclosure pursuant to Government 
Code § 7923.600, Government Code § 7922.000, Evidence Code § 1040, and Civil Code § 
1798.90.55.  
 
Investigatory Records Exemption and Investigative Files Exemption (Government Code § 
7923.600).  
The requested audit records reflect search activity conducted by a law enforcement agency in 
connection with criminal investigations. Each log entry documents the existence, timing, and 
nature of an investigative act by another agency, including the category of criminal offense under 
investigation and the time frame of the search. Disclosure of these records would reveal 
confidential investigative activity. These records are therefore exempt from disclosure as 
investigatory records compiled by a law enforcement agency for law enforcement purposes 
pursuant to Government Code section 7923.600. 
  
In addition, the audit log entries at issue reflect the investigative decisions and activities of other 
law enforcement agencies, not those of the San Mateo Police Department. The Department is not 
the originating agency for these investigative records and is not in a position to assess whether 
disclosure would compromise another agency’s ongoing or pending investigations.  
 
Public Interest Balancing Test (Government Code § 7922.000).  
Even to the extent any portion of the requested records might not fall squarely within a 
categorical exemption, the Department withholds these records under the public interest 
balancing test set forth in Government Code section 7922.000. The audit logs contain a 
combination of data fields that, taken together, would allow identification of specific criminal 
investigations being conducted by specific agencies during specific time periods. The public 
interest served by nondisclosure — protecting the integrity of active and pending criminal 
investigations across multiple jurisdictions — clearly outweighs the public interest served by 
disclosure.  
 
Official Information Privilege (Evidence Code § 1040).

--- page 3 ---
The requested records constitute official information acquired by law enforcement personnel in 
confidence. Disclosure of the investigative search activity of other agencies would be against the 
public interest because there is a necessity for preserving the confidentiality of this information 
that outweighs the necessity for disclosure in the interest of justice. Revealing which agencies 
are searching for which categories of criminal activity, and when, would compromise 
investigative strategies and techniques across multiple law enforcement jurisdictions.  
 
Automated License Plate Reader (ALPR) Exemption (Civil Code § 1798.90.55).  
Your request has been denied pursuant to Government Code 7927.705, as records prohibited 
from disclosure under federal or state law. In this case, Civil Code section 1798.90.55, 
subdivision (b) prohibits a public agency from selling, sharing, or transferring ALPR 
information, except to another public agency, and only as otherwise permitted by law.  
In addition, the Flock Platform does not display the names of officers from external agencies on 
our portal. Any such information, if available, must be requested directly from the originating 
agency.
