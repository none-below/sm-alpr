Juliana,
Thank you for the September 14 production. Your message states that "the records provided confirm the source 
of Hotlist Alerts from NCIC and the California Stolen Vehicle System." That is sufficient for the hot-list 
portion of Item 6. The City may consider that portion fulfilled, and no further batches are needed.
My primary interest is Item 1. Flock's Privacy Policy names a data protection agreement and leaves to each 
customer the judgment whether its jurisdiction's data-protection law requires one; the 2023 Agreement at §3.2 
likewise places compliance with applicable law on the City. California's ALPR statute puts security obligations 
on the City as both operator and end-user, so that judgment was the City's to make. The August 31 letter's 
answer was "no responsive records." Based on previous CPRA responses, I anticipated that it didn't exist, but 
since its existence seems critical to compliance with state law, I wanted to give the City one more chance to 
locate it. That letter is captioned as a Department response, and the request was directed to the City Clerk, 
Finance, and the City Attorney's Office; please have those offices search their contract and vendor files for the 
agreement, under whatever title it carries. If the City determined at any point that no such agreement was 
required, the record of that determination is responsive as well. If the search locates neither, I will proceed on 
the basis that no agreement exists and no determination was made, with the August 31 letter as the record of it.
Brian

ALPR end-user under § 1798.90.53, and both sections require reasonable security procedures and practices —

destruction, use, modification, or disclosure. Flock directs its customers to execute a data protection
