Hi Ed,
 
Reformatted as a list with answers below each entry. Please let me know if there are any
points we should expand upon further. In brief summary: an engineer from a now-closed
company that NCRIC previously worked with in 2022 and 2023, posted his work publicly
on the code repository site Github, which unfortunately contained sensitive content
including an audit log with six months of officer queries, and ALPR device names
potentially revealing their locations; details which typically would be redacted or exempt
from public release. Efforts are underway with Github to have this taken down, and
direct outreach has been attempted with the individual. Further review is still occurring,
but fortunately I’ve yet to uncover any evidence that raw public ALPR reads have been
retained or were compromised. Note that, since 2024, NCRIC is not involved in any data
collection or replication efforts, for ALPR or any other records, from any other agencies,
including San Mateo PD.

Chief Barberini,
I just wanted to write to you to flag something new affecting the city, that you
may not have been made aware of.
Until recently, SMPD has shared ALPR data with NCRIC. SMPD's own SOP
205 says the department "partner[s] with the NCRIC to store the ALPR data
we collect with PIPs and Flock." Since February, a vendor contracted by
NCRIC has made public the software infrastructure code (it was posted by
an engineer of the company, not leaked). It's still up today, and multiple
other people have made their own copies of it since late August, rendering it
effectively permanently public.
The code that was published documents an automated process that fetches
each participating agency's previous day's plate data, and stored it into a
separate NCRIC database, with no expiration or purge setting enabled in the
published configuration. The system contains 341 datasets, including a
separate ALPR dataset for each contributing agency. None of them has an
expiration or purge value set. Because that copy sits outside Flock, searches
against it would not appear in the department's Flock audit logs.
TLDR: Every license plate read that san mateo recorded from at least
mid-2023 until the department stopped sharing with NCRIC appears to
remain in NCRIC's system (despite the department's 30-day flock retention
settings), and is not set to expire or purge.

The vendor that operated the platform seems to no longer be in business,
and that vendor also appears to retain copies of agency data, as well. It has
additionally made public audit logs and other operational records from
NCRIC's system, including San Mateo Police Department's search records
(without any redactions).
Section 6 of the department's MOU with NCRIC provides that member
agencies "remain the official custodian of all information contributed to the
NCRIC-DSP." I would be very interested to know how the department has
acted to protect its citizens' data at both another remote agency (NCRIC),
and a now defunct private business. I had previously filed a PRA (W012376)
asking for any records of the department restricting NCRIC's re-sharing of
San Mateo ALPR data -- and the "no responsive records" to that request
indicates that the department didn't even take the basic measure to protect
its residents as the NCRIC MOU outlines.
The MOU's Data Sharing section says data a member agency later declares
should not be shared "shall be withdrawn by all Hosts from all NCRIC-DSP
Systems within 48 hours, including deletion of any replications of the data" --
a step the department has produced no record of taking.
Separately, the published files include an export of NCRIC's search audit log.
As an example of what's readable right now: on September 11, 2023,
account atrujillo@cityofsanmateo.org ran three queries under case
, searching for plate numbers
