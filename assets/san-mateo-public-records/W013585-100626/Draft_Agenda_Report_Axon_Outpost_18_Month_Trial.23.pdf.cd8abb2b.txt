SUBJECT:
Axon Outpost Automated License Plate Recognition System - 18-Month Trial
RECOMMENDATION:
Approve Amendment No. [INSERT AMENDMENT NUMBER] to the City’s master services and purchasing agreement with Axon 
Enterprise, Inc., authorizing an 18-month trial of up to 54 Axon Outpost fixed automated license plate recognition cameras in an 
amount not to exceed $[INSERT TRIAL AMOUNT]; authorize the Chief of Police to execute the amendment in substantially the 
form presented; and direct staff to return to the City Council before entering into a long-term fixed ALPR agreement.
BACKGROUND:
Automated license plate recognition (ALPR) is an investigative and public-safety tool that captures license plate and vehicle 
information from vehicles traveling on public roadways. Alerts can assist in identifying stolen vehicles, vehicles associated with 
wanted or missing persons, and vehicles connected to serious investigations. ALPR information is an investigative lead only; 
officers must visually confirm the plate and independently verify an alert before taking enforcement action.
SMPD currently uses Axon body-worn cameras, Fleet 3 in-car video and mobile ALPR, Axon Evidence, and Axon Fusus. These 
systems support the Real Time Intelligence Center by bringing alerts, live information, digital evidence, and investigative 
workflows into a common operating environment. Fixed cameras would supplement mobile ALPR by providing consistent 
coverage at strategically selected roadways.
Vendor comparison. Staff evaluated fixed ALPR systems from Axon Enterprise, Inc., Flock Safety, and Motorola Solutions based 
on cost, performance, deployment, support, investigative tools, data ownership, retention, auditing, sharing, integration, 
political considerations, and community trust. Motorola presented the lowest normalized core-camera cost, and Flock offered a 
mature, rapidly deployable network. Axon received the strongest overall assessment because it best fits SMPD’s existing 
technology environment and provides a unified workflow for alerts, real-time operations, evidence, permissions, retention, and 
audits.
Axon Outpost is a solar-capable fixed ALPR platform that provides vehicle information, real-time alerts, and visual context to 
authorized users. Because SMPD already operates Fleet 3, Evidence, and Fusus, the trial can use familiar accounts, security 
controls, audits, and evidence workflows instead of creating a separate operational platform.
18-month evaluation. Staff recommends a trial rather than an immediate long-term procurement because performance varies 
with roadway geometry, lane configuration, speed, lighting, weather, plate condition, cellular coverage, solar exposure, and 
installation angle. The trial will allow SMPD to evaluate Axon under actual San Mateo conditions and compare its performance 
and value with other products before recommending a permanent solution.
Staff will measure uptime, plate-read accuracy, false and misread rates, day and night performance, alert latency, investigative 
value, maintenance response, administrative workload, and integration with Fusus and Evidence. The review will also consider 
alert outcomes, user compliance, audit findings, community feedback, and public-reporting capability. Periodic reviews will be 
conducted, followed by a final evaluation at the end of the 18-month period.
Privacy and accountability. The trial will operate under existing SMPD policy and California law. Access will be limited to 
authorized personnel for legitimate law-enforcement purposes, non-evidentiary data will be deleted under Department
