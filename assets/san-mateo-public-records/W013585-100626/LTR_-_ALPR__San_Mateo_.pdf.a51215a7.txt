Dear Members of the San Mateo City Council, 
 
 
I have been asked to describe the contribution that Automated License 
Plate Readers (ALPR) have made to public safety and more specifically, 
criminal prosecutions, in the hope that it is helpful to you in assessing the 
costs and benefits of this technology to our community.  By way of 
introduction, I am the supervisor of the Homicide Unit and have been 
involved in the prosecution of murders for nearly 20 years. 
 
 
In framing this discussion, it is important to understand that the benefits 
are not hypothetical.  In the City of San Mateo alone, 2 murder prosecutions 
have been made possible by this technology, and one additional investigation 
was substantially advanced by ALPR evidence.  In one instance, the 
identification of the perpetrator of a cold-blooded ambush killing would not 
have been possible without it.  In another, the apprehension of a man who 
strangled his pregnant girlfriend resulting in her death and the death of her 
unborn child, occurred directly as a result of ALPR evidence.  In both 
investigations, this information led police to murderers who had fled hundreds 
of miles from the scenes of their horrific crimes and may never have faced 
justice if not for ALPR technology. 
 
 
This is to say that I have stood shoulder to shoulder with homicide 
victims’ families while they hoped against hope that the people responsible 
for these tragedies are identified, arrested and prosecuted.  In some cases, that 
simply would not be possible without the information provided by ALPR 
technology.   Inevitably, serious crimes will go unsolved should ALPR 
readers be removed from the roadways of this community.

Moreover, there are considerable safeguards in place with an eye 
towards minimizing the intrusion into privacy: 1) The intentional omission of 
the vehicle’s occupants from any photos produced; 2) The requirement that a 
legitimate investigative need must exist to access the information; 3) The 
existence of audit logs; and 4) Robust policies addressing unauthorized or 
improper access. 
     
 
The law enforcement community acknowledges the concerns of the 
public and takes its responsibility as stewards of this information seriously, 
but the community should understand the price paid for being overzealous.  It 
is not hard to imagine a drive-by shooting, an armed robbery, or kidnapping 
where investigators are left looking at a public street corner where an ALPR 
camera used to be posted.  In view of this, what satisfactory answer could be 
given to victims and victims’ families when they wonder why such a valuable 
tool was removed from their police department. Thank you for your time and 
consideration.
