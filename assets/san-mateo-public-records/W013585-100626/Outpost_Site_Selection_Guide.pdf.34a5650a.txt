Axon Outpost Site Selection Guide
Why Site Selection Matters
The right site helps Outpost deliver accurate license plate reads, faster installation, and reliable uptime from day 
one. This guide outlines what to look for when identifying locations that work best.
High-Value Locations 
to Start With
•	 These locations typically deliver strong results and 
smoother deployments:
•	 City or town entry and exit points
•	 Highway on-ramps and off-ramps
•	 Jurisdictional boundaries
•	 Agency-identified crime areas
Roadway Basics 
Ideal conditions
•	 Straight sections of roadway
•	 Predictable traffic flow
•	 Clearly marked lanes
•	 One to two lanes per direction
May require additional review
•	 Sharp curves
•	 Complex intersections
•	 Areas with frequent stopping or parking
•	 Poorly marked or irregular lanes
New Pole Installation: 
What to Look For
Good candidates
•	 Near the roadway but well outside the travel lane risk of collision 
•	 Behind curbs or guardrails
•	 Clear, unobstructed views of traffic
•	 Enough space to install without blocking sidewalks
Locations That May Require Additional Review or an Alternate Site
•	 Narrow sidewalks
•	 Directly in front of driveways or entrances
•	 Heavily landscaped areas or tight medians with limited access

Mounting on Existing 
Poles: What to Look For
Good candidates
•	 Clear unobstructed views of target traffic lanes 
•	 Clear Mounting Surface: an unobstructed area on pole where camera 
hardware can be posited between ( 12-16ft) with banding clamps 
(round poles preferred)
•	 Round poles that allow hardware to be attached using banding clamps 
•	 Sufficient space to perform the installation without blocking the 
roadway or requiring traffic control
Locations That May Require Additional Review or an Alternate Site
•	 Poles with significant existing equipment already mounted
•	 Mounting or electrical restrictions due to pole ownership
•	 Irregular pole shapes
•	 Heavily landscaped areas or tight medians with limited access
Considerations to record when choosing Existing Poles
•	 Specify pole type and material: traffic, utility, decorative, wood, steel, etc.
•	 AC power check: visually verify whether the pole has an existing AC 
power source, junction box, and or disconnect available
•	 Determine whether AC or solar power will be used on the existing pole. 
Both are feasible with proper permission from the pole owner
Power Considerations
Existing power works best when
•	 Streetlight or utility poles are available
•	 There is visible power infrastructure
•	 The location has limited sunlight at times throughout the year 
(take winter into consideration)
Solar works best when
•	 The site has open sky exposure
•	 There is minimal shading from trees or buildings
•	 The location has good year-round sun access 
(take winter into consideration)
Visibility & 
Performance Tips
•	 The camera should have a clear line of sight to vehicle plates
•	 Max distance 75ft to target for outpost, max 120ft to target for lightpost
•	 Avoid placing cameras where reflective road signs are in direct camera 
field of view
•	 Choose locations with minimal visual clutter in field of view

Ownership & Permitting 
Knowing who controls the site early helps avoid delays. If ownership is 
unclear, no problem, just make a note of it.
•	 Pole ownership 
•	 City-owned sites often move fastest
•	 State- or DOT-controlled roads will require additional approvals
•	 Private property requires owner permission
What to Share 
with Your Axon Team
When submitting sites, please include the following information. You 
should receive a template to complete this; if you have not, please let your 
Axon Outpost or Lightpost representative know and one will be provided.
•	 Address or GPS (Latitude, Longitude) pin
•	 Notes on existing pole and power supply (if known)
•	 Road name and traffic direction of target lanes
•	 Confirmation that the agency supports the location
•	 Photos of the site, if available
