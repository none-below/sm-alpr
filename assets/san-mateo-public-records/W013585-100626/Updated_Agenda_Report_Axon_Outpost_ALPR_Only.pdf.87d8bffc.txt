SUBJECT
Authorize the City Manager to Execute an Amendment to the Police Department's Axon Agreement to Include Axon Outpost
Fixed Automated License Plate Reader Cameras for an 18-Month Trial
RECOMMENDATION
Staff recommends Council authorize the City Manager or designee to execute Amendment No. [INSERT AMENDMENT 
NUMBER] to the Police Department's Axon Master Services and Purchasing Agreement to include Axon Outpost fixed 
Automated License Plate Reader (ALPR) cameras for an initial 18-month trial; authorize the Chief of Police to implement the
phased deployment in substantially the form presented; and require staff to return to the City Council with the results of the 
evaluation before the City incurs a long-term payment obligation or selects a permanent fixed ALPR solution.
BACKGROUND
The San Mateo Police Department (SMPD) has utilized Axon technology for many years, including body-worn cameras, 
Fleet 3 in-car video and mobile ALPR, Axon Evidence, and Axon Fusus. These systems are used by officers in the field and 
by personnel assigned to the Real Time Intelligence Center. Axon recently released fixed-camera ALPR technology that 
operates within the same software environment and is designed to improve efficiency by bringing alerts, live information, 
investigative searches, digital evidence, permissions, retention, and auditing into a common platform.
SMPD evaluated fixed ALPR products from Axon Enterprise, Inc., Flock Safety, and Motorola Solutions. Motorola presented 
the lowest normalized core-camera cost, and Flock offered a mature and rapidly deployable fixed-camera network. Axon 
received the strongest overall assessment because it integrates with the Department's current technology, reduces the need
for separate accounts and evidence exports, and provides a unified operational and governance workflow. An 18-month trial 
will allow SMPD to validate that assessment under actual San Mateo conditions before recommending a long-term solution.
The Police Department complies with California Civil Code Sections 1798.90.5 et seq. regarding ALPR technology. Public 
agencies must maintain appropriate usage and privacy policies and provide an opportunity for public comment at a regularly 
scheduled public meeting before implementing an ALPR program. The proposed trial will operate under SMPD policy, 
applicable state law, and the Department's existing authorization and audit requirements.
DISCUSSION
ALPR is an investigative and public-safety tool that captures license plate and vehicle information from vehicles traveling on 
public roadways. The technology can assist with stolen vehicles, wanted or missing persons, serious crimes, organized retail
theft, and other authorized investigations. An ALPR alert is an investigative lead only. Officers are required to visually 
confirm the plate and verify the information through an independent law-enforcement source before taking enforcement 
action.
Axon Outpost is a compact, solar-capable fixed ALPR camera designed for public-safety use. The system provides real-time
plate alerts and searchable vehicle information through Axon Vehicle Intelligence. Outpost integrates with Axon Fusus and 
Axon Evidence, allowing authorized personnel to use the same environment as the Department's existing Fleet 3 mobile 
ALPR, body-worn camera, in-car video, and digital evidence systems. This integration is expected to improve workflow 
efficiency, reduce separate accounts and evidence transfers, and support consistent access, retention, and audit controls.
The updated Axon quote is limited to Outpost ALPR cameras and does not include Lightpost PTZ cameras. The pricing 
schedule includes 54 paid Outpost Technology Assurance Plan (TAP) plans, five additional Outpost plans and subsequent 
extension plans at no net charge, 59 ALPR software licenses and installation packages, and a delivery schedule listing 60 
Outpost cameras. The proposal also includes solar panels, battery and charger enclosures, poles, installation, maintenance,

exact number of deployed cameras and any spare equipment, installation locations, implementation schedule, and

Axon has proposed competitive pricing and a phased approach that places the first scheduled payment after the proposed 
evaluation period. During the initial 18 months, SMPD will evaluate reliability, plate-read performance, day and night 
operation, alert latency, cellular and solar performance, maintenance response, investigative value, administrative workload, 
and integration with Fusus and Evidence. Staff will also review user compliance, audit results, data retention, sharing 
controls, community feedback, and the ability to produce meaningful public reports.
The trial will give SMPD the opportunity to compare Axon's performance and total operational value with the Flock and 
Motorola products previously evaluated. At the conclusion of the 18-month period, staff will return to the City Council with the
results and a recommendation to continue with Axon, select another vendor, modify the deployment, or discontinue the fixed
ALPR program. This approach allows the Department and the community to evaluate the technology before the City makes 
a long-term commitment.
All security and policy requirements governing the Department's current ALPR program will apply to the trial. Access will be 
limited to authorized users for legitimate law-enforcement purposes; searches and system activity will be logged and 
audited; non-evidentiary information will be deleted in accordance with SMPD retention policy; and any sharing will be 
restricted by Department policy and California law. The trial does not expand the authorized uses of ALPR information.
BUDGET IMPACT
The updated Axon quote identifies a 99-month program beginning October 15, 2026 and ending January 14, 2035, with a 
net contract cost of $1,137,974.48 and estimated tax of $31,566.52, for a total contract value of $1,169,541.00. The quote 
reflects a list price of $2,220,466.71 and total savings of $1,082,492.23, or 48.75 percent. The payment schedule begins 
June 1, 2028, with annual payments increasing from $148,075.15 in 2028 to $187,362.26 in 2034, including estimated tax. 
No payment is scheduled during the initial evaluation period.
The quote was issued July 20, 2026 and is valid through September 29, 2026. If Council consideration or execution occurs 
after that date, staff will obtain an updated quote and final agreement language acceptable to the City Attorney. The 
amendment must preserve the City's ability to discontinue the program at the conclusion of the trial without incurring the 
remaining long-term charges. If the trial is successful and the City Council authorizes continuation, future payments will be 
included in the Police Department's operating budget through the annual budget process. Any increase above the quoted 
amount or any long-term obligation not authorized by this action will be returned to the City Council for approval.
ENVIRONMENTAL DETERMINATION
This action is categorically exempt from the California Environmental Quality Act under CEQA Guidelines Sections 15301 
and 15303 because it consists of minor alterations to existing public facilities and the installation of small public-safety 
equipment. The final environmental determination will be confirmed based on the approved installation locations.
