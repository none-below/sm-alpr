Chief Barberini, Lt. Casazza, Ms. Maravillas, and Assistant City Attorney Abdollahi,
Thank you for taking the time to meet on Thursday, June 4, 2026; I've been traveling, so apologies for taking some time to get
these meeting notes sent out. I was encouraged to hear that you are making changes to help keep our data of our city’s citizens
safe!
So that we are all working from the same record, below is my understanding of what was discussed. If I have misstated or left
anything out, please let me know and I will correct it.
The City's recent sharing changes
- Lt. Casazza stated that, as of June 3, 2026, the City stopped sharing data with El Cajon.
- Chief Barberini stated that the City has recently stopped sharing with colleges and universities.
- Chief Barberini and Lt. Casazza stated that the City's sharing with the University of the Pacific (UOP) was a mistake and was
live only briefly — on the order of two weeks.
- Lt. Casazza noted that he is in regular contact with Flock and met with Flock at the department in recent days to discuss these
issues.
How the City learns of problems with other agencies
- Chief Barberini stated that the City learned of El Cajon's legal problems recently, through news reporting.
- Chief Barberini stated that the City learned of Mountain View PD's problems through news reporting.
- Chief Barberini stated that the City does not proactively assess whether other agencies meet San Mateo's standards; it relies on
each agency to set and follow its own standards and the law, and typically learns of issues through the news.
- Charlotte noted that the news sources she follows often surface these issues months before the City becomes aware, and asked how
a resident could bring a potential issue to the City's attention. Chief Barberini responded that a resident could email him, Lt.
Casazza, and Ms. Maravillas, and the department would consider it, though he would not want material that had been entered into an
AI tool.
Federal and out-of-state sharing
- Chief Barberini stated that San Mateo's data has never been shared with federal or out-of-state agencies.
- I noted that Stockton PD ran approximately 120 searches over the past three months on behalf of the U.S. Marshals Service, that
those searches would have reached San Mateo data, and that Yuba City performed at least one search on behalf of the DEA.
- I also noted that San Francisco was recently reported to have repeatedly run searches on behalf of federal agencies.
- Lt. Casazza stated that searches other agencies run against San Mateo's data do not appear in SMPD's audit logs.
San Francisco
- Chief Barberini stated that San Francisco is critical to the City's enforcement practices, and that the City would not stop
sharing data with San Francisco under any circumstances, even if San Francisco were sharing with federal agencies.
The UOP authorization
- I noted that only two people were authorized to make the UOP sharing change — the Chief and the ALPR captain (Lt. Casazza) — and
asked how Phoenix O'Rourke had permission to add UOP.
- Lt. Casazza responded that the ALPR captain role has been held by several people and may have changed during the relevant
period, and that he does not recall who held the role at which times.
- Assistant City Attorney Abdollahi added that the ALPR captain role has changed multiple times.
Responsibility for downstream use of San Mateo data
- I described the recent Costa Mesa case, in which a suspended officer retained Flock access and used it to track a former partner
across jurisdictions, and asked whether the City's measures would protect a San Mateo resident in a comparable situation.
- Chief Barberini stated that the City cannot be responsible for what other agencies do and cannot reasonably protect its
residents when another agency fails to follow the law; that other agencies are outside San Mateo's jurisdiction; and that
enforcement of a violation by another agency falls to that agency.
- Chief Barberini stated that it would not be appropriate to remove an agency's access (such as Costa Mesa's) based on a limited
breach such as a suspended user retaining Flock access.
- Ms. Maravillas noted that her role includes account offboarding for SMPD, and mused whether an offboarding oversight on SMPD's
part should make the department ineligible to receive Flock data from other agencies.
- Chief Barberini stated that where another agency repeatedly and consistently violates the law (as with El Cajon), the City may
consider removing that agency's access, and that the City discovers such cases through the news.
The scope of data sharing
- I stated that the city’s sharing network means its data is directly searchable by roughly 100,000 sworn officers and civilians
at the remote agencies
- Chief Barberini agreed that the number is roughly what he would expect
- I asked whether the City needs to send its data to other cities in order to solve San Mateo crimes.
- Chief Barberini stated that the City must share its data broadly so that other cities share theirs in return, that this is
critical to investigations, and that the tools do not work without broad sharing.
- I identified cities and cases where sharing is not reciprocal — San Mateo shares with cities that do not return data, and other
cities share with San Mateo without San Mateo sharing back.
- Chief Barberini stated that the City must nonetheless share its data with other cities, including distant ones, so that it can

--- page 2 ---
investigate crimes.
- I asked whether hotlists — allowing other agencies to alert only on specific plates of interest, and allowing our city to
receive proactive notification if a wanted vehicle from another city enters ours — would be sufficient, rather than allowing other
agencies to search all plate data, criminal and non-criminal.
- Chief Barberini stated that hotlists are local to each agency, that ALPR data has been determined not to present a Fourth
Amendment issue, and that the City will continue broad sharing of plate data with police agencies.
Compliance
- Assistant City Attorney Bahar Abdollahi stated that, to her knowledge, the City's programs are in compliance with the law.
- I noted that my own review indicates otherwise, and that I would send my findings document after the meeting for the
department's review.
As noted at the meeting, I sent my findings document immediately afterward, and I would appreciate confirmation that it was
received. If any of the above does not match your recollection, please let me know by July 6, 2026 and I will correct the record;
if I have not heard back by then, I will treat these notes as an accurate summary of our meeting.
Thank you,
Brian
