Overview

Blue Springs MO PD uses Flock Safety technology to capture objective evidence without compromising on individual privacy. Blue Springs MO PD utilizes retroactive search to solve crimes after they've occurred. Additionally, Blue Springs MO PD utilizes real time alerting of hotlist vehicles to capture wanted criminals. In an effort to ensure proper usage and guardrails are in place, they have made the below policies and usage statistics available to the public.

Community Impact
Featured Success Stories

Escaped Prisoner out of Tennessee serving life in Prison for Murder Arrested

11/03/2026, BSPD officers were contacted by a detective out of Illinois advising a stolen vehicle that contained multiple weapons including two rifles and a shotgun, possibly driven by an escaped prisoner from Tennessee was seen on one of our Flock LPR Cameras. The escaped prisoner was identified as Jacob Allen Morley, serving a life sentence out of Tennessee for Murder 1st degree. It was determined that the vehicle was last seen by the LPR camera at the Lowes Home Improvement in Kansas City at 40 Hwy and Sterling. Officers had dispatch notify Independence and Kansas City of the wanted vehicle and possible suspect. BSPD Detectives sent an email to the KC Metro list advising surrounding jurisdictions of the vehicle and possible escaped prisoner. At approximately 0958 hours BSPD officers updated the Flock search for a more current location. BSPD officers called Missouri Highway Patrol and provided an updated location at Interstate 435 and Parvin Road. Shortly there after the vehicle was located in Clay County. Missouri State Highway Patrol, Kansas City Missouri PD along with Clay County Sheriff’s Department and the US Marshalls followed the suspect vehicle until they were able to take the suspect into custody in Ray County without incident.

The metro investigation on 11/03/26 originated in Blue Springs due to sharing LPR images with other jurisdictions. BSPD officers did an excellent job sharing intelligence with surrounding jurisdictions which led to the successful apprehension of an escaped prisoner within two hours of first being notified. The suspect, Mr. Jacob A. Morley, was serving a life sentence for Murder 1st degree out of Tennessee. No officers or citizens were injured during the apprehension on November 3, 2026.

Ulta Beauty Burglary - Success Story

In this case, a suspect burglarized the Ulta Beauty store located in the Adams Dairy Landing shopping center, resulting in a loss exceeding $11,000. Through the use of Flock Safety License Plate Reader (LPR) cameras, officers and detectives were able to identify the suspect vehicle and obtain a license plate number.

As detectives and our crime analyst coordinated with Ulta Beauty Loss Prevention and partner agencies, the investigation ultimately linked the same suspect to 15 additional Ulta Beauty burglaries across six states. The combined total loss from these incidents was approximately $300,000. The investigative lead generated by the Flock LPR cameras was instrumental in identifying the suspect, leading to an arrest and the filing of criminal charges.

Credit Card Skimming - Success Story

In January 2024, a citizen reported that a credit card reader at a Hy-Vee gas station pump was hanging by wires. Officers determined that the device was not the original manufacturer-installed equipment, but rather a skimming device placed with the intent to unlawfully capture customers’ credit card information.

Patrol officers conducted checks at additional gas stations in the area and located several more skimming devices. While reviewing surveillance footage from one of the locations, officers identified a suspect vehicle; however, the license plate was not readable. By utilizing Flock Safety LPR cameras in the area, officers were able to identify the vehicle. This information led to the location of the vehicle and the execution of a search warrant at a related residence, where additional skimming equipment and computers were seized. The suspect was ultimately linked to additional crimes, formally charged, and the case was turned over to federal authorities for prosecution.

The Flock Safety LPR cameras deployed in Blue Springs have also assisted in numerous other investigations, including homicides, missing persons cases, thefts, and hit-and-run incidents. The examples outlined above represent just two instances demonstrating how the Blue Springs Police Department has effectively leveraged Flock Safety LPR technology to identify suspects, support investigations, and enhance public safety within our community.

Domestic Assault - Success Story

On 03/01/2026, officers were dispatched to a residence regarding a reported violent domestic assault that had just occurred. The victim advised that the suspect fled the scene in a vehicle and provided the suspect’s name. Officers utilized the department’s computer system to locate a possible license plate number. The license plate was entered into the Flock Safety camera system, which indicated the vehicle traveling northbound on a nearby highway.

Based on investigative information, the suspect was believed to be traveling toward another residence. An officer positioned themselves in a nearby business and waited for the vehicle to pass. Upon locating the vehicle the officer activated emergency lights, and conducted a traffic stop.

The suspect was successfully apprehended without incident. The suspect was held pending the following charges:
• Domestic Assault 3rd Degree
• Domestic Assault 4th Degree
• Endangering the Welfare of a Child
• Harassment 1st Degree
• Property Damage 2nd Degree

Had it not been for the information provided by the Flock Safety System the suspect likely would not have been located as quickly as they were. The successful apprehension provided immediate safety to the juvenile and adult victim.

Package Theft from a Front Porch

06/17/26-Officers were dispatched to a stealing in the 3400 block of NW Duncan. Approximately $1,000 worth of items were stolen from the victims front porch. Officers were able to use Flock LPR to identify the car to which they identified the owner. Officers responded to the suspect's address and made contact with a female that admitted to taking the items. The suspect allowed officers to recover all stolen items valued at $1,000. The victim declined prosecution after recovering their property but the suspect was arrested on multiple warrants.

Myth vs. Fact
Myth

Flock has been hacked.

Fact

Flock's cloud environment has never been breached. Flock invests in a strong security posture, including adhering to NDAA, SOC2 (Type II), ISO 27001, ISO 27018 FedRAMP 20x, and Higher Education Community Vendor Assessment Tool (HECVAT) compliance. Additional security precautions include Role-Based Access Controls, data encrypted at-rest and in transit, required Multi-Factor Authentication, and regular penetration testing.

Myth

Flock sells and/or shares customer data.

Fact

Your community owns 100% of your data and it will never be sold. Only your agency decides who to share your data with, not Flock. Flock Safety does not publicly release agency search data. Flock does not operate or support any public-facing searchable database of law enforcement lookups, and we do not provide license plate search histories to websites or third parties without the agency's knowledge and authorization. Other agencies who you share footage with have been responding to FOIA requests and sharing this search data.

Myth

My data is fed into a national database.

Fact

Each agency controls access to its own data. Agencies may choose to share information through a broader network, but nothing is shared automatically. Participation is opt-in, governed by defined agreements, and limited to lawful investigative purposes.

Myth

Flock cameras use facial recognition.

Fact

Flock cameras do not use facial recognition technology. Searches are based on vehicle characteristics, and not identity. Flock cannot recognize, identify, or track individuals. It is purpose built for specific public safety investigations, not to monitor people.

RTCC Policy

Subject: 80.1 Real-Time Crime Center

This order consists of the following sections:

80.1.1 Real-Time Crime Center

80.1.1 REAL-TIME CRIME CENTER

Purpose: The Blue Springs Police Department Real-Time Crime Center (RTCC) supports law enforcement operations by providing real-time intelligence, enhancing situational awareness, and improving response to calls for service. The RTCC is intended to promote officer safety and crime reduction by sharing critical information and data among internal departments as well as local, state, and federal law enforcement partners.

Scope: All personnel assigned to or authorized to utilize the Real-Time Crime Center (RTCC), including RTCC operators/analysts, supervisors, and other department personnel with approved access to RTCC systems shall abide by the following policy and procedures that guide RTCC operations, including real-time incident support, information gathering and dissemination, investigative support, and analytical functions. RTCC technologies and systems may include the use of CAD, RMS, License Plate Reader (LPR) systems, camera networks, and other integrated data platforms. RTCC operations shall include the coordination and sharing of information between the RTCC and patrol personnel, investigative units, command staff, and external local, state, and federal law enforcement partners, as permitted by law and departmental policy.

DEFINITIONS:

Real Time Crime Center – A centralized unit that utilizes technology and data analysis to provide real-time intelligence to law enforcement personnel.

CAD (Computer-Aided Dispatch) – A system used to manage calls for service and dispatch law enforcement resources.

RMS (Records Management System) – A database used to store reports, records, and law enforcement data.

sUAS (small Unmanned Aircraft System) – A drone and its associated equipment used for law enforcement operations, to include using a drone as a first responder.

LPR (License Plate Reader) – A system that captures and analyzes vehicle license plate information.

A. RTCC USE

The Blue Springs Police Department has established the use of a Real-Time Crime Center (RTCC) to:

1. Enhance officer safety by providing real-time intelligence during critical incidents, traffic stops, pursuits, and high-risk calls.

2. Improve operational effectiveness by reducing response times and investigative delays through immediate access to integrated data and technology.

3. Support investigations by assisting with suspect identification and case development to increase case clearance rates.

4. Enable proactive policing by identifying crime patterns, repeat offenders, and emerging threats.

5. Ensure responsible and effective use of technology in accordance with legal, ethical, and departmental standards.

B. RTCC OPERATIONS

1. The Real Time Crime Center (RTCC) operates as a centralized support unit providing real-time intelligence and operational assistance to law enforcement personnel. All RTCC activities shall be conducted in accordance with applicable federal, state, and local laws, as well as departmental policy.

2. RTCC personnel shall utilize authorized systems and technologies to support legitimate law enforcement activities. All information, resources, and recorded media accessed, analyzed, or disseminated by the RTCC will be used strictly for official purposes and retained in accordance with BSPD policy. Unauthorized use of systems or data is prohibited.

3. Information provided by the RTCC will be timely, accurate, and relevant to the incident or operational needs. Personnel shall exercise sound judgment in evaluating and communicating information, ensuring clarity while avoiding speculation.

4. RTCC personnel shall maintain the confidentiality of sensitive information and ensure access to RTCC systems is limited to authorized users.

5. RTCC personnel shall coordinate with patrol, investigative units, command staff, and partner agencies as appropriate to support operations and information sharing.

C. CALL MONITORING AND PRIORITIZATION

1. RTCC personnel shall maintain continuous monitoring of active calls for service and related data systems to identify incidents requiring operational support.

2. RTCC personnel will prioritize incidents based on severity, threat level, and potential impact on public and officer safety. Priority incidents include, but are not limited to:

a. Violent crimes in progress

b. Officer-involved incidents or emergencies

c. Vehicle pursuits

d. Weapons-related calls

e, Missing or endangered persons

f. Major incidents, traffic accidents or critical events

g. any other incident deemed a priority based on mitigating circumstances

3. Upon identification of a priority incident, RTCC personnel shall initiate real-time monitoring and information support efforts, including reviewing CAD activity, monitoring radio traffic, and utilizing available technology to assist responding units.

4. RTCC personnel shall provide timely updates as incidents evolve and adjust priorities based on changing conditions and operational needs.

D. INFORMATION GATHERING AND DISSEMINATION

1. RTCC personnel shall gather, analyze, and disseminate information to support active incidents, investigations, and operational needs.

2. Operators shall utilize authorized systems and technologies, including but not limited to:

a. CAD and RMS

b. License Plate Reader (LPR) systems

c. Camera systems (fixed, mobile, and partner-owned)

d. Field interview and intelligence databases

e. External databases and open-source intelligence (OSINT)

3. Information gathering efforts shall focus on identifying relevant persons, vehicles, locations, associates, and potential risks.

4. RTCC personnel shall disseminate relevant information to field personnel, supervisors, and partner agencies using approved communication methods, including CAD messaging, radio communication, and direct contact when appropriate.

5. Information shared shall be clear, concise, and operationally relevant, with priority given to actionable intelligence that supports officer safety, decision-making, and incident resolution.

6. RTCC personnel shall ensure information is reasonably verified prior to dissemination and will clearly communicate any limitations; operators will use sound judgment to avoid unnecessary or redundant transmissions during active incidents.

E. RTCC OPERATOR AND SUPERVISOR RESPONSIBILITIES

1. Operators shall provide real-time incident support by:

a. Monitoring CAD and radio traffic to identify incidents requiring RTCC support.

b. Conducting database queries related to persons, vehicles, locations, and associates.

c. Utilizing LPR’s, camera systems, and mapping tools to assist in locating suspects or vehicles.

d. Providing timely updates to field personnel, supervisors, and command staff.

e. Supporting incident coordination, including pursuits and major events.

2. Operators shall provide investigative support by:

a. Conducting follow-up analysis for ongoing investigations.

b. Identifying patterns, associates, and relevant intelligence using available data systems.

c. Assisting with timelines, mapping, and case development.

d. Supporting detectives and coordinating with partner agencies as appropriate.

3. Operators shall provide proactive crime analysis by:

a. Identifying crime trends, patterns, and emerging issues.

b. Supporting directed patrols and operational planning.

c. Producing bulletins, BOLOs, maps, summaries, and officer safety notifications as needed and in conjunction with the Crime Analyst.

4. Operators shall provide technology and system support by:

a. Utilizing RTCC technologies effectively and in accordance with policy.

b. Assisting with system use, training, and best practices as needed.

c. Identifying system limitations or issues and report them to appropriate personnel.

5. On-Duty Patrol Supervisors shall oversee daily RTCC operations, ensure compliance with departmental policy, and provide guidance and direction to RTCC personnel.

6. RTCC Supervisors shall also monitor system usage, review operational effectiveness, and address any issues related to RTCC functions or personnel.

F. sUAS (DRONE) OPERATIONS

The RTCC utilizes small Unmanned Aircraft Systems (sUAS), or Drones, to provide aerial support for real-time incident response, situational awareness, and operational coordination.

1. Drone operations may include a Drone as First Responder (DFR) model, where drones are deployed to calls for service to support responding personnel.

2. Drones may be deployed for, but are not limited to:

a. In-progress or high-risk calls.

b. Suspect searches and perimeter support.

c. Vehicle pursuits and fleeing suspects.

d. Missing or endangered persons.

e. Crime scene assessment and situational awareness.

f. Major incidents to include traffic accidents and other special events.

g. any other incident deemed appropriate.

3. RTCC personnel shall initiate UAS deployments based on call priority, operational need, and available resources. DFR deployments may be initiated proactively in response to qualifying calls for service.

4. Drones may be operated in an automated or pre-programmed flight mode, with the ability for operators to assume manual control at any time. Operators will continuously monitor flight operations and adjust deployment as needed to support field personnel.

a. In the event that an internet or power outage occurs, the RTCC drone can be operated through a mobile app and should only be used to immediately return the drone to its docking station or landed safely at the secondary landing pad. The drone should not continue any flights until power has been restored and a safety inspection has been conducted.

5. All drone operations will be conducted in accordance with Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) regulations, including Part 107 requirements, as well as departmental policy.

6. Drone deployments and activities shall be documented in accordance with department policy, including flight logs and any required reporting.

G. DOCUMENTATION AND REPORTING

1. RTCC personnel shall document actions taken in support of incidents, investigations, and operational activities in accordance with department policy.

2. Documentation shall be completed using appropriate systems, including CAD entries, RTCC logs, and other designated reporting platforms. Entries shall accurately reflect relevant actions, information obtained, and intelligence disseminated.

3. All documentation and reporting shall be clear, concise, and completed in a timely manner to support operational needs, accountability, and evidentiary requirements.

H. RTCC OPERATOR TRAINING

1. All RTCC Operators shall attend the following training as pre-service training (ICS training is through either the FEMA National Incident Command System training portal or in-person classes provided locally):

a. ICS 200

b. ICS 300

c. ICS 800

d. ICS 914

e. sUAS Part 107 training and certification

2. In-Service training shall be completed on as needed basis, may possibly include in-service training days with the department’s drone unit.
