Overview

Cathedral City CA PD uses Flock Safety technology to capture objective evidence without compromising on individual privacy. Cathedral City CA PD utilizes retroactive search to solve crimes after they've occurred. Additionally, Cathedral City CA PD utilizes real time alerting of hotlist vehicles to capture wanted criminals. In an effort to ensure proper usage and guardrails are in place, they have made the below policies and usage statistics available to the public.

Federal Hate Crime Investigation – Suspect Apprehended and Sentenced to 35 Years

On February 15, 2023, Jaime Tran intentionally targeted members of the Jewish community after researching neighborhoods with kosher markets and synagogues. Believing he would find Jewish victims in the Pico-Robertson area of Los Angeles, Tran shot a man wearing a yarmulke as he left religious services, intending to kill him. The following morning, Tran returned to the same neighborhood and shot a second Jewish victim under similar circumstances before fleeing.

Following the shootings, law enforcement agencies throughout Southern California were alerted to be on the lookout for the suspect and his vehicle. Cathedral City Police Department's Flock Safety camera system detected the suspect vehicle entering Cathedral City, immediately providing officers with actionable intelligence that the suspect had traveled into the city.

Using this information, Cathedral City Police officers began proactively searching for the suspect. While conducting those efforts, officers were dispatched to a separate emergency call involving a subject firing a rifle into the air on East Palm Canyon Drive. Officers quickly determined the individual was the same suspect responsible for the hate-motivated shootings in Los Angeles.

Tran was safely taken into custody without further violence, preventing additional potential victims and bringing a dangerous offender to justice. He was later prosecuted in federal court and sentenced to 35 years in federal prison for the hate-motivated shootings.

This case demonstrates how Flock Safety technology can provide real-time investigative leads that enable officers to rapidly locate violent offenders, improving both public safety and officer response while helping prevent additional acts of violence.

Murder Investigation – Three Suspects Identified and Arrested
Case #2602C-2240

On February 17, 2026, Cathedral City Police officers responded to a shooting in the 30900 block of San Gabriel Circle. Upon arrival, officers located an adult male suffering from multiple gunshot wounds. Despite lifesaving efforts by responding Fire Department personnel, the victim was pronounced deceased at the scene.
Detectives immediately began a homicide investigation. Although investigators obtained a description of the suspect vehicle, the identities and whereabouts of those responsible remained unknown.

Using Cathedral City's Flock Safety camera network, investigators quickly identified the suspect vehicle and were able to reconstruct its path of travel immediately following the homicide. Flock camera data showed the vehicle leaving Cathedral City and traveling toward San Diego, providing detectives with critical investigative leads during the earliest stages of the investigation.

Armed with this information, detectives coordinated with allied law enforcement agencies in the San Diego area, rapidly focusing investigative efforts on the suspects' location. The ability to trace the vehicle's movements significantly reduced the time required to identify those responsible and helped preserve valuable investigative momentum.

By combining Flock camera data with witness interviews, physical evidence, surveillance footage, and traditional investigative techniques, detectives identified all three suspects. The investigation ultimately resulted in the arrest of all three individuals, who were subsequently charged with murder.

This case demonstrates how Flock Safety technology can provide investigators with timely, objective vehicle intelligence that helps reconstruct a suspect's movements, identify investigative leads, coordinate with partner agencies, and bring violent offenders to justice more quickly.

Murder Investigation – 13-Year-Old Victim
Case #2508C-1880

On August 13, 2025, Cathedral City Police officers responded to a shooting near Whispering Palms Trail and Mission Indian Trail. Officers located a 13-year-old boy suffering from gunshot wounds. Fire Department personnel immediately began emergency medical treatment; however, due to the severity of his injuries, the victim was tragically pronounced deceased at the scene.

Detectives launched an extensive homicide investigation. Initial witness information provided only a general description of the suspect vehicle, leaving investigators with limited information during the critical early stages of the case.

Through the use of Cathedral City's Flock Safety camera system, detectives identified a vehicle consistent with witness descriptions and were able to analyze its travel patterns through the city. This information provided investigators with valuable leads that helped narrow the suspect pool and identify those responsible.

Working in conjunction with additional investigative techniques, detectives ultimately identified and arrested two suspects, both of whom were charged with murder.

This investigation highlights the value of automated license plate recognition technology in rapidly developing investigative leads during violent crime investigations. By helping investigators identify suspect vehicles within minutes rather than days, Flock Safety technology can significantly improve investigative efficiency while increasing the likelihood of a successful resolution.

Recovering Stolen Vehicles and Protecting Property
In addition to assisting with major violent crime investigations, Cathedral City's Flock Safety camera system has become an invaluable tool in recovering stolen vehicles and returning them to their rightful owners.

Since deployment, the system has helped officers locate and recover numerous stolen vehicles with a combined value exceeding $250,000. In many cases, Flock cameras alerted officers to stolen vehicles entering or traveling through Cathedral City, allowing officers to safely locate and recover the vehicles before they were stripped, damaged, or transported elsewhere.

Beyond the financial impact, recovering stolen vehicles often means victims regain essential transportation needed for work, school, medical appointments, and family responsibilities. The rapid identification of stolen vehicles also contributes to the arrest of offenders responsible for auto theft and other associated crimes.
Technology That Supports Investigations

Flock Safety cameras are not used to replace police officers or detectives—they are investigative tools that enhance their ability to solve crimes and protect the community. Information generated by the camera system is used alongside witness statements, physical evidence, surveillance footage, officer observations, and other investigative methods to develop leads and support criminal investigations.

By providing timely, objective vehicle information, Flock Safety technology allows officers and detectives to respond more quickly, identify suspects more efficiently, recover stolen property, and solve crimes that might otherwise remain unsolved. Most importantly, the technology helps improve public safety while supporting Cathedral City Police Department's commitment to transparency, accountability, and the responsible use of technology.

Policy & Trust

What's Detected

License Plates, Vehicles

What's Not Detected

Facial recognition, People, Gender, Race

Acceptable Use Policy

Data is used for law enforcement purposes only. Data is owned by Cathedral City CA PD and is never sold to 3rd parties.

Prohibited Uses

Immigration enforcement, traffic enforcement, harrassment or intimidation, usage based solely on a protected class (i.e. race, sex, religion), Personal use.

Access Policy

All system access requires a valid reason and is stored indefinitely.

Hotlist Policy

Hotlist hits are required to be human verified prior to action.
