Overview

We aim to leverage the use of Automated License Plate Recognition (ALPR) technology for the good of the public, using it as a reactive measure to solve crimes and keep the community safe. Since the implementation of ALPR systems, the City of Chula Vista embarked on an evaluation of the use of ALPR systems, resulting in CVPD’s current analysis of its use and commitment to transparency. We are dedicated to developing policies and guidelines that balance community safety with privacy rights and respect community concerns. We have made the below policies and usage statistics available to the public.

Acceptable Use Policy

Data utilized must be for lawful law enforcement investigations only. Data is not accessible to private entities. CVPD owns all the data contained in the ALPR database and does not sell that data. CVPD does not share any ALPR data with private companies (including those companies in California) that may have a contract with Flock Safety. CVPD does not allow those private companies to share their ALPR data with the CVPD.

Prohibited Uses

Prohibited usages include, but are not limited to immigration enforcement, traffic enforcement, harassment or intimidation, usage based solely on a protected class (i.e. race, sex, religion), and personal use. CVPD also reserves the right to discontinue sharing and allowing a specific California law enforcement agency from access to our ALPR data if that agency is found to be violating state law in terms of ALPR data sharing (SB-34) or found to have violated SB-54, the California Values Act regarding the utilization of ALPR data for immigration enforcement.

Access Policy

Any CVPD employee who has been trained on ALPR Technology and has read/understood/acknowledged CVPD Policy 460 may access the database. Any search requires a valid law enforcement reason. All searches must include an incident number or a case number. All searches are stored indefinitely and weekly audits on those search reasons are conducted by ALPR Administrators to ensure strict adherence to California law and CVPD policy.

Hotlist Policy

When an alert is received by a Dispatcher or Officer, verification must occur before taking any form of enforcement action. The vehicle must be confirmed stolen or wanted, the vehicle’s features must match what is shown in the photo, and upon locating that vehicle of interest, an officer must reverify the information to a Dispatcher.
