Overview

Citrus Heights CA PD uses Flock Safety technology to capture objective evidence without compromising on individual privacy. Citrus Heights CA PD utilizes retroactive search to solve crimes after they've occurred. Additionally, Citrus Heights CA PD utilizes real time alerting of hotlist vehicles to capture wanted criminals. In an effort to ensure proper usage and guardrails are in place, they have made the below policies and usage statistics available to the public.

Community Impact

Featured Success Stories

Armed Suspects Located Through ALPR and Regional RTIC Partnership

On August 9, 2026, after several adults attempted to force entry into a Citrus Heights apartment and one was seen with a firearm, the group fled in a vehicle before officers arrived. Using CHPD’s Real-Time Information Center and Automated License Plate Reader technology, personnel quickly identified the suspect vehicle, a blue Tesla, and determined it had traveled to Elk Grove. CHPD coordinated with the Elk Grove Police Department, which located and safely stopped the vehicle. Three adults were arrested, and a loaded handgun was recovered from inside. This case demonstrates how ALPR technology and regional law enforcement partnerships help CHPD quickly identify and locate vehicles connected to serious crimes, coordinate across jurisdictional boundaries, and safely apprehend potentially dangerous individuals. By reducing the time between a crime occurring and suspects being located, this technology helps protect victims, prevent additional crimes, and make the Citrus Heights community safer.

Featured Success Story: Flock Alert Leads Officers to Vehicle Connected to Statewide Jewelry Thefts

Sept. 1, 2026: Officers received a Flock alert for a vehicle associated with multiple jewelry store thefts occurring throughout California. Officers located the vehicle, but briefly lost sight of it in heavy traffic before later finding it parked and unoccupied outside a retail store. Working with the Real-Time Information Center (RTIC), officers determined that several occupants had left the vehicle and continued on foot. IMPACT Detectives responded to assist with the investigation and located one of the individuals nearby. Using surveillance video and other investigative information, detectives identified the individual as the primary suspect in the theft. He was arrested and transported to jail. The vehicle was impounded as evidence for further processing (CH26-06167). This case demonstrates how automated license plate reader technology, combined with real-time intelligence and coordinated investigative follow-up, can help officers locate vehicles connected to crimes occurring across multiple jurisdictions and develop actionable leads.

Featured Success Story: CHPD Uses Real-Time Technology & Community Camera Network to Locate Missing Senior

A 79-year-old woman experiencing cognitive decline walked away from her home and could not be located by her family, prompting an urgent response from the Citrus Heights Police Department. Given her medical condition and vulnerability, locating her quickly became a top priority. CHPD officers, detectives, dispatchers, and the Real Time Information Center (RTIC) worked together to develop leads and track her possible direction of travel. Using information gathered through the community camera network, personnel were able to narrow the search area and assist officers in locating her approximately 1.5 miles from her home. She was found safely in just under two hours and reunited with her family. This incident demonstrates how coordinated police response, real-time information, and community camera technology can help officers quickly locate vulnerable missing persons and bring them home safely.

Myth vs. Fact

Myth

Flock has been hacked.

Fact

Flock's cloud environment has never been breached. Flock invests in a strong security posture, including adhering to NDAA, SOC2 (Type II), ISO 27001, ISO 27018 FedRAMP 20x, and Higher Education Community Vendor Assessment Tool (HECVAT) compliance. Additional security precautions include Role-Based Access Controls, data encrypted at-rest and in transit, required Multi-Factor Authentication, and regular penetration testing.

Myth

Flock sells and/or shares customer data.

Fact

Your community owns 100% of your data and it will never be sold. Only your agency decides who to share your data with, not Flock. Flock Safety does not publicly release agency search data. Flock does not operate or support any public-facing searchable database of law enforcement lookups, and we do not provide license plate search histories to websites or third parties without the agency's knowledge and authorization. Other agencies who you share footage with have been responding to FOIA requests and sharing this search data.

Myth

My data is fed into a national database.

Fact

Each agency controls access to its own data. Agencies may choose to share information through a broader network, but nothing is shared automatically. Participation is opt-in, governed by defined agreements, and limited to lawful investigative purposes.

Myth

Flock cameras use facial recognition.

Fact

Flock cameras do not use facial recognition technology. Searches are based on vehicle characteristics, and not identity. Flock cannot recognize, identify, or track individuals. It is purpose built for specific public safety investigations, not to monitor people.
