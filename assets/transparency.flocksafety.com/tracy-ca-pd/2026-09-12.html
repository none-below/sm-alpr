Overview

Tracy CA PD uses Flock Safety technology to capture objective evidence without compromising on individual privacy. Tracy CA PD utilizes retroactive search to solve crimes after they've occurred. Additionally, Tracy CA PD utilizes real time alerting of hotlist vehicles to capture wanted criminals. In an effort to ensure proper usage and guardrails are in place, they have made the below policies and usage statistics available to the public.

Featured Success Stories

Featured Success Story

On August 21, 2026, Tracy PD patrol officers responded to a Flock alert on a stolen vehicle out of Stockton, CA traveling in the area of MacArthur Dr and Grant Line Rd. The driver of the stolen vehicle tried to flee the area. Officers were able to safely apprehend the suspect after a short pursuit. The suspect was booked into County Jail on several felony charges. On August 20, 2026, Tracy PD patrol officers responded to a Flock alert on a felony vehicle involved in sideshow activity out of Oakland, CA. Officers were safely able to locate and impound the vehicle. This was a secondary vehicle involved in the prior Oakland incident. On August 19, 2026, Tracy PD patrol officers responded to a Flock alert on a felony vehicle involved in sideshow activity out of Oakland, CA. Officers located the vehicle unoccupied, and the vehicle was subsequently towed. On August 18, 2026, Tracy PD patrol officers received a Flock alert on a felony vehicle out of San Jose, CA. The vehicle had an active seizure warrant for illegal sideshow activity. The vehicle was located and impounded without incident. On August 17, 2026, Tracy PD patrol officers received a Flock alert of a stolen vehicle out of Stockton, CA traveling in the area of Schulte Rd and Macarthur Dr, within four minutes an officer had eyes on the vehicle. One suspect was subsequently taken into custody without incident and booked into County Jail. On August 9, 2026, Tracy PD patrol officers were alerted via Flock to a stolen vehicle out of Stockton, CA traveling in the area of Naglee Rd and Highway 205. Officers located the vehicle parked at a nearby hotel. Officers launched a UAS to get a visual on the vehicle, while watching the vehicle it began to go mobile. Officers were able to conduct a high risk stop on the vehicle and two individuals were arrested without incident. Both suspects were also found to be on active probation. On August 2, 2026, Tracy PD patrol officers responded to a Flock alert on a stolen vehicle out of Menlo Park, CA. Within five minutes officers located the vehicle, at which time the vehicle attempted to flee from officers and drove into a dead-end court. The suspects quickly realized this error and attempted to flee from the vehicle. The driver and two passengers were quickly apprehended. Two suspects were booked into County Jail and the third was booked in Juvenile Hall.

Policy & Trust

What's Detected

License Plates, Vehicles

What's Not Detected

Facial recognition, People, Gender, Race

Acceptable Use Policy

Data is used for law enforcement purposes only. Data is owned by Tracy CA PD and is never sold to 3rd parties.

Prohibited Uses

Immigration enforcement, traffic enforcement, harrassment or intimidation, usage based solely on a protected class (i.e. race, sex, religion), Personal use.

Access Policy

All system access requires a valid reason and is stored indefinitely.

Myth vs. Fact

Myth

Flock has been hacked.

Fact

Flock's cloud environment has never been breached. Flock invests in a strong security posture, including adhering to NDAA, SOC2 (Type II), ISO 27001, ISO 27018 FedRAMP 20x, and Higher Education Community Vendor Assessment Tool (HECVAT) compliance. Additional security precautions include Role-Based Access Controls, data encrypted at-rest and in transit, required Multi-Factor Authentication, and regular penetration testing.

Myth

Flock sells and/or shares customer data.

Fact

Your community owns 100% of your data and it will never be sold. Only your agency decides who to share your data with, not Flock. Flock Safety does not publicly release agency search data. Flock does not operate or support any public-facing searchable database of law enforcement lookups, and we do not provide license plate search histories to websites or third parties without the agency's knowledge and authorization. Other agencies who you share footage with have been responding to FOIA requests and sharing this search data.

Myth

My data is fed into a national database.

Fact

Each agency controls access to its own data. Agencies may choose to share information through a broader network, but nothing is shared automatically. Participation is opt-in, governed by defined agreements, and limited to lawful investigative purposes.

Myth

Flock cameras use facial recognition.

Fact

Flock cameras do not use facial recognition technology. Searches are based on vehicle characteristics, and not identity. Flock cannot recognize, identify, or track individuals. It is purpose built for specific public safety investigations, not to monitor people.
