Overview

The use of Flock Safety technology helps advance the Wilton, CT Police Department’s public safety mission by turning vehicle and license plate information into timely, objective leads that support crime prevention, investigation, and emergency response. By automatically scanning plates and securely checking them against lists of stolen vehicles, wanted suspects, and missing or endangered persons, the technology enables officers to identify threats more quickly and accurately while on patrol. The system also supports solving crimes after they occur by allowing investigators, under policy-driven controls, to review historical vehicle detections connected to specific cases. Clear policies, strict access controls, and regular oversight ensure the technology is used only for legitimate law enforcement purposes. A public transparency portal shows how the technology is deployed, reinforcing community trust and demonstrating that safety, accountability, and respect for privacy go hand in hand.



Community Impact
Featured Success Stories

Success Story 1

On March 29, 2025, the Wilton Police Department received a report of public indecency involving a male subject who had allegedly exposed himself to two juvenile victims. The victims provided a description of the suspect's vehicle, which was searched in Flock’s database, yielding a match. Shortly thereafter, a neighboring police department responded to a similar report involving the same suspect vehicle, in which the operator allegedly exposed himself to a juvenile within their jurisdiction. With the objective evidence obtained from Flock the offender was identified and subsequently charged with Risk of Injury to a Child, Breach of Peace in the Second Degree, and Public Indecency.


Success Story 2

On May 15, 2025, the Wilton Police Department received a report of an elderly female resident who had failed to return home. A search of the Flock database revealed that her vehicle had recently been observed in New York State. The individual had no known reason for traveling to that location, and her family determined that the trip likely occurred during an episode of mental confusion. She was formally reported as missing, and local New York law enforcement were advised of the missing individual but could not locate her. The vehicle's registration was added to a Flock hotlist. Several hours later, on May 16, 2025, at approximately 12:25 a.m., Flock notified officers that the vehicle was actively traveling within the Town of Wilton. Responding personnel located the vehicle and successfully reunited the individual with her family.


Success Story 3

Multiple area police departments were experiencing a crime pattern of organized retail theft. Through collaborative investigations, law enforcement identified a suspect vehicle and entered its registration into a Flock hotlist. On June 21, 2025, the Wilton Police Department received a Flock alert indicating that the suspect vehicle had entered the town. Officers initiated a search of local shopping centers and located the vehicle parked in the lot of a grocery store. While engaging the vehicle's operator, a second individual emerged from the store pushing a shopping cart with merchandise. Upon observing the officers, the second individual attempted to depart the scene. Further investigation revealed that the operator and the second individual were traveling together. The merchandise in the cart was confirmed to have been shoplifted from the store. Additionally, during the course of the investigation, fentanyl was discovered inside the vehicle. In summary, the Flock Safety alert enabled officers to locate the suspect vehicle, identify its occupants, and interrupt a larceny in progress.


Success Story 4

On July 16, 2025, Flock LPR cameras in the Town of Wilton alerted officers to a vehicle traveling through the area, whose registered owner was the subject of an active arrest warrant for domestic violence. Utilizing the vehicle details provided by Flock, officers located the vehicle and took the subject into custody without incident.

Policy & Trust

What's Detected

License Plates, Vehicles

What's Not Detected

Facial recognition, People, Gender, Race

Acceptable Use Policy

Data is used for law enforcement purposes only. Data is owned by Wilton CT PD and is never sold to 3rd parties.

Prohibited Uses

Immigration enforcement, Reproductive care, harassment or intimidation, usage based solely on a protected class (i.e. race, sex, religion), Personal use.

Access Policy

All system access requires a valid reason and is stored indefinitely.

Hotlist Policy

Hotlist hits are required to be human verified prior to action.
